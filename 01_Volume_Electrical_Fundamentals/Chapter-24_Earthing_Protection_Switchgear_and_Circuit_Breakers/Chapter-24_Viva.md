\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Protection?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Protection is the coordinated process of detecting abnormal electrical conditions and isolating the affected section within a safe time.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Detection and isolation
\- Uses protective zones
\- Limits damage and danger
\- Supports continuity

\*\*Remember\*\*

Protection senses, decides and interrupts through coordinated devices.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Electrical Protection important?

It protects people and equipment, limits fault Energy and fire risk, supports system stability and keeps healthy circuits energized where selectivity permits.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Equipment protection
\- System stability
\- Continuity of service

Protection reduces fault consequences but does not replace safe design.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Earthing?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Earthing is the intentional connection of selected system points and exposed conductive parts to Earth or an Earthing system for safety and functional performance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Provides fault path
\- Controls reference Voltage
\- Supports surge dissipation
\- Works with bonding

\*\*Remember\*\*

Earth has finite Resistance and Impedance.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the main objectives of Earthing?

\- Shock protection

\- Fault clearing
\- Surge control

Earthing, bonding and protection must work as one system.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Plate, Pipe, Rod and Strip Earthing.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Plate Earthing uses a buried metal plate; Pipe Earthing uses a vertical pipe; Rod Earthing uses a driven electrode; Strip Earthing uses a horizontal conductor, ring or grid. Selection depends on soil, area, corrosion, fault duty and local practice.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Plate has large area
\- Pipe can be economical
\- Rod is compact and extendable
\- Strip/grid covers large sites

\*\*Remember\*\*

No Earth-electrode type is universally best.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

Is there one universally acceptable Earth-Resistance value?

\*\*Answer\*\*

No. Acceptance depends on the Earthing arrangement, Touch and Step Voltage, fault Current, protective-device operating time, utility requirements and locally adopted code. Values such as 1 Ω or 5 Ω may be project targets, not universal safety limits.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- No universal limit
\- Protection operation matters
\- Touch/Step limits matter
\- Local code controls

\*\*Remember\*\*

An ohm target without system context is incomplete.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

How is Earth Resistance measured by the fall-of-potential method?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Earth Tester injects Current between the electrode under test and a remote Current probe, measures Voltage using a Potential probe and calculates R = V/I. Probe spacing and curve stability are essential.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses Current probe
\- Uses Potential probe
\- Measures V/I
\- Needs adequate spacing

\*\*Remember\*\*

A single convenient probe position may give a misleading result.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Equipment Earthing and System Earthing.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Equipment Earthing connects exposed conductive parts to the Protective system for touch safety and fault clearing. System Earthing connects a source or network point, often Neutral, to Earth to control system Voltage and Earth-Fault behavior.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Equipment frame vs source point
\- Shock safety vs system reference
\- Different normal functions
\- Both need coordination

\*\*Remember\*\*

Neutral and Protective Earth are not interchangeable.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Short Circuit, Open Circuit, Earth Fault and overload.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Short Circuit is an unintended low-impedance path; an Open Circuit is a broken intended path; an Earth Fault connects a live part to Earth or Earthed metal; an overload is excessive Current through the intended path.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Short gives high Current
\- Open stops intended Current
\- Earth Fault uses Earth/PE path
\- Overload is sustained excess

\*\*Remember\*\*

Different faults require different detection methods.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Fuse, MCB and MCCB.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Fuse melts and must be replaced. An MCB is resettable and commonly has fixed thermal-magnetic characteristics for final circuits. An MCCB handles larger Current and fault duty and often provides adjustable trip functions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Fuse is one-time
\- MCB suits final circuits
\- MCCB suits larger duty
\- Breaking capacity must match fault level

\*\*Remember\*\*

Device rating and curve matter more than the device name.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare RCCB, ELCB and RCBO.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An RCCB detects residual-current imbalance but normally lacks Overcurrent protection. ELCB may mean older voltage-operated technology or may be used loosely. An RCBO combines residual-current and Overcurrent protection.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- RCCB senses imbalance
\- RCCB needs Overcurrent coordination
\- ELCB term can be ambiguous
\- RCBO combines functions

\*\*Remember\*\*

Read the device standard and markings; do not rely on a casual name.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Protective Relay?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Protective Relay receives measured quantities, applies settings and logic, and issues a trip or alarm command when its operating criteria are met. A Circuit Breaker then interrupts Current.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- CT/VT inputs
\- Pickup and timing settings
\- Trip output
\- Breaker interruption

\*\*Remember\*\*

Relay decides; Breaker interrupts.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Circuit Breaker and how does it work?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Circuit Breaker makes, carries and interrupts normal and specified fault Current. When contacts separate an arc forms; the interrupting medium controls the arc and restores dielectric strength after Current interruption.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Mechanical switching device
\- Has making and breaking ratings
\- Controls an arc
\- Can be Relay-operated

\*\*Remember\*\*

A Breaker must be rated for both continuous and fault duty.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare ACB, VCB, OCB and SF₆ Circuit Breakers.

\*\*Answer\*\*

ACBs use air and are common on LV main boards. VCBs use vacuum and are widely used at MV. OCBs use oil and need fire and oil maintenance controls. SF₆ Breakers provide strong HV/EHV interruption but require leak control and responsible gas handling.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- ACB: LV
\- VCB: MV
\- OCB: legacy oil systems
\- SF₆: HV/EHV

\*\*Remember\*\*

Arc medium and Voltage duty distinguish the major Breaker types.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Surge Protection Device?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An SPD limits transient overvoltage by becoming conductive during a surge, diverting surge Current and clamping Voltage to a coordinated protection level.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Protects against transients
\- Needs short connections
\- Types 1, 2 and 3 are coordinated
\- Needs Earthing and backup protection

\*\*Remember\*\*

An SPD does not correct sustained overvoltage or replace Overcurrent protection.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the basic parts of a Lightning Protection System?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An external LPS uses air terminations, Down Conductors and Earth termination. Internal protection uses Equipotential Bonding and coordinated SPDs to reduce dangerous sparking and surges.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Intercepts strike
\- Conducts lightning Current
\- Disperses Current
\- Controls internal surges

\*\*Remember\*\*

Lightning protection is a coordinated path, not only an air terminal.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is protection coordination?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Protection coordination selects ratings, settings and time–Current characteristics so the nearest suitable device clears a fault while upstream supply remains available where practical.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses fault study
\- Compares device curves
\- Checks breaking capacity
\- Balances selectivity and speed

\*\*Remember\*\*

Selectivity must be demonstrated over a defined Current range.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

What should be checked during protection-system maintenance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Inspect and test CT/VT circuits, Relay settings and logic, auxiliary DC supply, trip circuits, Breaker mechanisms, interlocks, contacts, Earthing and records. Functional tests should prove the complete trip chain.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Relay testing
\- Breaker timing and contact checks
\- Battery and trip circuit
\- Controlled records

\*\*Remember\*\*

A Relay test alone does not prove the Breaker will trip.

What safety and system checks are needed when adding a Generator or large load?

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- System data review
\- Fault and Earthing study
\- Protection coordination
\- Safe commissioning

\*\*Remember\*\*

A new source or load changes the complete protection system.
