\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Capacitor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Capacitor is a passive electrical component that stores separated electric charge and energy in an Electric Field. It normally consists of two Conductive Plates separated by an insulating Dielectric.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It stores energy in an Electric Field.
\- Its plates are separated by a Dielectric.
\- Its Voltage cannot change instantaneously in the ideal model.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacitor → Electric-Field Energy Storage

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Capacitance and state its unit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Capacitance is the ability of a Capacitor to store electric charge per unit Potential Difference. It is expressed as C = Q/V, and its SI unit is the Farad (F).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- C = Q/V.
\- Q = CV.
\- Common practical units are µF, nF and pF.

\*\*Remember\*\*

One Farad = One Coulomb per Volt

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What factors determine the Capacitance of an ideal parallel-plate Capacitor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For an ideal parallel-plate Capacitor, C = εA/d. Capacitance increases with Dielectric Permittivity ε and overlapping plate area A, but decreases when plate separation d increases.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- C is proportional to ε.
\- C is proportional to A.
\- C is inversely proportional to d.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Larger Area and Better Dielectric Increase Capacitance

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Name common types of Capacitors and state one selection rule.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Common types include Ceramic, Electrolytic, Tantalum, Film, Mica, Supercapacitor and Variable Capacitor. Selection must consider Capacitance, rated Voltage, polarity, tolerance, ESR, ripple Current, temperature and application frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ceramic Capacitors are common for high-frequency bypassing.
\- Film Capacitors provide stable, low-loss performance.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Select by Value + Voltage + Polarity + Loss + Temperature

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Capacitors combined in Series and Parallel?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Parallel Capacitances add directly: Ceq = C₁ + C₂ + .... Series Capacitances combine by reciprocal sum: 1/Ceq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + .... For two Series Capacitors, Ceq = C₁C₂/(C₁ + C₂).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Parallel Capacitors have the same Voltage.
\- Ideal Series Capacitors carry equal charge magnitude.
\- Series equivalent Capacitance is smaller than the smallest value.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacitors: Parallel Adds; Series Uses Reciprocal Sum

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Inductor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Inductor is a passive electrical component that stores energy in a Magnetic Field when Current flows through it. It usually consists of a coil of insulated conductor, with or without a magnetic core.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It stores energy in a Magnetic Field.
\- It opposes a change in Current.
\- Its Current cannot change instantaneously in the ideal model.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Inductor → Magnetic-Field Energy Storage

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Inductance and state its unit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Inductance is the property by which a change in Current produces an opposing induced EMF through changing magnetic Flux Linkage. Its SI unit is the Henry (H).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- vL = L(di/dt) under the passive reference convention.
\- For a linear magnetic system, L = NΦ/I.
\- One Henry produces 1 V for a Current-change rate of 1 A/s.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Inductance L → Henry (H)

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is magnetic saturation in an Inductor core?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetic saturation is the condition in which a large increase in magnetizing Current produces only a small additional increase in magnetic Flux. Effective Inductance may fall, causing Current ripple, loss and heating to increase.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Saturation is a nonlinear core condition.
\- Inductance may decrease during saturation.
\- Saturation Current is an important practical rating.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Core Saturation → Lower Effective L + Rapid Current Increase

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Name common types of Inductors and their uses.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Air-Core Inductors are useful at high frequency without magnetic saturation. Iron-Core Inductors provide high low-frequency Inductance. Ferrite-Core Inductors are common in switching converters, Powdered-Iron cores support energy storage with DC bias, and Toroidal Inductors reduce external magnetic leakage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Core material affects frequency, loss and saturation.
\- Ferrite has high electrical Resistivity.
\- A Choke opposes unwanted changing Current.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Choose the Core for Frequency, Energy and Current

Uncoupled Inductors: Series Adds; Parallel Uses Reciprocal Sum

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

State the energy formulas for a Capacitor and an Inductor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The energy stored in a Capacitor is WC = ½CV². The energy stored in an Inductor is WL = ½LI². Both energies are measured in Joules.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Capacitor energy depends on Voltage squared.
\- Inductor energy depends on Current squared.
\- Stored energy may remain after switching.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacitor: ½CV²; Inductor: ½LI²

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operation of a simple Series RC charging Circuit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Immediately after connection, an initially uncharged Capacitor has zero Voltage and charging Current is limited by the resistor. As charge accumulates, Capacitor Voltage rises, resistor Voltage falls and Current decreases. After a long time, Capacitor Voltage approaches the source Voltage and Current approaches zero.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Initial Current is V/R for the simple uncharged Circuit.
\- vC rises exponentially.
\- In final DC steady state, the ideal Capacitor is Open.

\*\*Remember\*\*

RC Charging: Voltage Rises, Current Falls

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operation of a simple Series RL energizing Circuit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Immediately after connection, an initially unenergized Inductor Current is zero and the Inductor supports the source Voltage. Current then rises exponentially while Inductor Voltage falls. After a long time, Current approaches V/R and the ideal Inductor behaves as a Short Circuit.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Initial Inductor Current is zero only if previously unenergized.
\- iL rises exponentially.
\- Final ideal Inductor Voltage is zero.

\*\*Remember\*\*

RL Energizing: Current Rises, Inductor Voltage Falls

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

How does a Capacitor discharge through a resistor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A charged Capacitor provides Current through the discharge resistor and its Voltage decreases exponentially. For initial Voltage V₀, vC(t) = V₀e⁻ᵗ/ᴿᶜ. After each Time Constant, 36.8% of the previous difference remains.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Stored Electric-Field energy supplies the discharge Current.
\- Voltage polarity does not reverse in a simple passive discharge.
\- The Current direction is opposite to the original charging direction under the same reference.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Discharge Remaining Value = Initial Value × e⁻ᵗ/τ

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a DC Transient Response?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A DC Transient Response is the time-dependent change in Circuit Voltage or Current immediately after a switching action or a change in a DC source, load or connection. It continues until the Circuit approaches its new steady state.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A transient follows a change in Circuit condition.
\- Stored energy determines important initial conditions.
\- A first-order transient decays exponentially.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Switching Change → Transient → New Steady State

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

State the continuity conditions of ideal Capacitors and Inductors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Ideal Capacitor Voltage is continuous, so vC(0⁺) = vC(0⁻). Ideal Inductor Current is continuous, so iL(0⁺) = iL(0⁻). Their values at switching depend on stored energy before switching and are not automatically zero.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Capacitor Voltage cannot jump.
\- Inductor Current cannot jump.
\- Initial conditions must be calculated from the pre-switch Circuit.

\*\*Remember\*\*

C Holds Voltage Continuity; L Holds Current Continuity

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the general form of a first-order DC Transient Response?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The general form is x(t) = x(∞) + [x(0⁺) − x(∞)]e⁻ᵗ/τ. The response starts at x(0⁺), moves exponentially and approaches x(∞) as the transient term decays.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- x(0⁺) is the initial value.
\- x(∞) is the final value.
\- τ determines the response speed.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Response = Final + Initial Error × Decaying Exponential

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Time Constant for RC and RL Circuits.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Time Constant measures the speed of a first-order response. For an RC Circuit, τ = RthC. For an RL Circuit, τ = L/Rth. Rth is the Resistance seen by the storage element in the post-switch Circuit.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- RC: τ = RthC.
\- RL: τ = L/Rth.
\- Time Constant is measured in seconds.

\*\*Remember\*\*

RC Multiplies R and C; RL Divides L by R

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

How do you analyze a first-order switching Circuit step by step?

\*\*Answer\*\*

First analyze the pre-switch Circuit to find vC(0⁻) or iL(0⁻). Apply continuity at 0⁺. Then find the post-switch final value, determine the Resistance seen by the storage element, calculate the Time Constant and use the general exponential response. Finally, check initial value, final value, polarity and units.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Find the initial stored-energy condition.
\- Find the new final DC condition.
\- Calculate τ from the post-switch Circuit.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Initial → Continuity → Final → Rth → τ → Response → Verify

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What practical checks are required when selecting and testing Capacitors and Inductors?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For Capacitors, check Capacitance, rated Voltage, polarity, ESR, ripple Current, temperature and lifetime. For Inductors, check Inductance, RMS Current, saturation Current, DC Resistance, core loss and frequency. Before testing, isolate supplies, control stored energy, discharge Capacitors safely and provide a safe path for Inductor Current.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electrical, thermal and environmental ratings matter.
\- Supply isolation does not automatically remove stored energy.
\- Use approved procedures and correctly rated instruments.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Correct Rating + Stored-Energy Control + Safe Measurement
