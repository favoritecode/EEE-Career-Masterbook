\# Reference Notes

\- An ideal Capacitor stores energy in an Electric Field, and an ideal Inductor stores energy in a Magnetic Field.

\- Capacitor Voltage cannot change instantaneously, while Inductor Current cannot change instantaneously.

\- Under constant DC steady-state conditions, an ideal Capacitor behaves as an Open Circuit and an ideal Inductor behaves as a Short Circuit.

\- The simple Series and Parallel Inductor formulas assume negligible Mutual Inductance.

\- Capacitor energy is WC = ½CV², while Inductor energy is WL = ½LI².

\- The RC Time Constant is τ = RthC, and the RL Time Constant is τ = L/Rth.

\- Determine Rth from the storage-element terminals in the post-switch Circuit with independent sources deactivated and dependent sources active.

\- After one Time Constant, 63.2% of the total change is complete; after five Time Constants, the response is treated as practically complete.

\- Practical Capacitor selection must consider rated Voltage, polarity, ESR, ripple Current, tolerance, temperature and lifetime.

\- Practical Inductor selection must consider rated Current, saturation Current, DC Resistance, core loss, frequency and temperature rise.

\- A disconnected Capacitor may retain hazardous charge, and an interrupted Inductor Current may produce a high switching Voltage.

\- Actual testing must follow approved isolation, discharge, instrument-rating and stored-energy-control procedures.
