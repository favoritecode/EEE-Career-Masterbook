\# Chapter 08

\# Glossary

\## Capacitor

A passive electrical component that stores separated electric charge and energy in an Electric Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: C for Capacitance; Capacitor Circuit Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F) for Capacitance

\## Conductive Plate

One of the two conducting surfaces of a Capacitor on which electric charge is stored.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Dielectric

An insulating material placed between Capacitor plates to separate charge and support an Electric Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Capacitance

The ability of a Capacitor to store electric charge per unit Potential Difference.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: C = Q/V

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\## Farad

The SI unit of Capacitance; one Farad equals one Coulomb of charge stored per Volt.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: F

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\## Microfarad

A unit of Capacitance equal to 10⁻⁶ Farad.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: µF

\*\*Unit\*\*: Microfarad (µF)

\## Nanofarad

A unit of Capacitance equal to 10⁻⁹ Farad.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: nF

\*\*Unit\*\*: Nanofarad (nF)

\## Picofarad

A unit of Capacitance equal to 10⁻¹² Farad.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: pF

\*\*Unit\*\*: Picofarad (pF)

\## Permittivity

A property of a material that describes its response to an Electric Field and affects Capacitance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ε

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad per Metre (F/m)

\## Relative Permittivity

The ratio of a material's Permittivity to the Permittivity of free space.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: εr

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\## Dielectric Constant

Another common name for Relative Permittivity.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: εr

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\## Dielectric Strength

The maximum Electric Field an insulating material can withstand without breakdown under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ebd

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt per Metre (V/m)

\## Dielectric Breakdown

Failure of a Dielectric when excessive Electric Field causes significant conduction through it.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Capacitor Voltage

The Potential Difference between the two terminals or plates of a Capacitor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: vC or VC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Capacitor Current

The Current associated with a change in Capacitor charge and Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: iC = C(dvC/dt)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Leakage Current

A small unwanted Current that flows through or around the Dielectric of a practical Capacitor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ileak

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Equivalent Series Resistance

The effective Series Resistance representing important internal losses of a practical Capacitor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ESR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Ripple Current

The alternating component of Current that a Capacitor or Inductor carries in power-electronic and filter applications.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Iripple

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Polarized Capacitor

A Capacitor designed to operate with a specified terminal polarity and normally not intended for reverse Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Polarized Capacitor Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Ceramic Capacitor

A Capacitor using ceramic Dielectric, commonly used for compact high-frequency bypassing and filtering.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Electrolytic Capacitor

A usually polarized Capacitor that uses an electrolyte and provides relatively high Capacitance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Polarized Capacitor Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Film Capacitor

A normally non-polarized Capacitor using plastic film Dielectric and offering stable, low-loss performance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Supercapacitor

A very-high-Capacitance component used for short-term energy storage and backup at comparatively low cell Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: C

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\## Equivalent Capacitance

A single Capacitance that produces the same terminal charge–Voltage relationship as a Capacitor network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ceq

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\## Inductor

A passive electrical component that stores energy in a Magnetic Field and opposes changes in Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L for Inductance; Coil Circuit Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H) for Inductance

\## Inductance

The property by which a change in Current produces an opposing induced EMF through changing magnetic Flux Linkage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Henry

The SI unit of Inductance; one Henry produces one Volt when Current changes at one Ampere per second.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: H

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Millihenry

A unit of Inductance equal to 10⁻³ Henry.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: mH

\*\*Unit\*\*: Millihenry (mH)

\## Microhenry

A unit of Inductance equal to 10⁻⁶ Henry.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: µH

\*\*Unit\*\*: Microhenry (µH)

\## Inductor Voltage

The Voltage produced across an Inductor by a change in its Current under the selected reference convention.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: vL = L(diL/dt)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Inductor Current

The Current flowing through an Inductor and producing its Magnetic Field and Flux Linkage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: iL or IL

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Self-Inductance

The property by which changing Current in a coil induces an EMF in the same coil.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Flux Linkage

The product of the number of coil turns and the magnetic Flux linking each turn under the assumed model.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: λ = NΦ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Weber-Turn (Wb-turn)

\## Mutual Inductance

The property by which changing Current in one coil induces a Voltage in another magnetically coupled coil.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: M

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Magnetic Core

A material path used to guide magnetic Flux and change the Inductance and magnetic behavior of a coil.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Air-Core Inductor

An Inductor without a ferromagnetic core, useful where magnetic saturation must be avoided and high-frequency operation is required.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Ferrite-Core Inductor

An Inductor using a high-Resistivity ferrite magnetic core, commonly used at high switching frequencies.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Toroidal Inductor

An Inductor wound around a ring-shaped core that provides a closed magnetic path and generally low external Flux leakage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Choke

An Inductor designed to oppose an unwanted changing Current component while allowing a required DC or lower-frequency component.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Winding Resistance

The electrical Resistance of the conductor used to form an Inductor winding.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RDC or Rw

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Core Loss

Energy loss in a magnetic core, mainly associated with Hysteresis and Eddy Current under changing magnetic conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Pcore

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\## Magnetic Saturation

A nonlinear core condition in which a large increase in magnetizing Current produces only a small additional increase in magnetic Flux.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Saturation Current

The specified Inductor Current at which Inductance falls by a defined amount because the magnetic core approaches saturation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Isat

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Equivalent Inductance

A single Inductance that produces the same terminal Voltage–Current relationship as an Inductor network under specified coupling conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Leq

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Stored Energy

Energy held temporarily in the Electric Field of a Capacitor or Magnetic Field of an Inductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: W

\*\*Unit\*\*: Joule (J)

\## Capacitor Energy

The energy stored in the Electric Field of a Capacitor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: WC = ½CV²

\*\*Unit\*\*: Joule (J)

\## Inductor Energy

The energy stored in the Magnetic Field of an Inductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: WL = ½LI²

\*\*Unit\*\*: Joule (J)

\## RC Circuit

A Circuit containing Resistance and Capacitance that produces a time-dependent Capacitor Voltage after a change in Circuit condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## RL Circuit

A Circuit containing Resistance and Inductance that produces a time-dependent Inductor Current after a change in Circuit condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RL

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Capacitor Charging

The process by which Capacitor charge, Voltage and stored energy move toward values established by a source.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Capacitor Discharging

The process by which a Capacitor releases stored charge and energy through an external path.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Transient

The temporary time-dependent part of a Circuit response after switching or another change in Circuit condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## DC Transient Response

The time-dependent behavior of Circuit Voltage and Current following a change in a DC source, load, switch or connection.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: x(t)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Response Quantity

\## Initial Condition

The Capacitor Voltage or Inductor Current that exists at the switching instant because of the pre-switch Circuit and stored energy.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: vC(0⁻), vC(0⁺), iL(0⁻) or iL(0⁺)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) or Ampere (A)

\## Final Value

The value approached by a Circuit quantity after the transient has decayed under the new constant DC condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: x(∞)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Quantity

\## Steady State

The condition in which Circuit quantities no longer change with time under a constant excitation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: t → ∞

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Natural Response

The Circuit response produced by energy initially stored in Capacitors or Inductors when considered without continuing independent excitation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: xn(t)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Response Quantity

\## Forced Response

The part of a Circuit response produced by an applied independent source.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: xf(t)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Response Quantity

\## Complete Response

The total Circuit response containing the natural and forced contributions, or equivalently the final value plus a decaying transient term.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: x(t)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Response Quantity

\## First-Order Circuit

A Circuit whose dynamic behavior can be described by one independent energy-storage state and one Time Constant.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Time Constant

A measure of how quickly a first-order Circuit response moves from its initial value toward its final value.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: τ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s)

\## RC Time Constant

The Time Constant of a first-order RC Circuit, equal to the Resistance seen by the Capacitor multiplied by Capacitance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: τRC = RthC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s)

\## RL Time Constant

The Time Constant of a first-order RL Circuit, equal to Inductance divided by the Resistance seen by the Inductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: τRL = L/Rth

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s)

\## Exponential Response

A response whose difference from its final value changes according to an exponential function of time.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: e⁻ᵗ/τ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless Factor

\## Five-Time-Constant Rule

The engineering approximation that a first-order response is practically complete after five Time Constants, when about 0.7% of the original difference remains.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: t ≈ 5τ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s)

\## Voltage Continuity

The ideal Capacitor condition stating that its Voltage immediately after switching equals its Voltage immediately before switching.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: vC(0⁺) = vC(0⁻)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Current Continuity

The ideal Inductor condition stating that its Current immediately after switching equals its Current immediately before switching.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: iL(0⁺) = iL(0⁻)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Flyback Diode

A diode connected across a DC inductive load to provide a path for Current and limit switch-off Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Diode Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Snubber Circuit

A protective network used to control switching Voltage, Current rate, oscillation or energy dissipation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Commonly RC or RCD

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Decoupling Capacitor

A Capacitor placed near a Circuit or device to supply local transient Current and reduce unwanted supply noise.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Cdec

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\## Bypass Capacitor

A Capacitor that provides a low-impedance path for an unwanted changing signal component around a Circuit section.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Cbypass

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)
