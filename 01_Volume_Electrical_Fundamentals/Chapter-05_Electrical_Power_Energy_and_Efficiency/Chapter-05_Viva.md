\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Power?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, converted or consumed. It is measured in Watt (W) and can be expressed as P = E/t.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power describes how quickly Energy is transferred.
\- Its symbol is P.
\- Its SI unit is Watt (W).

\*\*Remember\*\*

Power = Energy / Time

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is one Watt?

\*\*Answer\*\*

One Watt is the Power when one Joule of Energy is transferred or converted in one second. Therefore, 1 W = 1 J/s.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Watt is the SI unit of Power.
\- It relates Energy and Time.
\- The unit symbol is W.

\*\*Remember\*\*

1 W = 1 J/s

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

How is DC Electrical Power calculated?

\*\*Answer\*\*

DC Electrical Power is calculated by multiplying the Voltage across a load by the Current through it. The basic formula is P = VI.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P represents Power in Watt.
\- V represents Voltage in Volt.
\- I represents Current in Ampere.

\*\*Remember\*\*

P = V × I

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Energy?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Energy is the total electrical work done or energy transferred, converted or consumed during a period. It is calculated using E = Pt.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Energy accumulates over operating time.
\- Its SI unit is Joule (J).
\- Kilowatt-hour is used for practical Energy measurement.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Energy = Power × Time

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a kilowatt-hour?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the Energy consumed when a Power of one kilowatt operates for one hour. It is a practical unit of Energy, not Power.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- 1 kWh = 1000 Wh.
\- 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
\- One kWh is commonly called one unit of electricity.

\*\*Remember\*\*

1 Unit of Electricity = 1 kWh

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the relationship between Power and Energy?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Power is the rate of Energy transfer, while Energy is the total amount transferred over time. Their relationship is E = Pt and P = E/t.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power describes the rate.
\- Energy describes the total quantity.
\- Operating time connects Power and Energy.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Power Is a Rate; Energy Is a Total Amount

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Efficiency?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Efficiency is the ratio of useful output Power or Energy to total input Power or Energy. It is normally expressed as a percentage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Efficiency indicates conversion performance.
\- Its symbol is commonly η.
\- Efficiency = Useful Output / Total Input × 100%.

\*\*Remember\*\*

η = Output / Input × 100%

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is the Efficiency of a practical electrical device less than 100%?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Efficiency of a practical device is less than 100% because some input Energy is converted into unwanted forms such as heat, sound, friction and magnetic losses.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Every practical conversion process has losses.
\- Useful output is lower than total input.
\- Reducing losses improves Efficiency.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Input = Useful Output + Losses

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

How is domestic electrical Energy consumption measured?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Domestic electrical Energy consumption is measured by an energy meter in kilowatt-hours. Consumption during a billing period is the final meter reading minus the initial meter reading.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The meter records cumulative Energy.
\- The billing unit is normally kWh.
\- Consumption = Final reading − Initial reading.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Meter Reading Difference = Energy Consumed

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

How is the basic Energy charge on an electricity bill calculated?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The basic Energy charge is calculated by multiplying the consumed Energy in kWh by the applicable tariff rate per kWh. Other fixed, demand, tax or service charges may also apply.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Energy charge = kWh consumed × Rate per kWh.
\- Tariff rates may vary by consumer category or usage block.
\- The final bill may include additional charges.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Energy Cost = Consumption × Tariff Rate

\# Advanced Viva

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Derive the Power expressions P = I²R and P = V²/R for an ohmic resistor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Starting from P = VI, use Ohm's Law V = IR to obtain P = (IR)I = I²R. Alternatively, use I = V/R to obtain P = V(V/R) = V²/R.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The basic Power formula is P = VI.
\- Ohm's Law provides V = IR and I = V/R.
\- The derived expressions apply to ohmic resistive loads.

\*\*Remember\*\*

P = VI = I²R = V²/R

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does doubling Voltage across a fixed resistor increase Power four times?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For a fixed Resistance, P = V²/R. If Voltage becomes 2V, the new Power is (2V)²/R = 4V²/R. Therefore, Power becomes four times the original value.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power is proportional to the square of Voltage at constant Resistance.
\- Current also doubles according to I = V/R.
\- Excess Voltage can cause overheating and damage.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Constant R: V × 2 → P × 4

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Can a low-Power appliance consume more Energy than a high-Power appliance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Yes. Energy depends on both Power and operating time. A low-Power appliance operating for a long period can consume more Energy than a high-Power appliance operating briefly.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- E = Pt.
\- Power alone does not determine total Energy.
\- Operating habits strongly affect electricity bills.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Compare kWh, Not Watt Alone

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

How do you calculate the Energy consumed by several appliances?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Calculate the Energy of each appliance separately using E = Pt, keeping units consistent. Then add all individual Energy values to obtain the total consumption.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Convert Power to kW when calculating kWh.
\- Convert operating time to hours.
\- Sum the Energy values, not only the Power ratings.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Total Energy = E₁ + E₂ + E₃ + ...

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Power rating and Energy capacity used in backup system design?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Power rating determines whether an inverter, UPS or generator can supply the connected load at a given moment. Energy capacity determines how long the system can support that load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power capacity is measured in W, kW or appropriate VA ratings.
\- Stored Energy may be expressed in Wh or kWh.
\- Conversion losses reduce actual backup time.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Power → Load Capacity; Energy → Operating Duration

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

How does improving Efficiency reduce Energy consumption?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For the same useful output, a more efficient device requires less input Power. If operating time remains the same, the lower input Power produces lower Energy consumption and lower losses.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Higher Efficiency reduces required input.
\- Lower losses usually reduce heating.
\- Energy cost decreases for the same useful service.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Same Output + Higher Efficiency = Lower Input Energy

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Energy loss and Power loss?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Power loss is the rate at which Energy is wasted at a particular operating condition, usually measured in Watt. Energy loss is the total wasted Energy accumulated over time, measured in Joule, Wh or kWh.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power loss is a rate.
\- Energy loss depends on loss Power and time.
\- Energy loss = Power loss × Time.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Loss Energy = Loss Power × Time

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

How can an electricity bill be estimated before receiving the meter bill?

\*\*Answer\*\*

List each appliance's Power rating and expected operating time, calculate its Energy in kWh, and add all values. Multiply the estimated total kWh by the applicable tariff, then include known fixed or additional charges.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Prepare an appliance load schedule.
\- Calculate daily and monthly kWh.
\- Use the current applicable tariff structure.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Estimated Bill = Estimated kWh Charge + Applicable Additional Charges

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is load diversity important in practical Power planning?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Load diversity recognizes that all connected appliances may not operate at maximum Power simultaneously. It helps engineers estimate realistic demand while maintaining adequate safety and design margins.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Connected load is the sum of equipment ratings.
\- Maximum demand may be lower than connected load.
\- Diversity supports economical system sizing.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Connected Load ≠ Always Simultaneous Demand

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What practical steps can reduce electrical Energy consumption without reducing useful service?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Energy use can be reduced by selecting efficient equipment, switching off unnecessary loads, eliminating standby consumption, maintaining equipment, correcting poor operating practices and monitoring Energy use.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Use efficient lamps, motors and appliances.
\- Reduce unnecessary operating time.
\- Measure and review consumption regularly.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Reduce Waste, Not Useful Output
