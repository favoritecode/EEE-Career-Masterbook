\# Chapter 07

\# Glossary

\## Electrical Network

An interconnection of electrical elements and sources arranged to perform a function or provide paths for electric Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Network Theorem

A systematic principle used to simplify or analyze an electrical network while preserving the required electrical behavior.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Linear Circuit

A circuit in which the response is proportional to the excitation and the principles of proportionality and additivity are satisfied.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Bilateral Element

An element whose Voltage–Current relationship is the same in either direction under the specified operating conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Two-Terminal Network

A network viewed from two accessible terminals through which it interacts with an external load or another circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Port

A pair of terminals through which electrical energy or a signal enters or leaves a network, with equal and opposite terminal Currents.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Load

The circuit element or external network connected to an output port to receive electrical Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RL for a Resistive Load

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω) when Represented as Resistance

\## Superposition Theorem

The theorem stating that the Voltage or Current response in a linear circuit with multiple independent sources equals the algebraic sum of the responses produced by each independent source acting alone.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Individual Response

The Voltage or Current produced in an element when one independent source acts alone and all other independent sources are deactivated.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V₁, V₂, I₁, I₂, ...

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) or Ampere (A)

\## Algebraic Sum

A sum in which the sign of every quantity is included according to its selected polarity or direction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Σ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the Quantity

\## Source Deactivation

The process of setting an independent source to zero while retaining its internal Resistance and keeping dependent sources active.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Independent Voltage Source

A source that maintains a specified Voltage independently of other circuit variables within its ideal operating model.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vs

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Independent Current Source

A source that maintains a specified Current independently of other circuit variables within its ideal operating model.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Is

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Dependent Source

A Voltage or Current source whose value is controlled by another Voltage or Current elsewhere in the circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Controlled-Source Diamond Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) or Ampere (A)

\## Open Circuit

A path having ideally infinite Resistance and zero Current, although a Voltage may exist across its terminals.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Open-Switch Symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Short Circuit

A path having ideally zero Resistance and zero Voltage drop, while its Current is determined by the surrounding circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Conducting-Wire Connection

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Thevenin's Theorem

The theorem stating that a linear two-terminal network can be replaced by an ideal Voltage source in Series with an equivalent Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Thevenin Voltage

The open-circuit Voltage measured or calculated across the selected output terminals of a network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vth or Voc

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Thevenin Resistance

The equivalent Resistance seen from the selected load terminals after independent sources are deactivated, or found using an appropriate test-source or terminal-ratio method.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Rth

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Thevenin Equivalent Circuit

A simplified two-terminal circuit consisting of Vth in Series with Rth.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vth–Rth Model

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Open-Circuit Voltage

The terminal Voltage when no external Current flows between the selected terminals.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Voc

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Norton's Theorem

The theorem stating that a linear two-terminal network can be replaced by an ideal Current source in Parallel with an equivalent Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Norton Current

The short-circuit Current flowing between the selected output terminals of a network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IN or Isc

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Norton Resistance

The equivalent Resistance seen from the selected load terminals of the Norton network; it is equal to the Thevenin Resistance of the same network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RN

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Norton Equivalent Circuit

A simplified two-terminal circuit consisting of IN in Parallel with RN.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IN–RN Model

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Short-Circuit Current

The Current flowing between two terminals when they are connected by an ideal zero-Resistance path.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Isc

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Equivalent Resistance

A single Resistance that produces the same terminal Voltage–Current relationship as a resistor network under the specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Req

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Test Source

A known Voltage or Current source temporarily connected at a network port to determine the Resistance seen from that port.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vtest or Itest

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) or Ampere (A)

\## Terminal Equivalence

The condition in which two networks have the same Voltage–Current relationship at their corresponding external terminals.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Maximum Power Transfer Theorem

The theorem stating that a resistive load receives maximum Power from a linear DC network when its Resistance equals the Thevenin Resistance seen from the load terminals.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RL = Rth

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω) for the Matching Condition

\## Maximum Load Power

The greatest Power that a source network can deliver to a variable resistive load under the maximum Power transfer condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Pmax = Vth²/(4Rth)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\## Resistance Matching

The condition in a resistive DC circuit in which load Resistance equals source or Thevenin Resistance for maximum load Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: RL = Rth

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Efficiency

The ratio of useful output Power to input Power, commonly expressed as a percentage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: η

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless or Percent (%)

\## Source Transformation

The conversion between a practical Voltage source in Series with a Resistance and an equivalent practical Current source in Parallel with the same Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vs = IsR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Practical Voltage Source

An ideal Voltage source modeled in Series with an internal Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vs in Series with Rs

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) and Ohm (Ω)

\## Practical Current Source

An ideal Current source modeled in Parallel with an internal Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Is in Parallel with Rs

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A) and Ohm (Ω)

\## Delta Network

A three-terminal network of three elements connected end-to-end in a closed triangular form.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Δ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Star Network

A three-terminal network in which one end of each of three elements is connected to a common central point.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Y

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Delta-to-Star Transformation

The conversion of a three-terminal Delta network into an electrically equivalent Star network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Δ → Y

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Star-to-Delta Transformation

The conversion of a three-terminal Star network into an electrically equivalent Delta network.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Y → Δ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Balanced Delta Network

A Delta network in which all three branch Resistances have equal values.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Rab = Rbc = Rca = RΔ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Balanced Star Network

A Star network in which all three arm Resistances have equal values.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ra = Rb = Rc = RY

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Circuit Simplification

The process of replacing parts of a circuit with equivalent forms to reduce the number or complexity of the required calculations.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Load-Line Behavior

The relationship between terminal Voltage and load Current imposed by a source network or equivalent circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V = Vth − IRth

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) and Ampere (A)

\## Internal Resistance

The Resistance included in a practical source model to represent its internal Voltage drop and Power loss.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Rs or Ri

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Model Limitation

A condition or assumption that restricts where an equivalent circuit, theorem or simplified model provides an accurate result.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable
