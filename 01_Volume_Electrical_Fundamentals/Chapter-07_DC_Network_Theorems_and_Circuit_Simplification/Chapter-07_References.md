\# Reference Notes

\- Confirm that a circuit is linear before applying the Superposition, Thevenin or Norton Theorem in its standard form.

\- During Superposition, deactivate only independent sources; dependent sources must remain active.

\- Replace an ideal independent Voltage source with a short circuit and an ideal independent Current source with an open circuit when deactivating sources.

\- Superposition applies directly to Voltage and Current, but not directly to Power; calculate Power after finding the total response.

\- The Thevenin Voltage is the open-circuit terminal Voltage, while the Norton Current is the short-circuit terminal Current.

\- Thevenin and Norton Resistances are equal for equivalent representations of the same linear two-terminal network.

\- For a resistive DC circuit, maximum load Power occurs when RL = Rth; this condition gives 50% efficiency in the simple Thevenin model.

\- In Source Transformation, preserve the terminal polarity, source direction and associated Resistance value.

\- Label all three external terminals before applying Delta–Star or Star–Delta formulas.

\- Verify simplified and original circuits by comparing their terminal Voltage and Current under the same load condition.

\- Actual circuit testing must follow suitable isolation, instrument-rating and electrical-safety procedures.
