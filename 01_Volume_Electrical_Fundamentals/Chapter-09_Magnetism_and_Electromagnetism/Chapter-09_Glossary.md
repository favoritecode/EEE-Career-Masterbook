\# Chapter 09

\# Glossary

\## Magnetism

A physical phenomenon associated with moving electric charges, magnetic moments and Magnetic Fields that can produce attraction, repulsion and electromagnetic force.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnet

An object or material that produces a Magnetic Field and has identifiable North and South magnetic poles.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## North Pole

The pole of a freely suspended magnet that points approximately toward Earth's geographic north and from which external Magnetic Field Lines conventionally emerge.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## South Pole

The pole of a magnet into which external Magnetic Field Lines conventionally enter.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: S

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Permanent Magnet

A magnet made from a material that retains significant Magnetism after the external magnetizing influence is removed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PM

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Temporary Magnet

A magnetic object that is easily magnetized but loses most of its Magnetism after the external magnetizing influence is removed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnetic Material

A material whose atomic magnetic moments respond to an applied Magnetic Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Diamagnetic Material

A material that develops a weak magnetic response opposite to an applied Magnetic Field and is weakly repelled by it.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Paramagnetic Material

A material that is weakly attracted by an applied Magnetic Field and normally does not retain strong Magnetism after the field is removed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Ferromagnetic Material

A material that can be strongly magnetized through the alignment of Magnetic Domains, such as Iron, Cobalt or Nickel.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Ferrimagnetic Material

A material containing opposing magnetic moments of unequal magnitude, resulting in a net magnetic response; ferrites are common examples.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnetic Domain

A microscopic region in a magnetic material where many atomic magnetic moments have a common alignment.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Soft Magnetic Material

A material that is easy to magnetize and demagnetize and normally has high Permeability, low Coercivity and a narrow Hysteresis Loop.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Hard Magnetic Material

A magnetic material with high resistance to demagnetization, normally used for Permanent Magnets.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Curie Temperature

The temperature above which a Ferromagnetic Material loses its ferromagnetic ordering and becomes Paramagnetic.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: T c

\*\*Unit\*\*: Kelvin (K) or Degree Celsius (°C)

\## Magnetic Field

A region in which magnetic force can act on a magnet, a suitable Magnetic Material, a moving charge or a Current-carrying conductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnetic Field Line

An imaginary continuous line used to represent Magnetic Field direction and relative strength in a field diagram.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnetic Flux

A measure of the total Magnetic Field passing through a selected surface.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Φ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Weber (Wb)

\## Magnetic Flux Density

A vector quantity describing the concentration and direction of Magnetic Flux in a region.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: B

\*\*Unit\*\*: Tesla (T) or Weber per Square Metre (Wb/m²)

\## Magnetic Field Strength

A quantity describing the magnetizing influence produced by Current and winding arrangement, independent of the material response included in B.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: H

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere per Metre (A/m)

\## Permeability

A material property describing how Magnetic Flux Density relates to Magnetic Field Strength in a specified operating condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: μ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry per Metre (H/m)

\## Relative Permeability

The ratio of a material's Permeability to the Permeability of free space.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: μ r

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\## Flux Linkage

The total Magnetic Flux linked with all turns of a coil; when the same Flux links every turn, it equals NΦ.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: λ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Weber-turn (Wb-turn)

\## Magnetomotive Force

The magnetic-circuit driving quantity produced by a coil and equal to the product of turns and Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: MMF or NI

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere-turn (At)

\## Reluctance

The opposition that a magnetic path presents to the establishment of Magnetic Flux.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ℜ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere-turn per Weber (At/Wb)

\## Leakage Flux

The portion of Magnetic Flux that does not follow the complete intended magnetic path or link all intended windings.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Φ leakage

\*\*Unit\*\*: Weber (Wb)

\## Fringing

The spreading of Magnetic Flux at an air gap or near the edge of a magnetic path.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Magnetic Saturation

The nonlinear operating region in which a large increase in Magnetic Field Strength produces only a relatively small further increase in Magnetic Flux Density.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Hysteresis

The dependence of a magnetic material's present magnetic state on its previous magnetization history.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Coercivity

The magnitude of reverse Magnetic Field Strength required to reduce the magnetic response to a specified zero condition after magnetization.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: H c

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere per Metre (A/m)

\## Retentivity

The ability of a magnetic material to retain residual Magnetism after the magnetizing field is removed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Commonly related to B r

\*\*Unit\*\*: Tesla (T) when expressed as Remanent Flux Density

\## Electromagnet

A device in which Electric Current through a coil produces a controllable Magnetic Field, often strengthened by a Soft Magnetic core.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Solenoid

A coil arranged to produce a directed Magnetic Field and often used to create linear mechanical motion through a movable plunger.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Electromagnetic Induction

The production of an induced EMF when the Magnetic Flux Linkage associated with a conductor or circuit changes.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Induced EMF

The Voltage produced by a change in Magnetic Flux Linkage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: e

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Faraday's Laws

The laws stating that changing Magnetic Flux Linkage induces EMF and that its magnitude equals the time rate of change of Flux Linkage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: e = −dλ/dt

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Lenz's Law

The law stating that induced EMF and Current have a direction that produces a Magnetic Field opposing the change in Flux that caused the induction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Represented by the negative sign in Faraday's Law

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Self-Induction

The production of induced EMF in a coil because a change in its own Current changes its own Flux Linkage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: e = −L(di/dt)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) for induced EMF

\## Mutual Induction

The production of induced EMF in one coil because changing Current in another magnetically coupled coil changes the linked Flux.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: e₂ = −M(di₁/dt)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) for induced EMF

\## Inductance

The circuit property that relates Flux Linkage to Current in a linear magnetic system and opposes changes in Current through induced EMF.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Mutual Inductance

The property describing how a change of Current in one coil induces EMF in another magnetically coupled coil.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: M

\*\*Unit\*\*: Henry (H)

\## Eddy Current

A circulating Current induced in a bulk conductor by changing Magnetic Flux.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: I e

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Hysteresis Loss

Energy dissipated as heat in a magnetic material because it is repeatedly magnetized through a Hysteresis Loop.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P h

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\## Eddy-Current Loss

Power dissipated as heat by Eddy Currents induced in a conductive magnetic core.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P e

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\## Counter EMF

An induced EMF whose polarity opposes the applied Voltage or the change in Current that produces it.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: E b or e back

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)
