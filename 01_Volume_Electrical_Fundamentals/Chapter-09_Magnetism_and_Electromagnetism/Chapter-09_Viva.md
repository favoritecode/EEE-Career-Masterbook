\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Magnetism?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetism is a physical phenomenon associated with moving electric charges, magnetic moments and Magnetic Fields. It can produce attraction or repulsion and can exert force on magnets, suitable Magnetic Materials and Current-carrying conductors.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Magnetism is associated with Magnetic Fields.
\- A magnet has North and South Poles.
\- Magnetic effects can produce force.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Magnetism → Magnetic Field and Magnetic Force

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Permanent and Temporary Magnets.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Permanent Magnet retains significant Magnetism after the magnetizing influence is removed. A Temporary Magnet is easily magnetized but loses most of its Magnetism when the external field is removed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Permanent Magnets have high resistance to demagnetization.
\- Temporary Magnets are easy to magnetize and demagnetize.
\- Soft Iron is commonly used for temporary electromagnetic action.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Permanent → Retains Magnetism; Temporary → Easily Loses Magnetism

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Ferromagnetic Materials?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Ferromagnetic Materials are materials that can be strongly magnetized because many of their atomic magnetic moments can align in Magnetic Domains. Iron, Cobalt and Nickel are common examples.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- They respond strongly to a Magnetic Field.
\- They contain Magnetic Domains.
\- Their Permeability can be much greater than that of free space.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Iron, Cobalt and Nickel → Ferromagnetic

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Soft and Hard Magnetic Materials.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Soft Magnetic Materials are easy to magnetize and demagnetize and normally have low Coercivity and a narrow Hysteresis Loop. Hard Magnetic Materials resist demagnetization and are suitable for Permanent Magnets.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Soft materials are used in changing Magnetic Fields.
\- Hard materials retain Magnetism.
\- Their Hysteresis and Coercivity differ.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Soft → Cores; Hard → Permanent Magnets

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

State the important properties of Magnetic Field Lines.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetic Field Lines form continuous closed loops, do not intersect and indicate the local field direction through their tangent. In a qualitative diagram, a greater line density indicates a stronger field.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Field Lines are continuous and closed.
\- They never intersect.
\- Outside a magnet, their conventional direction is North to South.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Closed, Continuous and Non-Intersecting

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Magnetic Flux and state its unit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetic Flux is a measure of the total Magnetic Field passing through a selected surface. It is represented by Φ and measured in Weber (Wb).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Symbol: Φ
\- SI unit: Weber
\- For a uniform field, Φ = BA cos θ.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Magnetic Flux: Φ, Weber

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electromagnetism?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electromagnetism describes the relationship between Electricity and Magnetism. In Electrical Engineering, a key principle is that moving electric charges or Electric Current produce a Magnetic Field.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electric Current produces a Magnetic Field.
\- A coil concentrates the magnetic effect.
\- Electromagnetic force can produce motion.

Electricity and Magnetism Are Connected

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What factors generally increase the strength of an Electromagnet?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electromagnet strength generally increases with greater coil Current, more turns, a suitable high-Permeability core, a well-designed magnetic path and a smaller air gap where mechanically appropriate.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- MMF equals NI.
\- Core material and air gap affect Flux.
\- Thermal and saturation limits must be respected.

\*\*Remember\*\*

More NI and Better Magnetic Path → Stronger Electromagnet

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electromagnetic Induction?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electromagnetic Induction is the production of an induced EMF in a conductor or circuit when its associated Magnetic Flux Linkage changes. A closed circuit may then carry an induced Current.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Changing Flux Linkage is required.
\- Induced EMF can exist in an open circuit.
\- Induced Current requires a closed path.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Changing Flux Linkage → Induced EMF

\# Advanced Viva

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

State Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Faraday's First Law states that changing the Magnetic Flux Linkage of a circuit induces an EMF. Faraday's Second Law states that the magnitude of induced EMF equals the time rate of change of Flux Linkage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- First Law identifies when induction occurs.
\- Second Law determines EMF magnitude.
\- For a coil, e = −d(NΦ)/dt.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Faraday: Change of Flux Linkage Produces EMF

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

State Lenz's Law.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Lenz's Law states that the direction of induced EMF and induced Current is such that the Magnetic Field they produce opposes the change in Magnetic Flux that caused the induction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It determines induced polarity or direction.
\- It opposes the change in Flux.
\- It is consistent with conservation of energy.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Induced Effect Opposes the Causing Change

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Self-Induction and Mutual Induction.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Self-Induction occurs when changing Current in a coil induces EMF in the same coil. Mutual Induction occurs when changing Current in one coil induces EMF in another magnetically coupled coil.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Self-Induction involves the same coil.
\- Mutual Induction involves coupled coils.
\- Both require changing Flux Linkage.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Self → Same Coil; Mutual → Another Coupled Coil

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Magnetic Flux and Magnetic Flux Density.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetic Flux Φ measures the total field passing through a selected surface and is measured in Weber. Magnetic Flux Density B describes Flux concentration and direction and is measured in Tesla or Weber per square metre.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Flux symbol: Φ; unit: Wb.
\- Flux Density symbol: B; unit: T.
\- For a normal uniform field, B = Φ/A.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Flux → Total; Flux Density → Flux per Unit Area

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does a transformer require changing Magnetic Flux?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A transformer depends on Mutual Induction. The primary Current must create changing core Flux so that the secondary Flux Linkage changes and an EMF is induced according to Faraday's Law.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Transformer action uses Mutual Induction.
\- Secondary EMF requires changing Flux Linkage.
\- Steady DC does not provide normal continuous transformer action.

\*\*Remember\*\*

No Changing Flux → No Continuous Secondary EMF

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Eddy Currents, and how can they be useful or harmful?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Eddy Currents are circulating Currents induced in bulk conductors by changing Magnetic Flux. They are useful in induction heating, braking and damping but cause unwanted heating and Power Loss in many magnetic cores.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- They require changing Flux.
\- They follow Lenz's Law.
\- Laminations and high-Resistivity core materials reduce unwanted Eddy Currents.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Eddy Currents → Useful Heating or Unwanted Core Loss

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Magnetic Saturation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Magnetic Saturation is the region in which a Ferromagnetic Material is already strongly magnetized, so a large further increase in Magnetizing Force produces only a relatively small increase in Magnetic Flux Density.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- B-H behavior becomes strongly nonlinear.
\- Effective Permeability decreases near saturation.
\- Saturation can increase Magnetizing Current and distortion.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Near Saturation: Much More H, Little Additional B

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Why can a coil produce a high Voltage when its Current is interrupted suddenly?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Sudden Current interruption causes a rapid change in the coil's Flux Linkage. By Self-Induction, the coil produces an EMF that opposes the Current change, and a large rate of change can create a high switching Voltage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The effect is caused by Self-Induction.
\- Induced EMF depends on di/dt.
\- Suppression devices may be required to protect switching components.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Fast Current Change → High Inductive Voltage

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the energy conversions in a motor and a generator.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A motor mainly converts electrical energy into mechanical energy through electromagnetic force and torque. A generator mainly converts mechanical energy into electrical energy through changing Flux Linkage and induced EMF.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Motor: Electrical to Mechanical.
\- Generator: Mechanical to Electrical.
\- Both depend on Magnetic Fields and have practical losses.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Motor and Generator Perform Opposite Main Conversions

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

How can the direction and magnitude of an induced EMF be determined?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Use Faraday's Law to determine the magnitude from the rate of change of Flux Linkage. Use Lenz's Law with the selected reference direction and the Right-Hand Grip Rule to determine the induced polarity or Current direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Faraday's Law gives magnitude.
\- Lenz's Law gives opposing polarity or direction.
\- Reference direction and sign convention must remain consistent.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Faraday → Magnitude; Lenz → Direction
