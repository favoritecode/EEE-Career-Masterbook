\## Electrical Wiring, Cables and Installation

\---

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Wiring?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Wiring is the system of Conductors, Cables, containment, accessories and protective devices that safely distributes electrical Energy from a supply point to loads.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Carries electricity to utilization equipment
\- Includes containment and accessories
\- Requires Overcurrent and fault protection
\- Must limit Heat and Voltage drop

\*\*Remember\*\*

Wiring is a complete protected system, not only a wire.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the essential requirements of a safe wiring system?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A safe wiring system needs adequate ampacity, acceptable Voltage drop, effective Earthing and fault protection, suitable insulation, mechanical and environmental protection, correct identification and accessibility for maintenance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Thermal capacity
\- Electrical protection
\- Mechanical protection
\- Inspection and identification

\*\*Remember\*\*

Ampacity alone does not prove a wiring system is safe.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Cleat, Batten and Casing-and-Capping Wiring.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Cleat Wiring supports insulated Conductors on cleats and is mainly temporary. Batten Wiring fixes sheathed Cable to a batten for simple dry locations. Casing-and-Capping Wiring encloses Conductors in a covered channel and provides a neat, accessible route where permitted.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Cleat is inexpensive and temporary
\- Batten is simple but lightly protected
\- Casing and Capping is enclosed and accessible
\- Local code acceptance must be checked

\*\*Remember\*\*

Select the wiring method from environment, protection and permitted use.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Surface and Concealed Conduit Wiring?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Surface Conduit is installed visibly on walls or structures and is easier to inspect and modify. Concealed Conduit is embedded inside walls or slabs, giving a cleaner appearance but making alteration and fault access more difficult.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both route and protect Conductors
\- Surface Conduit improves access
\- Concealed Conduit improves appearance
\- Metallic Conduit needs correct bonding and Earthing

\*\*Remember\*\*

Surface favors maintenance; concealed favors appearance.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Wire Gauge and Current-Carrying Capacity?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Wire Gauge identifies conductor size under a defined system. Current-Carrying Capacity or ampacity is the permitted continuous Current under stated material, insulation, temperature and installation conditions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Gauge describes size
\- Ampacity describes thermal Current limit
\- Metric size is commonly stated in mm²
\- Installation conditions change ampacity

\*\*Remember\*\*

The same conductor size can have different ampacity in different installations.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

State the commonly accepted IEC-based colour identification for AC Conductors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Green-and-yellow commonly identifies the Protective Conductor, blue identifies Neutral, brown identifies a Single-Phase Line, and brown, black and grey identify Three-Phase Lines. Local regulations and legacy systems may differ.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Protective: green/yellow
\- Neutral: blue
\- Single-Phase Line: brown
\- Three-Phase Lines: brown, black, grey

\*\*Remember\*\*

Colour is identification assistance, not proof that a conductor is dead.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between a Cable joint and a Cable termination?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Cable joint connects two Cable lengths, while a termination connects a Cable end to equipment, a Busbar, an accessory or another endpoint.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Joint connects Cable to Cable
\- Termination connects Cable to equipment
\- Both need correct contact pressure
\- Sealing and strain control are important

\*\*Remember\*\*

Many Cable failures begin at joints and terminations.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the functions of a Distribution Board?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Distribution Board receives an incoming supply, provides isolation and divides it into protected outgoing circuits. It also provides organized Neutral, Earth, labeling and optional residual-current or surge protection arrangements.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Receives incoming supply
\- Divides outgoing circuits
\- Houses protective devices
\- Supports safe identification and isolation

\*\*Remember\*\*

DB = distribution plus coordinated protection.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why are domestic circuits divided into lighting, Socket and fixed-appliance circuits?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Circuit division permits suitable Cable and protection selection, limits the effects of a fault, reduces inconvenience and improves safe isolation and maintenance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Loads have different Current duties
\- Faults affect a smaller area
\- Isolation becomes clearer
\- Protection can match each circuit

\*\*Remember\*\*

One fault should not unnecessarily disconnect an entire home.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

How does Industrial Wiring differ from Domestic Wiring?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Industrial Wiring commonly handles larger Currents, higher fault levels, Motors, control and instrumentation circuits, vibration, harsh environments and critical production loads. It therefore needs robust containment, segregation, protection and maintenance planning.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Larger Power and fault duty
\- Motor starting and harmonics
\- Power-control segregation
\- Greater environmental and reliability demands

\*\*Remember\*\*

Industrial Wiring is designed around process duty and site risk.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the purpose of a continuity test?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A continuity test confirms that a conductor path is electrically unbroken and has acceptably low Resistance. It is performed on a safely isolated circuit using a suitable instrument.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Confirms an unbroken path
\- Important for Protective Conductors
\- Performed as a dead test
\- Parallel paths can affect readings

\*\*Remember\*\*

Continuity verifies connection, not insulation quality.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Short Circuit, Open Circuit and Overload.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Short Circuit is an unintended low-impedance path that can produce very high Current. An Open Circuit is a broken path that stops normal Current. An overload is excessive Current through an otherwise intended circuit path.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Short Circuit gives high fault Current
\- Open Circuit interrupts the path
\- Overload uses the normal path above rating
\- Protection response may differ

\*\*Remember\*\*

Short = wrong low-impedance path; open = no path; overload = too much Current in the normal path.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is a loose connection dangerous?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A loose connection increases contact Resistance and produces localized I²R heating. It can cause flicker, arcing, insulation damage, equipment failure and fire.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- High contact Resistance
\- Localized Heat
\- Intermittent operation
\- Fire and Arc risk

\*\*Remember\*\*

Correct connector, preparation and torque prevent many hot joints.

What should be checked before connecting a new industrial machine?

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Verify system capacity
\- Select and protect the Cable
\- Confirm fault and starting duty
\- Inspect, test and document

A new machine connection requires a system check, not only a Cable route.
