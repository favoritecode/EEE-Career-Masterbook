\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Safety?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Safety is the knowledge, procedures, equipment and behavior used to prevent Electric Shock, burns, fire, equipment damage and other harm caused by electrical energy.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It protects people, equipment and property.
\- It begins with Hazard recognition and planning.
\- De-energization is normally preferred before work.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Electrical Safety = Recognize, Isolate, Verify and Control

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Hazard and Risk?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Hazard is a source or condition with the potential to cause harm. Risk considers both the likelihood that harm will occur and the severity of its possible consequences.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Exposed energized parts are a Hazard.
\- Risk depends on exposure and consequence.
\- Controls are selected after hazards are identified.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Hazard = Potential Harm; Risk = Likelihood and Severity

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What are common electrical hazards?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Common electrical hazards include Electric Shock, direct and indirect contact, short circuit, overload, Earth Fault, Arc Flash, electrical fire and stored electrical energy.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Hazards may be visible or hidden.
\- Damaged insulation and loose connections are dangerous.
\- Batteries and capacitors can retain energy.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Shock, Arc, Heat, Fire and Stored Energy

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Personal Protective Equipment?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Personal Protective Equipment, or PPE, is equipment worn or used to reduce a person's exposure to a specific Hazard. Electrical PPE may include insulating gloves, eye protection, Arc-Rated clothing, helmets and protective footwear.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- PPE must match the assessed Hazard.
\- Rating, fit and condition are important.
\- PPE does not replace isolation.

\*\*Remember\*\*

PPE Reduces Exposure; It Does Not Remove the Hazard

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the purpose of a Wire Stripper?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Wire Stripper removes insulation from a conductor while reducing the chance of cutting, nicking or weakening the conductor strands.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The correct slot must match conductor size.
\- Damaged strands reduce connection quality.
\- The conductor should be de-energized before preparation.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Correct Stripper Size Protects the Conductor

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Ammeter and how is it connected?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Ammeter measures Electric Current and is connected in series with the branch so the branch Current passes through the instrument.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Its unit is Ampere.
\- A practical Ammeter has low internal Resistance.
\- Its Current rating must not be exceeded.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Ammeter → Series

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Voltmeter and how is it connected?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Voltmeter measures Potential Difference between two points and is connected in parallel across those points.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Its unit is Volt.
\- A practical Voltmeter has high input Resistance.
\- AC or DC and the correct range must be selected.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Voltmeter → Parallel

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the SI Units of Current, Voltage, Resistance and Power?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The SI Unit of Current is ampere (A), Voltage is volt (V), Resistance is ohm (Ω) and Power is watt (W).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Unit symbols are case-sensitive.
\- A space is normally used between a value and unit symbol.
\- Prefixes scale units by powers of ten.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Current–A, Voltage–V, Resistance–Ω, Power–W

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must a circuit be treated as energized until absence of Voltage is verified?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Opening a switch or operating a control does not prove that every source is isolated. Back-feed, induced Voltage, stored energy, wiring errors or incorrect labels may leave parts hazardous, so a suitable verification procedure is required.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- OFF is not proof of zero energy.
\- All possible sources must be considered.
\- Verification requires suitable test equipment.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Treat as Energized Until Verified

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the basic purpose of Lockout and Tagout.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Lockout prevents operation of an energy-isolating device, while Tagout communicates the identity, reason and status of the isolation. Together they help prevent unexpected energization during authorized work.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- All energy sources must be identified.
\- Stored energy must be controlled.
\- Isolation must be verified.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Identify → Isolate → Lock and Tag → Release Energy → Verify

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

What factors affect the severity of Electric Shock?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Shock severity depends on Current magnitude, contact duration, Current path through the body, AC or DC and Frequency, skin condition, contact area and the surrounding environment.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Current through the body causes injury.
\- Duration and path are critical.
\- Wet skin can reduce body Resistance.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Shock Effect Depends on Current, Time, Path and Condition

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is PPE considered the last layer of protection?

\*\*Answer\*\*

PPE does not remove electrical energy and may fail, become damaged or be used incorrectly. Eliminating or isolating the Hazard and applying engineering controls provide stronger protection before PPE is considered for remaining Risk.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Isolation controls the source.
\- PPE has limited ratings.
\- Selection follows a Risk assessment.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Eliminate, Isolate, Control, Then Protect

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must an Ohmmeter not be connected to an energized circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Ohmmeter uses an internal source to apply a test signal. External Voltage can cause false readings, damage the meter and create a safety Hazard. The circuit must be isolated and stored energy safely discharged.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Resistance testing requires power OFF.
\- Capacitors may retain charge.
\- Parallel paths can affect the reading.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Ohmmeter → De-Energized Circuit Only

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is a Current-input Multimeter dangerous when connected directly across a Voltage source?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Current input has very low Resistance. Connecting it in parallel across a source can create a high fault Current, resulting in fuse operation, arcing, meter damage or injury.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Current is measured in series.
\- The Current socket is usually fused.
\- Socket position must be checked before probing.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Current Mode Across Voltage Source = Short-Circuit Hazard

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

How does a Clamp Meter measure Current?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Clamp Meter senses the magnetic field produced by Current in a conductor and converts that field into a Current reading. The jaw normally surrounds one conductor.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It avoids opening the Circuit for many measurements.
\- Equal outgoing and return fields can cancel.
\- Not every Clamp Meter measures DC Current.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Clamp One Conductor, Not the Whole Cable

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between resolution and accuracy?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Resolution is the smallest change an instrument can display or distinguish. Accuracy describes how close the measured value is expected to be to the true value under specified conditions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- More digits can improve resolution.
\- Calibration affects accuracy.
\- High resolution does not guarantee high accuracy.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Resolution Shows Detail; Accuracy Shows Closeness

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the correct SI notation for 4.7 kilohms.

\*\*Answer\*\*

It is written as 4.7 kΩ. A space is placed between the numerical value and unit expression, lowercase k represents kilo and uppercase Greek omega is the ohm unit symbol.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- kilo represents 10³.
\- Prefix symbols are case-sensitive.
\- Unit symbols are not pluralized.

\*\*Remember\*\*

4.7 kΩ = 4700 Ω

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What should be done if an electrical task differs from the approved work plan?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Work should stop. The changed condition must be assessed, suitable controls selected and proper authorization obtained before the task continues.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Unexpected conditions can introduce new hazards.
\- Production pressure does not justify unsafe work.
\- Interlocks and protective devices must not be bypassed casually.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Stop, Assess, Control and Authorize
