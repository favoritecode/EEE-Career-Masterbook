\# Chapter 03

\# Glossary

\## Electrical Safety

The knowledge, procedures, equipment and behavior used to prevent injury, fire, equipment damage and other harm caused by electrical energy.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Hazard

A source or condition with the potential to cause injury, damage or another form of harm.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Risk

The combination of the likelihood of a harmful event and the severity of its possible consequences.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Electric Shock

The harmful physiological effect produced when Electric Current passes through the human body.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Electrocution

Death caused by Electric Shock or electrical injury.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Direct Contact

Contact by a person with a conductor or part that is energized during normal operation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Indirect Contact

Contact with an accessible conductive part that has become energized because of a fault.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Short Circuit

An unintended low-Impedance connection that can produce excessive Current between points of different Potential.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: SC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Overload

An operating condition in which equipment or a conductor carries Current above its intended rating without necessarily having a short circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A), according to the affected circuit

\## Earth Fault

An unintended electrical connection between an energized conductor and Earth or an earthed conductive part.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Arc Flash

The intense thermal and radiant energy released by an electrical arc.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Incident energy may be expressed in J/cm² or cal/cm²

\## Arc Blast

The pressure, sound and movement of material associated with a high-energy electrical arc.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Application-dependent

\## Lockout and Tagout

An energy-control process using locks and tags to help prevent unexpected energization or operation during authorized work.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: LOTO

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## De-Energized

A condition in which equipment is disconnected from electrical energy sources and the required safe-state procedure has been completed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Energized

Electrically connected to, or having a source of, Voltage or electrical energy.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Qualified Person

A person with suitable knowledge, training and demonstrated ability to recognize electrical hazards and perform assigned work safely.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Personal Protective Equipment

Equipment worn or used to reduce a person's exposure to a specific Hazard.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PPE

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Insulating Gloves

Voltage-rated protective gloves designed and maintained to provide electrical insulation for specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Voltage class or rating in Volt (V)

\## Arc-Rated Clothing

Protective clothing evaluated for exposure to electrical-arc thermal energy under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: AR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Arc rating commonly expressed in cal/cm²

\## Protective Earth

An Earthing connection intended primarily to protect people against Electric Shock under fault conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PE

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Circuit Ground

A reference point or common return in a Circuit that is not automatically identical to Protective Earth.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: GND

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Schematic Diagram

A technical drawing that uses symbols and connections to represent the electrical functions of a Circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Junction

A point where two or more conductors are electrically connected.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Commonly represented by a junction dot

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Basic Electrical Tool

A hand tool or supporting device used to prepare, connect, install, inspect or maintain electrical wiring and equipment.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Wire Stripper

A tool used to remove conductor insulation while reducing damage to conductor strands.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Conductor size or cross-sectional capacity

\## Crimping Tool

A tool that applies controlled compression to join a compatible terminal or lug to a conductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Die and conductor size are application-dependent

\## Insulated Tool

A tool constructed, tested and marked to provide specified electrical insulation when used within its rating and instructions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Rated Voltage in Volt (V)

\## Ammeter

An instrument used to measure Electric Current and normally connected in series with the measured branch.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: A inside an instrument circle

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Voltmeter

An instrument used to measure Potential Difference and connected in parallel between two points.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V inside an instrument circle

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Ohmmeter

An instrument that uses an internal test source to measure Resistance on an isolated circuit or component.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ω inside an instrument circle

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Multimeter

An instrument combining multiple functions such as Voltage, Current, Resistance and continuity measurement.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: DMM for Digital Multimeter

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the selected quantity

\## Clamp Meter

An instrument that measures Current by sensing the magnetic field around a conductor without normally opening the Circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Test Lead

An insulated conductor and probe assembly used to connect a measuring instrument to test points.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Rated by Voltage, Current and measurement category

\## Measurement Range

The interval between the minimum and maximum values that an instrument or selected setting can measure as specified.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Same as the measured quantity

\## Resolution

The smallest change in a quantity that an instrument can display or distinguish.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Same as the measured quantity or display counts

\## Accuracy

The degree of closeness between a measured value and the true quantity value under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Often stated as a percentage and additional digits or counts

\## Calibration

The documented comparison that establishes the relationship between an instrument's indication and values provided by measurement standards.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Measurement Category

A classification used with specified test equipment to indicate suitability for particular transient environments and points within an electrical installation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: CAT followed by a category designation

\*\*Unit\*\*: Category and rated Voltage

\## Continuity

The presence of a complete conductive path with Resistance within the detection criteria of the test instrument.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Often represented by a sound-wave or buzzer symbol

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω), according to the instrument threshold

\## Absence-of-Voltage Verification

A controlled test using suitable equipment to verify that exposed Circuit parts are not energized before work proceeds.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## International System of Units

The internationally accepted measurement system used to express physical and electrical quantities consistently.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: SI

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Electrical Quantity

A measurable property of an electrical phenomenon, component or Circuit, such as Current, Voltage or Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Depends on the quantity

\*\*Unit\*\*: Depends on the quantity

\## Unit Symbol

A standardized letter or group of letters representing a measurement unit, such as A, V, Ω or W.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not Applicable

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not Applicable

\## Ampere

The SI Unit of Electric Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: A

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\## Volt

The SI derived Unit of Voltage, Potential Difference and Electromotive Force.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\## Ohm

The SI derived Unit of electrical Resistance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ω

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\## Watt

The SI derived Unit of Power, equal to one Joule per Second.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: W

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W) or Joule per Second (J/s)

\## Milli

The SI prefix representing one-thousandth or 10⁻³ of a unit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: m

\*\*Unit\*\*: 10⁻³

\## Kilo

The SI prefix representing one thousand or 10³ times a unit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: k

\*\*Unit\*\*: 10³

\## Mega

The SI prefix representing one million or 10⁶ times a unit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: M

\*\*Unit\*\*: 10⁶
