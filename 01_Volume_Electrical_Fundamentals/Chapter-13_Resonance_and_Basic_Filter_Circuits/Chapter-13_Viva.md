\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Resonance in an AC Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Resonance is the condition at which Inductive Reactance and Capacitive Reactance are equal in magnitude. Their opposite reactive effects cancel, so the input behaves as purely Resistive under the ideal tuned condition.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- XL = XC at Resonance.
\- Net Reactance becomes zero.
\- Resonance occurs at a particular Frequency.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Resonance → XL = XC

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Series Resonance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Series Resonance occurs in a Series RLC Circuit when XL = XC. The net Reactance becomes zero, total Impedance becomes minimum and purely Resistive, and supply Current becomes maximum for a fixed supply Voltage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Z = R at Resonance.
\- I = V/R.
\- Source Voltage and Current are in phase.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series Resonance → Minimum Z, Maximum I

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Why can Inductor and Capacitor Voltages exceed supply Voltage at Series Resonance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The minimum Series Impedance produces a large Current. Therefore, VL = IXL and VC = IXC may each be large. They are equal in magnitude and opposite in phase, so they cancel in the total KVL equation even though each component Voltage may exceed the supply.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Resonant Current can be large.
\- VL and VC are opposite Phasors.
\- Component Voltage ratings must be checked.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Phasor Cancellation Does Not Remove Component Stress

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Parallel Resonance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Parallel Resonance occurs when the reactive Inductive and Capacitive branch Current components cancel at the input. Input Impedance becomes maximum and supply Current minimum under the ideal tuned condition.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Input Admittance becomes purely Conductive.
\- Input Power Factor is unity.
\- Large reactive Current may circulate between L and C.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Parallel Resonance → Maximum Z, Minimum Supply I

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Series and Parallel Resonance.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Series Resonance produces minimum input Impedance and maximum supply Current. Parallel Resonance produces maximum input Impedance and minimum supply Current in the ideal model. Both have reactive cancellation and unity input Power Factor at Resonance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Series Resonance magnifies component Voltage.
\- Parallel Resonance magnifies branch Current.
\- Their input-Impedance responses are opposite.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series: Low Z; Parallel: High Z

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

State the formula for Resonant Frequency.

\*\*Answer\*\*

For the ideal basic RLC model, f₀ = 1/(2π√LC). Angular Resonant Frequency is ω₀ = 1/√LC and f₀ = ω₀/(2π).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The formula comes from XL = XC.
\- Increasing L lowers f₀.
\- Increasing C lowers f₀.

\*\*Remember\*\*

f₀ = 1/(2π√LC)

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Quality Factor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Quality Factor is a dimensionless measure of Resonance sharpness and relative energy loss. A high-Q Circuit has low relative loss, narrow Bandwidth and high Selectivity.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Q is dimensionless.
\- For Series RLC, Q = ω₀L/R.
\- For a standard response, Q = f₀/BW.

\*\*Remember\*\*

High Q → Sharp Resonance

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Bandwidth?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Bandwidth is the difference between the upper cutoff Frequency and lower cutoff Frequency: BW = fH − fL. It states the width of a defined response band.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Bandwidth is measured in Hertz.
\- Cutoff criterion must be stated.
\- Q = f₀/BW for the standard Resonant response.

\*\*Remember\*\*

BW = fH − fL

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is the cutoff point commonly called the −3 dB point?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At the standard half-power point, Power is half its reference maximum. For the same Resistive reference, amplitude is 1/√2 or approximately 0.707 of maximum, corresponding to about −3 dB.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Half Power = 0.5 of maximum.
\- Amplitude ratio ≈ 0.707.
\- The exact measured quantity and reference must be stated.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Half Power → 0.707 Amplitude → −3 dB

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Selectivity?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Selectivity is the ability of a Circuit or Filter to respond to desired Frequencies while attenuating nearby unwanted Frequencies. At a fixed center Frequency, higher Q and narrower Bandwidth normally give greater Selectivity.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Selectivity is Frequency discrimination.
\- Loading can reduce Selectivity.
\- Excessive Selectivity may remove useful signal content.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Narrower Response → Greater Selectivity

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Filter is a Frequency-selective Circuit that passes a desired range of Frequencies and attenuates unwanted Frequencies. Basic response types are Low Pass, High Pass, Band Pass and Band Stop.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Passband is accepted.
\- Stopband is attenuated.
\- Transition Band connects them.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Filter → Pass What Is Needed, Reduce What Is Not

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Low Pass Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Low Pass Filter passes lower Frequencies with relatively low attenuation and reduces higher Frequencies. In a basic RC Low Pass Circuit, output is taken across the Capacitor.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- At low Frequency, XC is large.
\- At high Frequency, the Capacitor diverts more signal.
\- For the simple unloaded model, fc = 1/(2πRC).

\*\*Remember\*\*

RC Low Pass → Output Across C

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a High Pass Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A High Pass Filter passes higher Frequencies with relatively low attenuation and reduces lower Frequencies. In a basic RC High Pass Circuit, the Capacitor is in Series and output is taken across the resistor.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The Series Capacitor blocks ideal steady DC.
\- At high Frequency, XC becomes small.
\- For the simple unloaded model, fc = 1/(2πRC).

\*\*Remember\*\*

RC High Pass → Series C, Output Across R

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Band Pass Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Band Pass Filter passes a selected Frequency range between fL and fH while attenuating Frequencies below and above that band. Its Bandwidth is fH − fL.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A Resonant RLC Circuit can form a Band Pass response.
\- High Pass and Low Pass stages may be cascaded.
\- Center Frequency is commonly √(fLfH).

\*\*Remember\*\*

Band Pass → Middle Band Passes

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Band Stop Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Band Stop Filter attenuates a selected middle Frequency band while passing Frequencies below and above it. A narrow Band Stop Filter is commonly called a Notch Filter.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It is also called a Band Reject Filter.
\- Notch depth depends on matching and loss.
\- It is used to remove a narrow interference tone.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Band Stop → Middle Band Rejected

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Low Pass, High Pass, Band Pass and Band Stop Filters.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Low Pass passes lower Frequencies, High Pass passes higher Frequencies, Band Pass passes a selected middle band, and Band Stop rejects a selected middle band while passing Frequencies on both sides.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Each Filter has a defined Passband.
\- Cutoff Frequencies define response boundaries.
\- Application requirements select the response type.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Low, High, Middle-Pass or Middle-Stop

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

How does loading affect a passive Filter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The load combines with Filter components and changes effective Resistance, gain, cutoff Frequency, Q and Bandwidth. Source Resistance also forms part of the real network, so an unloaded formula may not predict the loaded response accurately.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Source and load Impedance are part of the Circuit.
\- Loading can reduce gain and Q.
\- Buffering can reduce stage interaction.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Always Analyze the Complete Loaded Network

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does practical Resonant Frequency differ from the calculated ideal value?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Real components have tolerance, winding Resistance, ESR, core loss and parasitic Capacitance or Inductance. Source and load connections also change the network, while temperature, Voltage, Current and aging may change component values.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ideal formulas use simplified models.
\- Parasitic elements alter response.
\- Measurement connections can cause loading.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Calculated f₀ Is a Starting Point—Measure the Loaded Circuit

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Give practical applications of Resonance and Filters.

\*\*Answer\*\*

They are used in radio tuning, communication channel selection, power-supply ripple reduction, audio crossover networks, sensor signal conditioning, mains-hum rejection, resonant converters, induction heating and harmonic filtering.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Resonance provides tuning and energy exchange.
\- Filters shape the Frequency spectrum.
\- Practical design depends on source, load and ratings.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Tune, Pass, Reject, Measure and Protect

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety and reliability checks are required for Resonant Circuits?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Check component Voltage, Current, Power, insulation, ESR, winding Resistance, core saturation and temperature. Use correctly rated instruments and protection because internal Resonant Voltage or Current may exceed the supply quantity. High-power work requires approved engineering and safety procedures.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Resonant magnification creates hidden stress.
\- Loss produces heating.
\- Simulation and controlled measurement should verify design.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Resonance Is Useful Only Within Safe Ratings
