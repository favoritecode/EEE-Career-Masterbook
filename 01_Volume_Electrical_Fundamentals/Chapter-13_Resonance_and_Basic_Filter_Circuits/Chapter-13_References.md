\# Reference Notes

\- Resonance occurs when Inductive Reactance and Capacitive Reactance are equal at a particular frequency.

\- The ideal resonant frequency of an RLC Circuit is f_r = 1/(2π√LC).

\- At Series Resonance, the net Reactance becomes zero and the Circuit Impedance is minimum.

\- At Parallel Resonance, the input Impedance is maximum in the ideal parallel RLC model.

\- A practical Inductor has winding Resistance and parasitic Capacitance, so measured behavior may differ from an ideal calculation.

\- Quality Factor indicates the sharpness of Resonance and the relative energy storage of a resonant Circuit.

\- For a simple resonant response, Bandwidth is the difference between the upper and lower half-power frequencies: BW = f_2 − f_1.

\- For a commonly used Series RLC approximation, Q = f_r/BW when the component model and measurement conditions are consistent.

\- The half-power frequencies are also called the −3 dB frequencies because output Power becomes one-half of its reference value.

\- Selectivity describes how effectively a Circuit accepts a desired frequency range and rejects unwanted frequencies.

\- A Low Pass Filter passes lower frequencies and attenuates frequencies above its Cutoff Frequency.

\- A High Pass Filter passes higher frequencies and attenuates frequencies below its Cutoff Frequency.

\- A Band Pass Filter passes a selected band, while a Band Stop Filter rejects a selected band.

\- A Notch Filter is a narrow Band Stop Filter used to suppress a particular unwanted frequency, such as Power-line interference.

\- Filter Cutoff and Resonant Frequency are different concepts, although both are expressed in Hertz and may appear in the same Circuit.

\- Source Resistance, Load Resistance and component tolerance can shift practical Cutoff Frequency, Bandwidth and Quality Factor.

\- Passive Filters do not provide Power gain; any apparent Voltage magnification near Resonance must be interpreted using Circuit energy exchange and loading.

\- Frequency response should be measured by changing one input frequency at a time while keeping the source amplitude and measurement method consistent.
