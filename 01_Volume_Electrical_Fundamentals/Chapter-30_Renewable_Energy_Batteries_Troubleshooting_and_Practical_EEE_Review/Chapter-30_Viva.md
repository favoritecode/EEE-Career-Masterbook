\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Renewable Energy, and why is it important?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Renewable Energy comes from naturally replenished sources such as sunlight, wind, flowing water, Biomass, geothermal heat and tides. It can diversify Energy supply, reduce dependence on finite fuels, support distributed electrification and lower operational greenhouse-gas emissions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Naturally replenished resources
\- Supports Energy security
\- Enables distributed generation
\- Still requires environmental assessment

\*\*Remember\*\*

Renewable does not mean continuous output or zero impact.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Solar, Wind, Hydro, Biomass, Geothermal and Tidal Energy.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Solar and Wind are widely deployable but weather dependent. Hydropower is efficient and can be dispatchable with storage, but it is site specific and may have major environmental effects. Biomass can be dispatchable but needs sustainable feedstock and emission control. Geothermal can provide steady output at suitable sites but requires costly exploration. Tidal Energy is predictable but limited to suitable marine locations.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Solar and Wind are variable
\- Hydro may provide flexible generation
\- Biomass requires sustainable fuel supply
\- Geothermal and Tidal are strongly site dependent

\*\*Remember\*\*

Technology selection begins with resource and site assessment.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the working principle of a Solar Photovoltaic system.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Light absorbed in a Semiconductor PV cell creates charge carriers. The cell's internal electric field separates them and produces DC Voltage and Current. Cells form modules, modules form strings or arrays, and Power Electronics condition the output for a Load, Battery or grid.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Photovoltaic effect
\- Direct light-to-DC conversion
\- Cell, module, string and array
\- Power Electronics interface

\*\*Remember\*\*

PV modules produce DC electricity without a rotating generator.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is MPPT, and why is it used in a Solar system?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Maximum Power Point Tracking is a Power Electronic control method that adjusts the PV operating Voltage and Current to extract near-maximum available Power as irradiance, temperature and Load conditions change.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Searches for a high-Power operating point
\- Responds to changing conditions
\- Common in Solar Inverters and charge controllers
\- Does not create Energy beyond what the array can supply

\*\*Remember\*\*

MPPT optimizes the operating point; it does not increase sunlight.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Battery, and what are its basic parts?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Battery is an electrochemical Energy-storage device containing one or more cells. A cell includes positive and negative Electrodes, electrolyte, separator, current collectors and terminals. During discharge it converts chemical Energy into electrical Energy; a rechargeable Battery reverses part of the process during charging.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electrochemical storage
\- Positive and negative Electrodes
\- Electrolyte and separator
\- Charge and discharge processes

\*\*Remember\*\*

Cells in series increase Voltage; suitable parallel connections increase Capacity and Current capability.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Lead-Acid, Lithium-Ion and Nickel-Cadmium Batteries.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Lead-Acid is mature, economical and capable of high surge Current but is heavy and lower in Energy density. Lithium-Ion provides high Energy density and Efficiency but needs precise charging, protection and thermal management. Nickel-Cadmium is rugged and works over a wide temperature range in suitable designs, but Cadmium is toxic and tightly controlled.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Lead-Acid: mature and heavy
\- Lithium-Ion: high Energy density and BMS dependent
\- Nickel-Cadmium: rugged but toxic material
\- Charging and hazards are chemistry specific

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never apply one chemistry's charging method to another chemistry.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Battery Capacity in Ah and Energy in Wh.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Ampere-hour is a charge Capacity rating, approximately Current multiplied by time under specified conditions. Watt-hour is an Energy rating. Simplified nominal Battery Energy is Voltage multiplied by Ampere-hour Capacity: E_Wh ≈ V_nom × C_Ah.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ah represents charge Capacity
\- Wh represents Energy
\- Voltage is required to convert Ah into nominal Wh
\- Actual usable Energy is lower than simple nominal Energy

\*\*Remember\*\*

A 24 V, 100 Ah bank has 2400 Wh of simplified nominal Energy.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is C-rate? Give an example.

\*\*Answer\*\*

C-rate expresses charge or discharge Current relative to rated Capacity. For a 100 Ah Battery, 1C corresponds to 100 A, 0.5C to 50 A and 0.1C to 10 A. Actual permitted rates depend on chemistry, temperature and manufacturer limits.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Current relative to Capacity
\- 1C equals rated Ah expressed in Amperes
\- High rate can reduce usable Capacity or increase heating
\- Manufacturer limits control safe operation

\*\*Remember\*\*

C-rate is not the same as Coulomb or Capacitor symbol C.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain basic Battery charging and maintenance principles.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Charging Voltage, Current, temperature compensation and termination must match the exact chemistry and manufacturer instructions. Maintenance includes visual inspection, connection checks, temperature and charger verification, cell or block comparison, Capacity testing when required, cleaning and trend records.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Chemistry-specific charging
\- Lead-Acid may use bulk, absorption and float stages
\- Lithium-Ion commonly uses controlled CC-CV with BMS
\- Inspection and trending support reliability

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never improvise Battery charging limits.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the main Battery hazards and safe practices?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Battery hazards include very high short-circuit Current, Arc Flash, corrosive or alkaline electrolyte, toxic materials, gas ignition, fire, thermal runaway and heavy lifting. Use insulated tools, remove jewelry, verify polarity, provide protection and ventilation, wear suitable PPE, isolate safely and follow emergency and recycling procedures.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- High fault Current
\- Chemistry-specific fire and chemical hazards
\- Correct Fuse and disconnecting means
\- Ventilation, PPE and emergency planning

\*\*Remember\*\*

Low Battery Voltage does not guarantee low hazard.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Troubleshooting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Troubleshooting is the safe and systematic process of identifying abnormal operation, locating its cause, correcting the root problem and verifying restored performance. It uses knowledge of normal operation, drawings, history, inspection, measurements and logical testing.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Safe and systematic diagnosis
\- Symptom versus root cause
\- Evidence from drawings and measurements
\- Repair verification and documentation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Random component replacement is not professional troubleshooting.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Describe a systematic troubleshooting procedure.

\*\*Answer\*\*

First make the situation safe. Confirm the symptom and review normal operation, drawings, ratings and history. Inspect safely, divide the system into blocks, form a supported hypothesis, select a safe test, compare results with expected values, correct the root cause, verify operation and document the work.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Safety assessment first
\- Confirm symptom and normal behavior
\- Divide and test logically
\- Correct, verify and document

\*\*Remember\*\*

Make one controlled change at a time.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare an Open Circuit, Short Circuit, Overload and Earth Fault.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Open Circuit breaks the intended path and stops Load Current. A Short Circuit creates an unintended low-impedance path and can produce very high Current. An Overload draws more than the normal rated Current through the intended path. An Earth Fault sends Current through an unintended path to Earth or exposed conductive parts.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Open: broken path
\- Short: low-impedance unintended path
\- Overload: excessive Current in intended path
\- Earth fault: unintended Current to Earth

\*\*Remember\*\*

Protection and diagnosis differ for each fault type.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety procedure should be followed before de-energized electrical testing?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify every Energy source, isolate, apply Lockout/Tagout, control stored and mechanical Energy, wait for specified discharge, prove a correctly rated tester, verify absence of Voltage at every relevant point and re-prove the tester. Use required PPE and restore guards and Earthing before operation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Full Energy-source identification
\- Lockout/Tagout
\- Stored-Energy control
\- Prove-test-re-prove

\*\*Remember\*\*

Pressing Stop or opening a control switch is not safe electrical isolation.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

How would you investigate a three-Phase Motor that is overheating?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Make the work safe, confirm temperature and operating history, compare Load with rating, inspect mechanical freedom and cooling, measure phase Currents and Voltages using an authorized procedure, review protection settings and test the Motor and cable after safe isolation. Check overload, phase imbalance, phase loss, bearings, low Voltage and blocked ventilation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electrical, mechanical and process causes
\- Current and Voltage balance
\- Cooling and bearing condition
\- Rating and protection setting

\*\*Remember\*\*

Do not repeatedly reset an overload without finding the cause.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate corrective, preventive, predictive and proactive maintenance.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Corrective maintenance repairs after failure. Preventive maintenance follows planned intervals. Predictive or condition-based maintenance uses measured trends to plan work. Proactive maintenance removes recurring root causes, such as correcting misalignment that repeatedly damages bearings.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Corrective: after failure
\- Preventive: scheduled
\- Predictive: condition trend
\- Proactive: root-cause removal

\*\*Remember\*\*

Trend data is usually more meaningful than one isolated reading.
