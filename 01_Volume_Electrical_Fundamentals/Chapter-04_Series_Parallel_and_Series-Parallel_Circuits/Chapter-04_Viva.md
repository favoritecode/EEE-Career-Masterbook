\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Electric Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Electric Circuit is an interconnection of electrical components that provides one or more paths for electric current and transfers electrical energy from a source to a load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A source supplies electrical energy.
\- Conductors provide the Current path.
\- A load converts electrical energy into a useful form.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Circuit = Source + Path + Load

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the basic components of an Electric Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A basic Electric Circuit contains a source, conducting wires and a load. It may also contain a switch or other control device and a fuse or circuit breaker for protection.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Source establishes Potential Difference.
\- Conductors connect circuit elements.
\- Control and protection are added according to circuit requirements.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Source Supplies; Load Converts; Switch Controls; Fuse or Breaker Protects

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Open Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Open Circuit is a circuit with a broken or incomplete conducting path. Continuous Current cannot flow through an ideal Open Circuit, so its Current is zero.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The conducting path is incomplete.
\- Ideal Open-Circuit Current is zero.
\- Ideal Open-Circuit Resistance is infinite.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Open Path → No Continuous Current

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Closed Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Closed Circuit has a complete conducting path. When a source provides Voltage and the path has finite Resistance, Current can flow through the intended load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The Current path is complete.
\- The load can receive electrical energy.
\- A closed switch normally permits load operation.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Closed Path + Applied Voltage → Current

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Short Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Short Circuit is an abnormal connection that creates a very low-Resistance path between points of different potential. It can allow dangerously high Fault Current to flow.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- The fault path has very low Resistance.
\- Current may become extremely high.
\- Short Circuits can cause heat, arc, fire and equipment damage.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Very Low R → Very High Fault Current

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Series Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Series Circuit is a circuit in which components are connected end-to-end to create only one Current path. The same Current flows through every series component.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A Series Circuit has one path.
\- Current is common to all components.
\- Source Voltage divides across the components.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series → One Path → Same Current

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Parallel Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Parallel Circuit has two or more Current paths. Its components are connected across the same two nodes, so the same Voltage appears across every branch.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A Parallel Circuit has multiple branches.
\- Voltage is common to all branches.
\- Total Current is the sum of branch Currents.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Parallel → Same Two Nodes → Same Voltage

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

How is equivalent Resistance calculated in Series and Parallel Circuits?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Series Resistances are added directly: R total = R₁ + R₂ + ... . For Parallel Resistances, the reciprocal relationship is 1/R total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ... .

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Series connection increases total Resistance.
\- Parallel connection decreases equivalent Resistance.
\- Parallel R total is lower than the smallest branch Resistance.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series R Adds; Parallel Conductance Adds

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Series-Parallel Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Series-Parallel Circuit contains both Series and Parallel connections in the same network. It is also called a Combination Circuit.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Some components share the same Current.
\- Some components share the same Voltage.
\- The network can often be reduced step by step.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series-Parallel = Combination of Both Connection Types

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

How are an Ammeter and a Voltmeter connected?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Ammeter is connected in series with the load so that it carries the same Current. A Voltmeter is connected in parallel across the two points whose Potential Difference is required.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ammeter has very low internal Resistance.
\- Voltmeter has very high internal Resistance.
\- Incorrect connection can damage equipment or create a hazard.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Ammeter → Series; Voltmeter → Parallel

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is the same Current present throughout a Series Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Series Circuit has no branch point where Current can divide. Charge cannot continuously accumulate at an intermediate component, so the same rate of charge flow passes through every series element.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- There is only one Current path.
\- No branch permits Current division.
\- Current continuity is maintained throughout the path.

\*\*Remember\*\*

No Branch Point → No Current Division

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is the same Voltage present across all Parallel branches?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Every Parallel branch is connected between the same two electrical nodes. Because Voltage is the Potential Difference between two points, all branches sharing those points have the same Voltage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Parallel branches share both end nodes.
\- Voltage is measured between two points.
\- Branch Currents may differ even when Voltage is the same.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Same Two Nodes → Same Potential Difference

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Parallel equivalent Resistance lower than the smallest branch Resistance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Each additional Parallel branch provides another path for Current. At the same applied Voltage, total Current increases. Since R total = V/I total, the increased Current produces a lower equivalent Resistance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Parallel paths increase total conductance.
\- Total Current is greater than any single branch Current.
\- Equivalent Resistance therefore decreases.

\*\*Remember\*\*

More Current Paths → Lower Equivalent Resistance

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the Voltage Divider principle.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In a Series Circuit, the same Current flows through every resistor. Therefore, each Voltage drop is proportional to its Resistance. For R₁, V₁ = V total × R₁/R total.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Voltage division applies to a Series network.
\- Larger Resistance receives a larger Voltage drop.
\- Loading the divider can change its output Voltage.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series Voltage Drop ∝ Resistance

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the Current Divider principle.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In a Parallel Circuit, Current divides inversely according to branch Resistance. A lower-Resistance branch carries more Current. For two branches, I₁ = I total × R₂/(R₁ + R₂).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Parallel branches have common Voltage.
\- Current is inversely related to Resistance.
\- Branch Currents add to total Current.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Lower Branch R → Higher Branch I

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

How do you analyze a basic Series-Parallel Circuit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify the simplest Series or Parallel group, calculate its equivalent Resistance and replace it. Repeat until total Resistance is found, calculate source Current and then work backward to determine section Voltages and branch Currents.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Correctly identify nodes and branches.
\- Reduce the network step by step.
\- Work backward after finding source Current.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Identify → Reduce → Calculate → Work Backward → Verify

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between a Short Circuit and an Overload?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Short Circuit is a low-impedance fault path that bypasses all or part of the intended load. An Overload is excessive Current flowing through the normal load path because the connected demand exceeds the circuit rating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both conditions can cause excessive Current.
\- Short-Circuit Current is commonly much higher.
\- Protective devices must interrupt dangerous Current safely.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Short Circuit → Fault Path; Overload → Normal Path with Excessive Load

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must an Open Circuit not automatically be considered safe?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Although continuous Current is zero, a dangerous Potential Difference may still exist across open points. Contact, reconnection or closing the path can allow Current to flow, so isolation and absence of Voltage must be verified correctly.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Zero Current does not guarantee zero Voltage.
\- Open contacts may have full supply Voltage across them.
\- Safe isolation procedures remain necessary.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Open Does Not Automatically Mean De-energized

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why are household loads connected in parallel instead of series?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Parallel connection gives each appliance the rated supply Voltage and allows independent switching. If one appliance opens, the other complete branches can normally continue operating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Every branch receives supply Voltage.
\- Appliances draw Current according to their own load.
\- Independent operation improves usability and reliability.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Building Loads Need Rated Voltage and Independent Control

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

How can a Series-Parallel Circuit solution be verified?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Verify that Series Currents are equal, Parallel Voltages are equal, branch Currents add to the entering Current, Voltage drops balance around each closed path and total Power supplied equals total Power absorbed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Check Current relationships at branch points.
\- Check Voltage relationships around closed paths.
\- Check the total Power balance.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Current Balance + Voltage Balance + Power Balance
