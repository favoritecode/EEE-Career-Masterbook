\# Reference Notes

\- Positive accelerating torque is Tacc = Tm − TL.

\- DC starting Current is high because Back EMF is zero at standstill.

\- DOL applies full line Voltage and produces high starting Current.

\- Star-Delta starting provides approximately one-third DOL line Current and torque during Star connection.

\- Autotransformer and Soft-Starter torque fall approximately with applied Voltage squared.

\- Rotor-Resistance starting requires a wound Rotor and Slip Rings.

\- DC-Motor speed is approximately proportional to effective Armature Voltage divided by field Flux.

\- Induction-Motor speed is N = (120f/P)(1−s).

\- Synchronous-Motor speed is set by frequency and pole number.

\- A Soft Starter controls RMS Voltage during start/stop but does not provide normal continuous speed control.

\- A VFD controls Motor Voltage and frequency and contains a Rectifier, DC Link and Inverter.

\- Preventive maintenance follows schedule; predictive maintenance follows condition trends.

\- Voltage unbalance can cause much larger Current unbalance and Motor heating.

\- A Thermal Overload Relay does not replace short-circuit protection.

\- An RCCB detects residual Current and normally requires separate overcurrent protection.

\- VFD DC-Link capacitors may retain hazardous Voltage after input isolation.

\- Safe maintenance must control electrical, mechanical, pressure, gravity and automatic-start energy.

\- Chapter-21 begins the power-system section with Generation of Electrical Power.
