\# Basic MCQ

\### MCQ-001

\*\*Question\*\*

The accelerating torque of a Motor-load system is:

A. Tacc = Tm + TL
B. Tacc = Tm − TL
C. Tacc = TL − Tm under all conditions
D. Tacc = TmTL

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*B. Tacc = Tm − TL\*\*

\*\*Explanation\*\*

The Motor accelerates when developed Motor torque exceeds opposing load torque.

\*\*Why Not Others?\*\*

\- A incorrectly adds opposing torques.
\- C gives the decelerating sign for the stated convention.
\- D has incorrect units and meaning.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Positive Tacc is required for acceleration.

\### MCQ-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is DC-Motor starting Current very high without a starter?

A. Back EMF is zero at standstill and Armature Resistance is small
B. Field Flux is always zero
C. Supply Voltage is always zero
D. Armature Resistance is infinite

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*A. Back EMF is zero at standstill and Armature Resistance is small\*\*

\*\*Explanation\*\*

At start, Ia = V/Ra because Eb = 0. Small Ra can therefore permit destructive Current.

\*\*Why Not Others?\*\*

\- B is false for a correctly excited Motor.
\- C would produce no starting Current.
\- D would reduce Current toward zero.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Start: N = 0 → Eb = 0 → Ia can be very high.

\### MCQ-004

\*\*Question\*\*

During Star-Delta starting, starting torque is approximately:

A. Three times DOL torque
B. Equal to DOL torque
C. One-third of DOL torque
D. Zero for every Motor

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*C. One-third of DOL torque\*\*

\*\*Explanation\*\*

Star connection reduces winding Voltage by √3, and torque varies approximately with Voltage squared.

\*\*Why Not Others?\*\*

\- A gives the opposite effect.
\- B ignores reduced phase Voltage.
\- D is false because reduced non-zero torque is developed.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star-Delta: about one-third DOL line Current and torque at start.

\### MCQ-007

\*\*Question\*\*

DC-Motor speed is approximately proportional to:

A. (V − IaRa)/Φ
B. Φ/(V − IaRa)
C. VΦRa only
D. Ia² only

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*A. (V − IaRa)/Φ\*\*

\*\*Explanation\*\*

Effective Armature Voltage increases speed, while stronger field Flux reduces speed for comparable conditions.

\*\*Why Not Others?\*\*

\- B reverses the standard relationship.
\- C is not the DC-Motor speed equation.
\- D does not represent speed dependence.

\*\*Remember\*\*

DC speed ≈ effective Armature Voltage divided by Flux.
