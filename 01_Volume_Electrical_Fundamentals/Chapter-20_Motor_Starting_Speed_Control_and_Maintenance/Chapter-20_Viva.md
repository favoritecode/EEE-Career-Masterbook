\## Motor Starting, Speed Control and Maintenance

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Motor starting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Motor starting is the controlled process of energizing a Motor and accelerating its Rotor and connected load from standstill to the required operating speed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Begins at standstill
\- Controls electrical Current
\- Produces accelerating torque
\- Limits mechanical and thermal stress

\*\*Remember\*\*

A successful start reaches speed without exceeding electrical or mechanical limits.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is accelerating torque?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Accelerating torque is the difference between Motor torque and opposing load torque: Tacc = Tm − TL. It produces angular acceleration according to Tacc = Jdω/dt.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Tacc = Tm − TL
\- Must be positive to accelerate
\- Depends on load torque
\- Inertia determines acceleration rate

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rated Power alone does not prove that a Motor can accelerate a load.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does a DC Motor require a starter?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At standstill, speed and Back EMF are zero. Since Armature Resistance is very small, direct connection would produce Ia = V/Ra and dangerously high Current. A starter limits Current until speed and Back EMF rise.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- N = 0 at start
\- Eb = 0 at start
\- Small Armature Resistance
\- Starting Resistance or controlled drive limits Current

\*\*Remember\*\*

Zero Back EMF is the main DC-Motor starting problem.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare three-point and four-point DC-Motor starters.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Both provide starting Resistance and protective functions. In a three-point starter, the no-Voltage coil is associated with the Shunt-field circuit. In a four-point starter, its hold coil is connected independently, avoiding unwanted release during intentional field weakening.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both limit starting Current
\- Three-point hold coil relates to field path
\- Four-point hold coil is independent
\- Separate field-loss protection remains important

\*\*Remember\*\*

Four-point design is more compatible with field-weakening speed control.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain DOL starting.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Direct-On-Line starting connects the Motor directly to full line Voltage through a contactor and coordinated protection. It is simple and provides maximum available starting torque, but draws high starting Current and produces sudden mechanical torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Full line Voltage
\- High starting Current
\- Maximum available starting torque
\- Simple and economical

\*\*Remember\*\*

DOL is suitable only when the Motor, supply and load permit direct starting.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Star-Delta starting.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Motor designed to run Delta at line Voltage starts with its windings connected in Star and later changes to Delta. Starting line Current and torque are each approximately one-third of their DOL values.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Star during start
\- Delta during normal run
\- About one-third DOL Current
\- About one-third DOL torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star-Delta is unsuitable when the load needs high starting torque.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Autotransformer and Rotor-Resistance starting.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Autotransformer starter applies selectable reduced Stator Voltage, so torque falls approximately with Voltage squared. Rotor-Resistance starting inserts external Resistance into a Slip-Ring Motor Rotor to limit Current and improve starting torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Autotransformer gives selectable reduced Voltage
\- Reduced Voltage reduces torque
\- Rotor Resistance requires a wound Rotor
\- Rotor Resistance suits heavy starting duty

\*\*Remember\*\*

Autotransformer acts on Stator Voltage; Rotor Resistance acts through Slip Rings.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Single-Phase Motors started?

\*\*Answer\*\*

They may use a resistive auxiliary winding, start/run Capacitors, a shading coil or a Universal-Motor commutator arrangement. These methods create directional starting torque or inherently maintain torque direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Basic Single-Phase induction type is not self-starting
\- Auxiliary winding creates phase split
\- Capacitor improves phase displacement
\- Starting method depends on required torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacitor-Start gives stronger torque than Shaded-Pole starting.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain DC-Motor speed-control methods.

\*\*Answer\*\*

DC speed follows N ∝ (V − IaRa)/Φ. Armature-Voltage control is used mainly below base speed, field weakening above base speed and Armature Resistance for simple but inefficient lower-speed control. Electronic drives provide efficient closed-loop control.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Armature Voltage controls speed below base
\- Field weakening raises speed
\- Weak field reduces torque capability
\- Resistance control wastes Power

\*\*Remember\*\*

Voltage below base speed; weak field above base speed.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Induction-Motor speed control.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Induction-Motor speed is N = (120f/P)(1−s). It can be controlled through frequency, pole number or slip. VFD frequency control is the main modern method; Voltage and Rotor-Resistance methods have limited applications.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Frequency changes Synchronous Speed
\- Pole changing gives discrete speeds
\- Voltage control suits fan-type loads
\- Rotor Resistance requires a Slip-Ring Motor

\*\*Remember\*\*

VFD control is efficient and provides a wide continuous range.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

How is Synchronous-Motor speed controlled?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Because Ns = 120f/P, continuous speed control normally uses a compatible variable-frequency drive. Pole changing provides discrete speed, while changing field excitation controls reactive behavior rather than speed at fixed frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Frequency sets continuous synchronous speed
\- Pole number sets discrete speed
\- Excitation controls Flux and Power Factor
\- Control must preserve synchronism

\*\*Remember\*\*

At fixed f and P, a synchronized Motor has fixed speed.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Soft Starter and how does it work?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Soft Starter uses controlled thyristors to gradually increase the RMS Voltage applied to an AC Motor. This limits Current and mechanical shock, but reduced Voltage also reduces available starting torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Thyristor phase control
\- Voltage ramp
\- Reduced inrush and shock
\- No normal continuous speed control

\*\*Remember\*\*

Soft Starter controls the start/stop ramp, not running frequency.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a VFD and what are its main stages?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A VFD supplies controlled Voltage and frequency to an AC Motor. Its main Power stages are an input Rectifier, DC Link and output Inverter, coordinated by a controller.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Rectifier converts AC to DC
\- DC Link smooths and stores energy
\- Inverter produces controlled AC
\- Controller sets ramps, speed and limits

\*\*Remember\*\*

VFD = Rectifier → DC Link → Inverter.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare a Soft Starter and a VFD.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Soft Starter controls RMS Voltage mainly during starting and stopping, then often bypasses its electronics. A VFD continuously controls both Voltage and frequency, providing starting torque, speed control and process regulation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Soft Starter: fixed-speed duty
\- VFD: continuous variable speed
\- Soft Starter is simpler and often cheaper
\- VFD provides greater control but adds complexity

\*\*Remember\*\*

Need only smooth fixed-speed start → Soft Starter; need speed control → VFD.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What should be included in routine Motor maintenance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Routine work includes observing noise, smell, vibration and temperature; measuring Voltage and Current; inspecting terminals, cooling, earthing, coupling and bearings; lubricating correctly; reviewing trips; and recording trends.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electrical measurements
\- Mechanical inspection
\- Cooling and cleanliness
\- Lubrication and records

\*\*Remember\*\*

Small changes in trend can reveal a developing fault.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare preventive and predictive maintenance.

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Preventive follows schedule
\- Predictive follows condition
\- Predictive needs baseline and repeatable data
\- Criticality determines monitoring effort

\*\*Remember\*\*

Preventive asks “when scheduled?”; predictive asks “what does condition show?”

List common causes of Motor overheating.

Common causes include overload, Voltage unbalance, low or high Voltage, phase failure, frequent starting, blocked cooling, high ambient temperature, wrong connection, bearing friction and winding faults.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Supply and load conditions
\- Cooling and environment
\- Starting duty
\- Mechanical and winding faults

\*\*Remember\*\*

Overheating is a symptom; its root cause may be electrical or mechanical.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Fuse, MCB, MCCB and Thermal Overload Relay functions.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Fuse provides one-time overcurrent interruption according to its characteristic. An MCB provides thermal-magnetic protection at lower ratings. An MCCB handles higher ratings and may be adjustable. A Thermal Overload Relay protects against sustained Motor overload but does not replace short-circuit protection.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Fuse is replaced after operation
\- MCB suits lower-current circuits
\- MCCB supports higher/adjustable ratings
\- Overload relay models Motor heating

\*\*Remember\*\*

Short-circuit and overload functions must be coordinated.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare RCCB and ELCB terminology.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An RCCB is a current-operated residual protective device that detects imbalance between live-conductor Currents. Historically, Voltage-operated ELCBs sensed Voltage on an earth path and had limitations. Modern installations commonly use current-operated RCD/RCCB technology according to applicable rules.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- RCCB detects residual Current
\- RCCB normally lacks overload protection
\- Legacy Voltage-operated ELCB differs
\- Device selection follows applicable standards

\*\*Remember\*\*

Residual-current protection does not replace overcurrent protection.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety precautions are essential before Motor maintenance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify every energy source, stop and isolate the equipment, apply required lockout/tagout, release stored energy, verify absence of Voltage, secure the load and shaft, prevent automatic restart and restore guards before return to service.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Isolate main and control supplies
\- Discharge VFD and Capacitor energy
\- Control pressure, gravity and rotation
\- Verify and restore protection

\*\*Remember\*\*

Input OFF does not prove that the machine is electrically or mechanically safe.
