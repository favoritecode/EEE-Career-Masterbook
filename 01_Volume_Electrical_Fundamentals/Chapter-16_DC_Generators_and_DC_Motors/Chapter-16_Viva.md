\## DC Generators and DC Motors

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a DC Machine?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A DC Machine is a reversible electromechanical energy converter. As a Generator it converts mechanical energy into DC electrical energy; as a Motor it converts DC electrical energy into mechanical energy.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Rotating energy converter
\- Generator and Motor modes
\- Reversible construction
\- Electrical and mechanical losses occur

\*\*Remember\*\*

Generator: mechanical to electrical; Motor: electrical to mechanical

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the operating principle of a DC Generator?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A DC Generator operates on Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction. When Armature conductors rotate and cut Magnetic Flux, an EMF is induced. The Commutator provides unidirectional terminal output.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Faraday's Law
\- Relative motion and Flux cutting
\- Induced Armature EMF
\- Mechanical rectification

\*\*Remember\*\*

Motion through Flux produces EMF

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

State Fleming's Right-Hand Rule.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Hold the right-hand thumb, first finger and second finger mutually perpendicular. The first finger indicates Magnetic Field, the thumb indicates conductor motion and the second finger indicates induced Current or EMF direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Used for Generator action
\- First finger represents Field
\- Thumb represents motion
\- Second finger represents induced Current

\*\*Remember\*\*

Generator uses the Right Hand

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the function of the Commutator?

\*\*Answer\*\*

In a Generator, the Commutator mechanically rectifies the internally alternating Armature EMF to give unidirectional output. In a Motor, it reverses Armature-coil Current at the correct instant so torque remains in one direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Segmented copper cylinder
\- Generator mechanical Rectifier
\- Motor Current reversal
\- Works with Brushes

\*\*Remember\*\*

One component, two related switching functions

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

State and explain the DC Generator EMF Equation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The equation is Eg = PΦZN/(60A), where P is poles, Φ is Flux per pole in Weber, Z is total Armature conductors, N is speed in rpm and A is parallel paths. For a fixed machine, Eg is proportional to ΦN.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Eg unit is Volt
\- Flux and speed determine EMF
\- Z means conductors
\- A depends on winding type

\*\*Remember\*\*

Eg = PΦZN/(60A)

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the operating principle of a DC Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Current-carrying conductor placed in a Magnetic Field experiences force. Forces on Armature conductors form a torque that rotates the Motor Shaft. The torque relationship is T = kΦIa.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electromagnetic force
\- Armature Current and field Flux
\- Torque production
\- T is proportional to ΦIa

\*\*Remember\*\*

Current × Flux produces Motor torque

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Back EMF?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Back EMF is the EMF induced in a rotating Motor Armature that opposes the applied supply Voltage. It is Eb = kΦN and limits Armature Current during normal operation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Induced by Motor rotation
\- Opposes supply Voltage
\- Proportional to Flux and speed
\- Provides self-regulation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Back EMF is the Motor's internal counter Voltage

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is DC Motor starting Current high?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At standstill, speed and Back EMF are zero. Therefore Ia ≈ V/Ra. Since Armature Resistance is very low, Current can become dangerously high unless a Starter or electronic drive limits it.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- N = 0 at start
\- Eb = 0 at start
\- Armature Resistance is small
\- Starting protection is required

\*\*Remember\*\*

No Back EMF at start means high possible Current

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must a DC Series Motor not run without load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At light load, Armature Current and Series Field Flux become small. Because speed is approximately inversely related to Flux, the Motor can accelerate to a dangerously high speed and suffer mechanical damage.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Field depends on Armature Current
\- Light load gives low Flux
\- Low Flux causes high speed
\- Mechanical load must remain connected

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never operate a Series Motor unloaded

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the basic DC Motor speed equation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Since Eb = kΦN and Eb ≈ V−IaRa, Motor speed is approximately N ∝ (V−IaRa)/Φ. Speed can therefore be controlled through Armature Voltage, Armature Resistance or Field Flux.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Speed follows effective Armature Voltage
\- Speed is inversely related to Flux
\- Internal drop affects speed
\- Machine limits still apply

\*\*Remember\*\*

N ∝ (V−IaRa)/Φ

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare the main DC Motor speed-control methods.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Armature-Voltage control efficiently controls speed mainly below base speed. Field weakening increases speed above base speed but reduces torque capability. Added Armature Resistance lowers speed inefficiently and causes poor regulation and heating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Voltage control is efficient
\- Field weakening raises speed
\- Resistance control wastes Power
\- Electronic drives provide feedback and protection

\*\*Remember\*\*

Voltage below base; weakened field above base

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Shunt- and Series-Motor characteristics.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Shunt Motor has approximately constant Flux, moderate starting torque and good speed regulation. A Series Motor has very high starting torque but large speed variation and dangerous no-load speed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Shunt speed is nearly constant
\- Shunt torque is approximately proportional to Ia
\- Series torque is approximately proportional to Ia² before saturation
\- Series speed varies greatly with load

\*\*Remember\*\*

Shunt for speed; Series for starting torque

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

What causes heavy Brush sparking in a DC Machine?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Possible causes include incorrect Brush position or pressure, wrong Brush grade, rough or contaminated Commutator, overload, Armature faults, poor Interpole operation and vibration. The machine should be safely isolated and investigated.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Brush and Commutator condition
\- Load and winding faults
\- Neutral-plane or Interpole issue
\- Isolation before inspection

\*\*Remember\*\*

Sparking is a symptom, not a complete diagnosis

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety and maintenance steps are essential for a DC Machine?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify and isolate Armature, Field, control and mechanical energy sources; secure against re-energization; verify absence of Voltage; prevent unexpected rotation; and use rated PPE and instruments. Inspect Brushes, Commutator, bearings, alignment, insulation, cooling and protection, then record results.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Electrical and mechanical isolation
\- Starting and field-loss protection
\- Brush, Commutator and bearing inspection
\- Measurement records and trend analysis

\*\*Remember\*\*

Isolate, verify, inspect, test and record
