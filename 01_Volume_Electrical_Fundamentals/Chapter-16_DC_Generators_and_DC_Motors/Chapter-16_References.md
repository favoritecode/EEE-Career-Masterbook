\# Reference Notes

\- A DC Machine is reversible, but energy-flow direction and terminal conditions determine Generator or Motor operation.

\- Generator action follows Faraday's Law and Fleming's Right-Hand Rule; Motor action uses Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.

\- The Generator EMF Equation is Eg = PΦZN/(60A).

\- For simplex windings, A = P for Lap and A = 2 for Wave winding.

\- Practical Generator terminal Voltage includes Armature, Brush and magnetic effects.

\- Motor torque is T = kΦIa, and Back EMF is Eb = kΦN.

\- Neglecting Brush Drop, V = Eb + IaRa and Ia = (V−Eb)/Ra.

\- Back EMF is zero at standstill; starting Current therefore requires limitation.

\- A DC Series Motor must not normally operate without mechanical load.

\- Field weakening increases speed but reduces torque capability and can cause overspeed.

\- Armature-resistance speed control is inefficient because it produces I²R loss.

\- Heavy Brush sparking is a symptom requiring investigation, not a normal condition to ignore.

\- Insulation testing must follow manufacturer instructions and protect connected electronic drives.

\- Maintenance isolation must control Armature, Field, auxiliary, mechanical and stored-energy sources.

\- Chapter-17 extends rotating-machine study to Three-Phase Induction Motors.
