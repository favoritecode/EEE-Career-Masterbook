\## Generation of Electrical Power

\---

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Power Generation?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Power Generation is the controlled conversion of primary Energy into electrical Energy for local use or delivery through a Power System.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Begins with a primary Energy source
\- Uses one or more conversion stages
\- Produces usable electrical output
\- Includes control, protection and auxiliary systems

\*\*Remember\*\*

Generation is Energy conversion, not creation of Energy.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a prime mover in a Power Plant?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A prime mover is the machine that converts available fluid or fuel-derived Energy into mechanical shaft Power to drive a Generator.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Supplies mechanical input to the Generator
\- Examples include Steam, Water, Gas and Wind Turbines
\- A Diesel Engine can also be a prime mover
\- Its speed and torque influence Generation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Prime mover drives; Generator converts shaft Power to electrical Power.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate renewable and non-renewable Energy sources.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Renewable sources are replenished naturally on a useful human time scale, while non-renewable sources are finite and are depleted by use.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Solar, Wind, Hydro and tides are renewable
\- Coal, oil, gas and nuclear fuel are non-renewable
\- Renewable does not mean impact-free
\- Resource availability affects plant design

\*\*Remember\*\*

Classification depends on replenishment, not simply on whether the source is clean.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the working sequence of a Steam Thermal Power Plant.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Fuel Heat converts water into high-pressure steam, steam expands through a Turbine, the Turbine drives an Alternator, and exhaust steam is condensed and returned as feedwater.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Boiler produces steam
\- Steam Turbine produces shaft Power
\- Alternator produces electricity
\- Condenser closes the water-steam cycle

\*\*Remember\*\*

Chemical → Thermal → Mechanical → Electrical Energy.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

State the hydraulic Power equation for a Hydroelectric Plant.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The approximate output is P = ρgQHη, where ρ is water density, g is gravitational acceleration, Q is flow, H is net head and η is overall Efficiency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Power increases with flow
\- Power increases with net head
\- Losses are included through Efficiency
\- Units must be consistent

\*\*Remember\*\*

Hydro Power depends mainly on Q and H.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

How does a Nuclear Power Plant generate electricity?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Controlled nuclear fission releases Heat, the Heat produces steam, and the steam drives a Turbine-Generator to produce electricity.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Reactor is the Heat source
\- Control Rods regulate the fission process
\- Turbine-Generator stage resembles a steam plant
\- Containment and engineered safety are essential

\*\*Remember\*\*

Nuclear fission replaces fossil combustion as the Heat source.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Open-Cycle and Combined-Cycle Gas-Turbine plants.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Open-Cycle plant discharges hot Gas-Turbine exhaust, while a Combined-Cycle plant recovers that Heat to produce steam and additional Power through a Steam Turbine.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both contain a Gas Turbine
\- Combined Cycle adds Heat recovery
\- Combined Cycle gives higher fuel utilization
\- Open Cycle generally starts faster and is simpler

\*\*Remember\*\*

Combined Cycle = Gas-Turbine cycle + steam cycle.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the main Energy conversion in a Solar PV Plant.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Photovoltaic cells convert sunlight directly into DC electricity, and an inverter converts and controls it as AC suitable for the load or Grid.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- No rotating prime mover is required
\- Modules form strings and arrays
\- Inverter provides Grid-compatible AC
\- Output depends on irradiance, temperature and system losses

\*\*Remember\*\*

Sunlight → DC → controlled AC.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is available Wind Power proportional to the cube of wind speed?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The kinetic Energy transported by moving air depends on mass flow and velocity squared; mass flow also increases with velocity, giving Pwind = ½ρAv³.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- ρ is air density
\- A is swept Rotor area
\- v is wind speed
\- A Turbine captures only part of available Power

\*\*Remember\*\*

A small wind-speed change can cause a large available-Power change.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Base Load and Peak Load.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Base Load is the relatively continuous minimum system demand, while Peak Load is the higher demand occurring for shorter periods.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Base demand persists for long periods
\- Peak demand needs flexible additional resources
\- Plant duty depends on system economics and capability
\- Intermediate plants follow ordinary load changes

\*\*Remember\*\*

Base and peak describe system demand, not permanent technology labels.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Load Curve?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Load Curve is a chronological plot of Power demand against time for a selected period.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Preserves time sequence
\- Shows Peak and minimum demand
\- Area represents Energy
\- May be daily, monthly or annual

\*\*Remember\*\*

Load Curve tells both how much load and when it occurred.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Load Duration Curve?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Load Duration Curve is formed by arranging load values from highest to lowest and plotting them against cumulative duration.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Removes chronological order
\- Shows duration above a load level
\- Has the same Energy area as the original Load Curve
\- Helps capacity and utilization planning

\*\*Remember\*\*

Duration is preserved, but the time of occurrence is not.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Load Factor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Load Factor is the ratio of Average Load to Maximum Demand during the same period.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Load Factor = Average Load/Maximum Demand
\- It is dimensionless
\- It can be expressed as a percentage
\- Higher value indicates steadier capacity use

\*\*Remember\*\*

Use the same period for Energy, average and Maximum Demand.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Capacity Factor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Capacity Factor is actual Energy generated divided by rated capacity multiplied by total hours in the same period.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Energy-based utilization ratio
\- Uses total period hours
\- Influenced by resource, outages and dispatch
\- Must be interpreted according to plant duty

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacity Factor = actual MWh ÷ maximum rated MWh.

\*\*Question\*\*

Why should the definition of Plant Factor be stated before calculation?

Different textbooks and utilities may use Plant Factor or Plant Capacity Factor with different denominators, so stating the formula removes ambiguity.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Terminology is not fully uniform
\- Define rated capacity or plant Maximum Demand clearly
\- State total or actual operating hours
\- Keep numerator and denominator periods consistent

A correct formula definition is part of a correct answer.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is installed capacity different from actual generation?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Installed capacity is the combined nameplate rating, while actual generation depends on available units, fuel, maintenance, dispatch, weather, transmission limits and demand.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Installed capacity is expressed in MW
\- Actual Energy is expressed in MWh or GWh
\- Derating reduces available output
\- Plants may be available but not dispatched

\*\*Remember\*\*

Installed MW, available MW and generated MWh are not interchangeable.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is electrical isolation alone sometimes insufficient in a Power Plant?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Equipment may also contain pressure, Heat, flowing fluid, rotating inertia, gravity, chemical Energy or an independent automatic-start source that remains hazardous.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Identify every Energy source
\- Use approved Lockout/Tagout and Permit-to-Work systems
\- Verify absence of Voltage
\- Control stored mechanical and process Energy

\*\*Remember\*\*

Safe isolation is multi-Energy isolation.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Power Generation important to industry?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Reliable Generation supplies the Motors, heating, cooling, automation, data and safety systems required for continuous industrial production and essential services.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Reliability affects production and product quality
\- Power quality affects equipment performance
\- Energy cost affects competitiveness
\- Resilience supports recovery from failures

\*\*Remember\*\*

Industry needs reliable, quality and economical Power, not nameplate capacity alone.
