\## Generation of Electrical Power

\---

\## About This Chapter

This chapter was prepared using established Power-Plant Engineering and Power-System textbooks, renewable-energy references, international Generator and generating-plant standards, electrical-safety guidance, and official Bangladesh Power-sector sources. The resources support deeper study of generating technologies, plant operation, performance factors, environmental impact, safety and Bangladesh's generation mix.

Power-sector capacities and fuel shares change over time. Any numerical sector data should therefore be checked against the latest dated publication from the responsible authority.

\# Reference Notes

\- Power Generation converts Primary Energy into electrical Energy.

\- Most conventional plants use a prime mover and Synchronous Generator.

\- Solar PV converts sunlight directly into DC electricity before inverter conversion.

\- Net Generation equals Gross Generation minus Plant Auxiliary consumption.

\- Hydro Power is approximately P = ρgQHη.

\- Available Wind Power is Pwind = ½ρAv³.

\- Combined-Cycle plants recover Gas-Turbine exhaust Heat for additional steam-cycle output.

\- Base Load is the relatively continuous minimum demand; Peak Load occurs for shorter periods.

\- The area under a Load Curve or Load Duration Curve represents Energy.

\- Load Factor equals Average Load divided by Maximum Demand.

\- Capacity Factor compares actual generated Energy with rated maximum possible Energy.

\- Plant Factor terminology varies; define the formula before calculation.

\- Installed capacity, available capacity, actual Power and generated Energy are different quantities.

\- Bangladesh Power-sector statistics must include an official source and reference date.

\- BPDB, Power Division and SREDA are primary official sources for Bangladesh Generation and renewable-sector information.

\- Every generation technology has lifecycle environmental effects.

\- Safe Power-Plant work requires control of electrical, pressure, thermal, mechanical, chemical and automatic-start Energy.
