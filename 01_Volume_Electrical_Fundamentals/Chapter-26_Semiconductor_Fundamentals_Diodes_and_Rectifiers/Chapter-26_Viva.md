\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Semiconductor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a Conductor and an Insulator. Its conductivity can be controlled by Doping, temperature, light or an Electric Field. Silicon is the most widely used Semiconductor material.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Intermediate conductivity
\- Controllable electrical properties
\- Silicon is widely used
\- Basis of modern electronic devices

\*\*Remember\*\*

Controlled conductivity makes Semiconductors useful for switching, sensing and signal processing.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

How do Conductors, Insulators and Semiconductors differ?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Conductors have many free charge carriers and very low resistivity. Insulators have almost no free carriers and very high resistivity. Semiconductors have intermediate resistivity, and their carrier concentration can be controlled for useful electronic operation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Conductor: high conductivity
\- Insulator: very low conductivity
\- Semiconductor: controllable conductivity
\- Energy Bandgap explains the difference

\*\*Remember\*\*

The size of the Energy Bandgap strongly influences electrical conductivity.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Valence Band, Conduction Band and Forbidden Energy Gap?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Valence Band contains electrons normally bound to atoms. The Conduction Band contains electrons that can move through the material and carry Current. The Forbidden Energy Gap or Bandgap is the energy range between these bands in which no allowed electron state exists.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Valence Band contains bound electrons
\- Conduction Band supports Current
\- Bandgap separates the two bands
\- Smaller Bandgap allows easier excitation

\*\*Remember\*\*

An electron must gain sufficient energy to cross from the Valence Band to the Conduction Band.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Intrinsic Semiconductor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Intrinsic Semiconductor is a chemically pure Semiconductor without intentional impurity atoms. Thermal energy creates Electron-Hole pairs, so the electron concentration equals the Hole concentration: n = p = nᵢ.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Chemically pure material
\- No intentional Doping
\- Electrons and Holes are generated in pairs
\- Electron and Hole concentrations are equal

\*\*Remember\*\*

In an Intrinsic Semiconductor, n = p.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Extrinsic Semiconductor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Extrinsic Semiconductor is produced by adding a small controlled amount of impurity to a pure Semiconductor. This process, called Doping, increases the concentration of either electrons or Holes and creates N-Type or P-Type material.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Formed by Doping
\- Conductivity increases
\- Produces majority carriers
\- Classified as N-Type or P-Type

\*\*Remember\*\*

Doping changes carrier concentration without changing the basic crystal structure.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain N-Type and P-Type Semiconductors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

N-Type material is formed by adding pentavalent donor impurities, making electrons the majority carriers and Holes the minority carriers. P-Type material is formed using trivalent acceptor impurities, making Holes the majority carriers and electrons the minority carriers.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- N-Type uses donor impurity
\- P-Type uses acceptor impurity
\- Electrons dominate in N-Type
\- Holes dominate in P-Type

\*\*Remember\*\*

N-Type is not negatively charged and P-Type is not positively charged; both materials are electrically neutral overall.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

How is a PN Junction formed?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A PN Junction is formed when P-Type and N-Type regions exist in one continuous Semiconductor crystal. Electrons and Holes diffuse across the boundary and recombine. The uncovered ions create a Depletion Region and a built-in electric field that opposes further diffusion.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P-Type and N-Type regions meet
\- Carrier diffusion occurs
\- Recombination leaves fixed ions
\- Depletion Region and barrier potential form

\*\*Remember\*\*

The Depletion Region contains very few mobile charge carriers.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Forward Bias of a PN Junction Diode?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Forward Bias connects the P-side to the positive terminal and the N-side to the negative terminal of a supply. It reduces the barrier and narrows the Depletion Region, allowing substantial Current after the applied Voltage becomes sufficient.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P to positive
\- N to negative
\- Barrier decreases
\- Forward Current increases rapidly

\*\*Remember\*\*

Forward Bias makes a Diode conduct in its permitted direction.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Reverse Bias of a PN Junction Diode?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Reverse Bias connects the P-side to the negative terminal and the N-side to the positive terminal. It increases the barrier and widens the Depletion Region. Only a small reverse leakage Current flows until breakdown occurs.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P to negative
\- N to positive
\- Depletion Region widens
\- Very small leakage Current normally flows

\*\*Remember\*\*

Excessive reverse Voltage can cause breakdown and may damage an ordinary Diode.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Describe the V-I characteristic of a PN Junction Diode.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In Forward Bias, Current remains small at low Voltage and then rises rapidly near the knee region. In Reverse Bias, a small nearly constant leakage Current flows until the breakdown Voltage is reached, after which reverse Current rises sharply.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Nonlinear V-I relationship
\- Forward knee region
\- Small reverse leakage
\- Sharp Current rise at breakdown

\*\*Remember\*\*

A Diode conducts asymmetrically, which enables Rectification.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Zener Diode and where is it used?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Zener Diode is designed to operate safely in its specified reverse breakdown region. With a suitable series resistor, it maintains an approximately constant Voltage and is used for basic Voltage regulation, reference circuits and overvoltage clamping.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Operates in reverse breakdown
\- Specified Zener Voltage
\- Requires Current limiting
\- Used as Voltage reference or regulator

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never connect a Zener Diode to a Voltage source without proper Current limiting.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare LED, Photodiode, Schottky Diode and Varactor Diode.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An LED emits light under Forward Bias. A Photodiode detects light, commonly under Reverse Bias. A Schottky Diode offers low forward drop and fast switching. A Varactor Diode behaves as a Voltage-controlled junction capacitance under Reverse Bias.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- LED: light emission
\- Photodiode: light detection
\- Schottky: fast, low-drop operation
\- Varactor: Voltage-controlled capacitance

\*\*Remember\*\*

Select a Diode according to the required electrical or optical function.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Rectifier?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Rectifier is a circuit that converts Alternating Current or Voltage into unidirectional pulsating DC. Semiconductor Diodes perform this conversion by conducting mainly in one direction. Filters and regulators may then reduce ripple and stabilize the output.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Converts AC to pulsating DC
\- Uses unidirectional Diode action
\- Output initially contains ripple
\- Filter and regulator improve the output

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rectification alone does not normally produce perfectly smooth DC.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operation of a Half-Wave Rectifier.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Half-Wave Rectifier uses one Diode. It conducts during one half-cycle of the AC input and blocks the other half-cycle. For an ideal sinusoidal input with peak value Vₘ, the average output is Vdc = Vₘ/π, and the ripple frequency equals the supply frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses one Diode
\- Uses only one input half-cycle
\- High ripple
\- Low ideal maximum efficiency of about 40.6%

\*\*Remember\*\*

Half-Wave Rectification is simple but poorly utilizes the AC source.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operation of a Full-Wave Centre-Tapped Rectifier.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Full-Wave centre-tapped Rectifier uses two Diodes and a centre-tapped transformer secondary. Each Diode conducts on an alternate half-cycle, but Load Current keeps the same direction. Ideally, Vdc = 2Vₘ/π and ripple frequency is twice the supply frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses two Diodes
\- Requires centre-tapped secondary
\- Uses both AC half-cycles
\- Ripple frequency equals 2f

\*\*Remember\*\*

Each Diode in a centre-tapped Rectifier must withstand a relatively high Peak Inverse Voltage.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

How does a Bridge Rectifier work, and what are its main advantages?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Bridge Rectifier uses four Diodes. Two Diodes conduct in each half-cycle so that Load Current always flows in one direction. It provides Full-Wave Rectification without a centre-tapped transformer and gives better transformer utilization, although Current passes through two forward-biased Diodes.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Four-Diode bridge
\- Two Diodes conduct at a time
\- No centre tap required
\- Full-Wave output with ripple frequency 2f

\*\*Remember\*\*

Bridge output loses approximately two Diode forward drops in each conducting path.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Ripple Factor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Ripple Factor is the ratio of the RMS value of the AC component in a Rectifier output to its DC component. A smaller Ripple Factor indicates smoother DC. For ideal unfiltered outputs, it is approximately 1.21 for Half-Wave and 0.482 for Full-Wave Rectification.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Measures AC content in DC output
\- Lower value means smoother DC
\- Half-Wave ripple is larger
\- Filtering reduces ripple

\*\*Remember\*\*

Full-Wave Rectification is easier to filter because its ripple is lower and occurs at twice the supply frequency.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Rectifier Efficiency?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Rectifier Efficiency is the ratio of DC power delivered to the Load to AC input power supplied to the Rectifier, expressed as a percentage. The ideal maximum is approximately 40.6% for a Half-Wave Rectifier and 81.2% for a Full-Wave Rectifier.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- η = Pdc/Pac × 100%
\- Measures AC-to-DC power conversion
\- Full-Wave is more efficient
\- Practical losses reduce efficiency

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rectifier Efficiency is different from Ripple Factor.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

How do filter and Voltage-regulator stages improve a Rectifier output?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A filter reduces the AC ripple component in the pulsating Rectifier output. A shunt capacitor charges near waveform peaks and supplies the Load between peaks. A Voltage regulator then keeps the DC output more stable against input and Load changes, within its designed limits.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Filter smooths pulsating DC
\- Capacitor stores and releases energy
\- Larger capacitance generally reduces ripple
\- Regulator stabilizes output Voltage

\*\*Remember\*\*

The normal sequence is Rectifier, Filter, then Voltage Regulator.
