\# Glossary

\## Semiconductor Fundamentals, Diodes and Rectifiers

\---

\## Semiconductor

A material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a Conductor and an Insulator and can be controlled by Doping, temperature, light or an Electric Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Conductor

A material containing many mobile charge carriers and therefore having high electrical conductivity.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Insulator

A material having very few mobile charge carriers and very low electrical conductivity under normal conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Valence Electron

An electron in the outermost occupied shell of an atom that participates in bonding and electrical behavior.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Valence Band

The range of allowed electron energies normally occupied by electrons bound to atoms in a solid.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Electronvolt (eV)

\---

\## Conduction Band

The range of allowed energies in which electrons can move through a material and contribute to Electric Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Electronvolt (eV)

\---

\## Energy Bandgap

The forbidden energy range between the Valence Band and the Conduction Band.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: E_g

\*\*Unit\*\*: Electronvolt (eV)

\---

\## Electron-Hole Pair

A free electron and the corresponding Hole created when a Valence Electron gains enough energy to enter the Conduction Band.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: EHP

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Hole

The absence of an electron in a nearly filled Valence Band, treated as a mobile positive charge carrier.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: h⁺

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Intrinsic Semiconductor

A chemically pure Semiconductor without intentional impurity atoms, in which electron and Hole concentrations are equal.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Intrinsic Carrier Concentration

The equilibrium concentration of electrons or Holes in a pure Semiconductor at a specified temperature.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: n_i

\*\*Unit\*\*: Per cubic metre (m⁻³) or per cubic centimetre (cm⁻³)

\---

\## Extrinsic Semiconductor

A Semiconductor whose electrical properties are intentionally modified by adding controlled impurities.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Doping

The controlled addition of impurity atoms to a pure Semiconductor to change its carrier concentration and conductivity.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Dopant

An impurity atom intentionally introduced into a Semiconductor during Doping.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## N-Type Semiconductor

An Extrinsic Semiconductor in which electrons are the majority carriers, commonly produced using pentavalent donor impurities.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N-Type

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## P-Type Semiconductor

An Extrinsic Semiconductor in which Holes are the majority carriers, commonly produced using trivalent acceptor impurities.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P-Type

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Donor Impurity

A Dopant that contributes an additional electron to a Semiconductor and helps produce N-Type material.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N_D

\*\*Unit\*\*: Per cubic metre (m⁻³) or per cubic centimetre (cm⁻³)

\---

\## Acceptor Impurity

A Dopant that creates a Hole by accepting an electron and helps produce P-Type material.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N_A

\*\*Unit\*\*: Per cubic metre (m⁻³) or per cubic centimetre (cm⁻³)

\---

\## Majority Carrier

The more numerous charge-carrier type in a doped Semiconductor: electrons in N-Type or Holes in P-Type material.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Minority Carrier

The less numerous charge-carrier type in a doped Semiconductor: Holes in N-Type or electrons in P-Type material.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Carrier Mobility

The proportionality between charge-carrier drift velocity and applied Electric Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: μ_n for electrons; μ_p for Holes

\*\*Unit\*\*: Square metre per volt-second (m²/V·s)

\---

\## Conductivity

A measure of a material's ability to conduct Electric Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: σ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Siemens per metre (S/m)

\---

\## PN Junction

The boundary formed between P-Type and N-Type regions in a continuous Semiconductor crystal.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Depletion Region

The region near a PN Junction depleted of most mobile carriers and containing fixed ionized impurity atoms.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Metre (m) for width

\---

\## Barrier Potential

The built-in potential difference across the Depletion Region that opposes further majority-carrier diffusion.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_bi

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## PN Junction Diode

A two-terminal Semiconductor device that conducts readily in one direction and restricts Current in the opposite direction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: D

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Anode

The terminal connected to the P-Type region of a PN Junction Diode.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: A

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Cathode

The terminal connected to the N-Type region of a PN Junction Diode.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: K

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Forward Bias

A Diode connection in which the P-side is made positive relative to the N-side, reducing the junction barrier and permitting substantial Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_F

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Reverse Bias

A Diode connection in which the P-side is made negative relative to the N-side, increasing the junction barrier and restricting Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_R

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Forward Voltage Drop

The Voltage appearing across a conducting forward-biased Diode.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_F

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Reverse Leakage Current

The small Current that flows through a normally reverse-biased Diode before breakdown.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: I_R

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Breakdown Voltage

The reverse Voltage at which Diode Current increases sharply because of junction breakdown.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_BR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Knee Voltage

The approximate forward Voltage near which Diode Current begins to increase rapidly.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_K

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Zener Diode

A Diode designed to operate safely in a specified reverse-breakdown region for Voltage reference, regulation or clamping.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ZD

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Zener Voltage

The specified reverse Voltage maintained approximately across a conducting Zener Diode under defined test conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_Z

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Light-Emitting Diode

A Diode that emits light when suitable Forward Current passes through it.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: LED

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Photodiode

A light-sensitive Diode that produces or controls Current according to incident optical power, commonly operated under Reverse Bias.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PD

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Schottky Diode

A metal-semiconductor junction Diode characterized by fast switching and a relatively low forward Voltage drop.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Varactor Diode

A reverse-biased Diode designed to provide Voltage-controlled junction capacitance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: C_j or C_T

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F) for junction capacitance

\---

\## Rectifier

A circuit that converts Alternating Current or Voltage into unidirectional pulsating DC.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Half-Wave Rectifier

A Rectifier that uses only one half-cycle of an AC input waveform.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: HWR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Full-Wave Rectifier

A Rectifier that converts both half-cycles of an AC input into Load Current of one direction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: FWR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Bridge Rectifier

A four-Diode Full-Wave Rectifier in which two Diodes conduct during each input half-cycle.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Peak Inverse Voltage

The maximum reverse Voltage a Diode must withstand while it is not conducting in a Rectifier circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PIV

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Ripple

The unwanted AC variation remaining in the output of a Rectifier after AC-to-DC conversion.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V) or Ampere (A), as applicable

\---

\## Ripple Factor

The ratio of the RMS value of the AC component in a Rectifier output to its DC component.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: γ or r

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\---

\## Rectifier Efficiency

The ratio of DC power delivered to the Load to AC input power supplied to a Rectifier.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: η

\*\*Unit\*\*: Percent (%)

\---

\## Filter Circuit

A circuit used to reduce ripple and smooth the pulsating DC output of a Rectifier.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Capacitor Filter

A shunt capacitor arrangement that charges near Rectifier waveform peaks and supplies the Load between peaks to reduce output ripple.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: C

\*\*Unit\*\*: Farad (F)

\---

\## Voltage Regulation

The ability of a circuit or supply to maintain a nearly constant output Voltage when input Voltage or Load Current changes.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Percent (%) or Volt (V), according to definition

\---

\## Line Regulation

The change in regulated output Voltage caused by a change in input Voltage while the Load condition is controlled.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Millivolt per volt (mV/V), percent, or equivalent specified form

\---

\## Load Regulation

The change in regulated output Voltage as Load Current changes between specified conditions while the input is controlled.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Percent (%) or Volt (V)

\---
