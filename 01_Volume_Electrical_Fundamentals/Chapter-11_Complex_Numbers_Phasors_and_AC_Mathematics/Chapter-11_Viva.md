\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Complex Number?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Complex Number contains a Real Part and an Imaginary Part and is commonly written as z = a + jb. Here a and b are Real Numbers and j = √−1.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- a is the Real Part.
\- b is the Imaginary Part coefficient.
\- Complex Numbers represent magnitude and phase efficiently.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Complex Number → Real Part + Imaginary Part

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does Electrical Engineering use j as the Imaginary Unit?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Engineering uses j = √−1 because the symbol i is commonly used for instantaneous electric Current. Using j prevents confusion between Current and the Imaginary Unit.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Mathematics commonly uses i.
\- Electrical Engineering commonly uses j.
\- j² = −1.

\*\*Remember\*\*

i for Current; j for Imaginary Unit

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the Real and Imaginary Parts of a + jb?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Real Part is a and the Imaginary Part is b. The Imaginary Part means the Real coefficient of j, not the complete term jb.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Re(z) = a.
\- Im(z) = b.
\- The sign of b determines the Imaginary direction.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Im(a + jb) = b

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Complex Conjugate?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Complex Conjugate of a + jb is a − jb. The Real Part remains unchanged while the sign of the Imaginary Part is reversed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- z = a + jb.
\- z\* = a − jb.
\- zz\* = a² + b², which is Real.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Conjugate Reverses Only the Imaginary Sign

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Rectangular Form?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Rectangular Form expresses a Complex Number as z = a + jb, where a is the horizontal Real component and b is the vertical Imaginary component. It is also called Cartesian Form.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It shows components directly.
\- Signs identify the Complex Plane quadrant.
\- It is convenient for addition and subtraction.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rectangular → a + jb

\### Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Polar Form?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Polar Form expresses a Complex Number by magnitude and phase angle as z = r∠θ. The angle is measured from the positive Real Axis under the selected sign convention.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- r is non-negative magnitude.
\- θ is the phase angle.
\- Polar Form is convenient for multiplication and division.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Polar → Magnitude ∠ Angle

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

How do you convert Rectangular Form to Polar Form?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For z = a + jb, calculate r = √(a² + b²). Calculate the reference angle from inverse tangent, determine the correct quadrant from the signs of a and b, and write z = r∠θ.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Use both component signs.
\- Check calculator degree or radian mode.
\- atan2(b, a) handles quadrants when used correctly.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Magnitude First, Quadrant Check Second

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

How do you convert Polar Form to Rectangular Form?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For z = r∠θ, calculate a = r cos θ and b = r sin θ. Then write z = a + jb.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Real component = r cos θ.
\- Imaginary component = r sin θ.
\- Component signs follow the angle quadrant.

\*\*Remember\*\*

r∠θ = r cos θ + j r sin θ

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Complex Numbers added and subtracted?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Write the numbers in Rectangular Form. Add or subtract the Real Parts together and the Imaginary Parts together. For subtraction, apply the negative sign to both components of the second number.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Real combines with Real.
\- Imaginary combines with Imaginary.
\- Polar quantities are converted before general addition.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Addition and Subtraction → Rectangular Form

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Complex Numbers multiplied and divided in Polar Form?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For multiplication, multiply magnitudes and add angles. For division, divide magnitudes and subtract the denominator angle from the numerator angle.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Multiplication: r₁r₂∠(θ₁ + θ₂).
\- Division: (r₁/r₂)∠(θ₁ − θ₂).
\- This method is efficient for Phasor Ohm's Law.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Multiply → Add Angles; Divide → Subtract Angles

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Phasor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Phasor is a Complex Number representing the magnitude and phase angle of a sinusoidal quantity at a specified common frequency. The common time factor is omitted from the written Phasor.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It represents sinusoidal steady state.
\- It carries magnitude and phase.
\- Frequency must be known separately.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Phasor = Sinusoidal Magnitude and Phase at One Frequency

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why are RMS Phasors commonly used in Electrical Engineering?

\*\*Answer\*\*

RMS values express the effective heating or power-related magnitude of AC quantities and match common equipment ratings and meter readings. Using RMS Phasors makes power and Circuit calculations practical, provided the convention is consistent.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- For a pure sine wave, Xrms = Xm/√2.
\- RMS and Peak conventions must not be mixed.
\- Equipment AC ratings are commonly RMS.

\*\*Remember\*\*

State the Magnitude Convention and Use It Consistently

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

How is a cosine waveform represented as an RMS Phasor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For x(t) = Xm cos(ωt + φ), divide the Peak magnitude by √2 and retain the phase angle. The RMS Phasor is X = (Xm/√2)∠φ.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Cosine is the selected reference.
\- Peak is converted to RMS.
\- ω is not written inside the constant Phasor.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Xm cos(ωt + φ) → (Xm/√2)∠φ RMS

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

How is a sine waveform converted to a cosine-reference Phasor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Use sin α = cos(α − 90°). Subtract 90° from the sine phase angle, then apply the selected Peak or RMS magnitude convention.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Convert the reference before writing the angle.
\- Sine-to-cosine conversion subtracts 90°.
\- Keep all Phasors on one reference convention.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Sine to Cosine → Angle − 90°

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What do leading and lagging mean in a Phasor diagram?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A quantity leads another when its phase angle is more positive relative to the selected reference. It lags when its phase angle is more negative. The comparison is meaningful for quantities at the same frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Reference angle is commonly set to 0°.
\- Positive relative phase indicates lead.
\- Negative relative phase indicates lag.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Relative Angle Determines Lead or Lag

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Phasors added?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Confirm that the Phasors have the same frequency and magnitude convention. Convert them to Rectangular Form, add the Real components and Imaginary components separately, and convert the result back to Polar Form when magnitude and phase are required.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Same frequency is required.
\- Rectangular Form is used for addition.
\- Magnitudes are not simply added at different angles.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Polar → Rectangular → Add → Polar

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Complex Impedance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Complex Impedance combines Resistance and net Reactance as Z = R + jX. Under the standard convention, positive jX is Inductive and negative jX is Capacitive.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Real Part represents Resistance.
\- Imaginary Part represents net Reactance.
\- Impedance has unit Ohm.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Z = R + jX

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

State Phasor Ohm's Law and give a simple example.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Phasor Ohm's Law is V = IZ, so I = V/Z. If V = 100∠0° V and Z = 5∠30° Ω, then I = 20∠−30° A.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Divide magnitudes.
\- Subtract the Impedance angle from the Voltage angle.
\- Interpret the resulting Current phase.

\*\*Remember\*\*

I = V/Z → Magnitudes Divide, Angles Subtract
