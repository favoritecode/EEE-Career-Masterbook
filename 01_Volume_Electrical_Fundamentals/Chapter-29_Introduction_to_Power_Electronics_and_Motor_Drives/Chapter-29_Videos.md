\## Introduction to Power Electronics and Motor Drives

\---

\## About This Section

The following educational resources are recommended for strengthening this chapter. Select videos that use clear converter Power-flow diagrams, Power Semiconductor symbols, switching waveforms, PWM animation, Rectifier and Inverter waveforms, VFD internal block diagrams, Motor Torque-speed behavior, Energy measurements and controlled industrial demonstrations.

Power Electronic equipment may contain hazardous AC Voltage, high DC-link Voltage, stored Capacitor Energy, Arc-Flash risk and rotating machinery. Beginner practical work must use current-limited extra-low-Voltage educational hardware or isolated simulation. Work on industrial VFDs, Soft Starters and converters requires trained authorization, approved Lockout/Tagout, manufacturer discharge time, absence-of-Voltage verification, suitable PPE and site procedures.

\# Video Selection Checklist

Before using a video, confirm that it:

\- Uses correct Power-device symbols and terminal names.
\- Distinguishes SCR latching from MOSFET or IGBT Gate-controlled turn-OFF.
\- Shows Current paths for every switching state.
\- Identifies Voltage, Current, Frequency and thermal ratings.
\- Explains PWM, Duty Cycle and Dead Time correctly.
\- Distinguishes Rectifier, DC-DC Converter, Inverter and AC Controller functions.
\- Shows the VFD Rectifier, DC link and Inverter stages.
\- Distinguishes a Soft Starter from a VFD.
\- Treats pump and fan Energy savings as application dependent.
\- Warns that DC-link Capacitors can remain charged after isolation.
\- Uses Lockout/Tagout, discharge time and absence-of-Voltage verification in industrial demonstrations.
\- Uses current manufacturer manuals and applicable safety standards.
