\# Chapter 29

\# Glossary

\## Introduction to Power Electronics and Motor Drives

\---

\## Power Electronics

The engineering field concerned with converting and controlling Electrical Power using solid-state devices and control techniques.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PE

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Power Converter

An electronic system that changes Electrical Power from one form or level into another controlled form or level.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Power Semiconductor Device

A solid-state component designed to block, conduct or control significant Voltage and Current in a Power circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Power Diode

An uncontrolled two-terminal Power Semiconductor that conducts mainly from Anode to Cathode when forward biased.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: D

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Silicon Controlled Rectifier

A unidirectional, Gate-triggered, latching Thyristor used for controlled high-Power switching and Rectification.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: SCR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Anode

The main SCR or Diode terminal from which conventional forward Current enters under normal forward-conduction notation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: A

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Cathode

The main SCR or Diode terminal from which conventional forward Current leaves under normal forward-conduction notation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: K

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Gate

The control terminal used to command switching in an SCR, TRIAC, MOSFET or IGBT according to the device type.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: G

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Latching Current

The minimum main Current required to maintain SCR conduction immediately after the triggering signal is removed under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: I_L

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Holding Current

The minimum main Current below which a conducting SCR returns to its blocking state under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: I_H

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## TRIAC

A bidirectional Gate-triggered Power device commonly used for controlling AC Load Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: TRIAC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## DIAC

A bidirectional breakover device commonly used to trigger a TRIAC in a phase-control circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: DIAC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Breakover Voltage

The magnitude of Voltage at which a blocking breakover device changes into conduction under specified conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_BO

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Power MOSFET

A Voltage-controlled Power Transistor with an insulated Gate and fast switching capability.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: MOSFET or Q

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Drain-to-Source ON Resistance

The effective resistance of a conducting MOSFET channel under specified Gate Voltage, Current and temperature conditions.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: R_DS(on)

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\---

\## Insulated-Gate Bipolar Transistor

A Voltage-controlled Power device combining insulated-Gate control with a bipolar conduction structure.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IGBT or Q

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Gate Driver

A circuit that supplies the Voltage, Current, timing and sometimes isolation required to control a Power Semiconductor Gate.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: GD

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Switching Frequency

The number of repeated switching cycles completed in one second.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: f_s

\*\*Unit\*\*: Hertz (Hz)

\---

\## Switching Period

The time required for one complete switching cycle.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: T_s

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s)

\---

\## Duty Cycle

The ratio of switch ON time to total switching period.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: D = t_ON/T

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless or Percent (%)

\---

\## Pulse-Width Modulation

A control method that varies pulse width or switching pattern to regulate a converter output.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PWM

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Conduction Loss

Power dissipated in a Semiconductor while it carries Load Current in its conducting state.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P_cond

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Switching Loss

Power dissipated mainly during switching transitions when device Voltage and Current overlap.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P_sw

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Dead Time

A short intentional interval between switching commands that helps prevent simultaneous conduction of two devices in one bridge leg.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: t_d

\*\*Unit\*\*: Second (s), commonly microsecond or nanosecond

\---

\## Shoot-Through

A destructive or severe fault condition in which upper and lower switches of a bridge leg conduct simultaneously across the DC link.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Rectifier

A converter that changes AC Power into DC Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: REC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Uncontrolled Rectifier

An AC-DC Converter using Diodes without Gate-based output control.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Controlled Rectifier

An AC-DC Converter that controls average DC output through controlled Power devices, commonly SCRs.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Firing Angle

The delay angle measured from a selected AC reference at which a Thyristor receives its turn-ON trigger.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: α

\*\*Unit\*\*: Degree (°) or Radian (rad)

\---

\## Freewheeling Diode

A Diode that provides a safe continuing Current path for an Inductive Load when the main supply switch turns OFF.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: D_F

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## DC-DC Converter

A switching converter that changes one DC Voltage level into another controlled DC level.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Buck Converter

A DC-DC Converter whose basic purpose is to produce an output Voltage lower than its input Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Buck

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Boost Converter

A DC-DC Converter whose basic purpose is to produce an output Voltage higher than its input Voltage.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Boost

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Buck-Boost Converter

A DC-DC Converter family capable of providing an output magnitude lower or higher than the input, with polarity depending on topology.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Buck-Boost

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Inverter

A converter that changes DC Power into controlled AC Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: INV

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## H-Bridge

A four-switch bridge arrangement used to apply either polarity of a DC source across a Load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: H-Bridge

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Voltage-Source Inverter

An Inverter supplied from a DC link that behaves mainly as a Voltage source.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VSI

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## AC-DC Converter

A converter family that accepts AC input and delivers controlled or uncontrolled DC output.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: AC-DC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Active Front End

A fully controlled AC-DC conversion stage capable of regulating a DC link, shaping input Current and potentially supporting bidirectional Power Flow.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: AFE

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## DC Link

The DC intermediate circuit connecting converter stages and commonly containing Energy-storage Capacitors or Inductors.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: DC link

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## DC-Link Voltage

The Voltage across the DC intermediate circuit of a converter.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V_DC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Pre-Charge Circuit

A circuit that limits inrush Current while DC-link Capacitors initially charge.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## AC Voltage Controller

An AC-AC Converter that controls RMS output Voltage while normally retaining the input Frequency.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ACVC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Cycloconverter

A direct AC-AC Converter that synthesizes a lower variable output Frequency from an AC input without a conventional DC link.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: CYC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Matrix Converter

A direct controlled-switch AC-AC Converter that changes Voltage and Frequency without a large DC-link Energy-storage Capacitor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: MC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Motor Drive

A coordinated system that controls Motor starting, stopping, speed, Torque, direction, braking and protection.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Drive

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Variable Frequency Drive

An AC Motor Drive that controls Motor supply Frequency and Voltage to regulate speed and Torque.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VFD

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Synchronous Speed

The rotational speed of the magnetic field in an AC machine.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N_s = 120f/P

\*\*Unit\*\*: Revolution per Minute (rpm)

\---

\## Scalar Control

A VFD control method that regulates quantities mainly by magnitude, commonly maintaining an approximate Voltage-to-Frequency relationship.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: V/f

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Vector Control

An advanced AC Drive method that controls Motor flux and Torque components for improved dynamic performance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: FOC or Vector Control

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Soft Starter

A Semiconductor Motor controller that gradually controls terminal Voltage to reduce starting and stopping stress.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: SS

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Bypass Contactor

A Contactor that carries Motor Current after Soft Starter acceleration to reduce running loss in the Semiconductor path.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: K_BYP

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Servo Drive

A closed-loop Drive designed for precise position, speed and Torque control of a Servo Motor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Regenerative Braking

A braking mode in which mechanical Energy is converted into electrical Energy and returned to a DC link, storage system or supply when the system permits.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Regen

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Interlock

A control condition that prevents unsafe, conflicting or incorrectly sequenced operations.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Permissive

A required healthy condition that must be satisfied before equipment operation is allowed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PERM

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Trip

A protective command that stops or isolates equipment after a detected fault or unsafe condition.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: TRIP

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Affinity Laws

Approximate relationships for suitable centrifugal Loads in which flow varies with speed, pressure with speed squared and Power with speed cubed.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Q ∝ N; H ∝ N²; P ∝ N³

\*\*Unit\*\*: Relationship among applicable quantities

\---

\## Harmonic

A sinusoidal component whose Frequency is an integer multiple of a fundamental Frequency.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: h

\*\*Unit\*\*: Harmonic order is dimensionless; Frequency in Hertz (Hz)

\---

\## Electromagnetic Interference

Unwanted electromagnetic disturbance that degrades the operation of equipment or signals.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: EMI

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Electromagnetic Compatibility

The ability of equipment to function correctly in its electromagnetic environment without causing unacceptable disturbance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: EMC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Lockout/Tagout

A controlled isolation procedure that prevents Energy restoration and identifies the responsible isolation before work.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: LOTO

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Arc Flash

A hazardous release of thermal and pressure Energy caused by an electrical arc fault.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: —

\*\*Unit\*\*: Incident Energy commonly expressed in cal/cm² where applicable

\---

\## Personal Protective Equipment

Protective clothing and equipment selected to reduce worker exposure to identified hazards.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PPE

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---
