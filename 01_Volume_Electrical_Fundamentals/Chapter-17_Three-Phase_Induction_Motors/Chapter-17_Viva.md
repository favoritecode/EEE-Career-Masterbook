\## Three-Phase Induction Motors

\---

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Three-Phase Induction Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Three-Phase Induction Motor is an AC Motor whose balanced Stator supply creates a Rotating Magnetic Field. The field induces Rotor Current, and field–Current interaction produces torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Three-Phase Stator supply
\- Rotor Current by induction
\- Self-starting RMF
\- Asynchronous operation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rotor energy is transferred across the Air Gap by induction.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operating principle.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Balanced Three-Phase Stator Currents produce an RMF at Synchronous Speed. Relative motion induces Rotor EMF and Current. Their interaction with the Stator field develops torque in the RMF direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Stator produces RMF
\- Relative motion induces EMF
\- Closed Rotor carries Current
\- Interaction produces torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

RMF → Rotor EMF → Rotor Current → Torque.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Stator and Rotor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The Stator is stationary, receives the Three-Phase supply and produces the RMF. The Rotor rotates, receives electrical energy by induction and develops torque. Stator frequency is f; Rotor frequency is sf.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Stator receives supply
\- Rotor receives induced energy
\- Stator creates field
\- Rotor develops torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Stator: input and field; Rotor: induction and torque.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Squirrel-Cage and Slip-Ring Rotors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Cage Rotor has bars shorted by End Rings, no Brushes, low cost and low maintenance. A Slip-Ring Rotor has a Three-Phase winding connected to Slip Rings, allowing external Resistance for high-torque starting but requiring more maintenance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Cage is rugged
\- Slip Ring accepts external Resistance
\- Cage maintenance is lower
\- Slip Ring suits heavy starts

\*\*Remember\*\*

Cage for most duties; Slip Ring for controlled heavy starting.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Rotating Magnetic Field?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An RMF is the nearly constant-magnitude resultant field produced by balanced Three-Phase Currents in windings separated by 120 electrical degrees. It rotates at Ns=120f/P.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Three displaced windings
\- Three displaced Currents
\- Constant resultant field
\- Speed equals Ns

\*\*Remember\*\*

Balanced Three-Phase supply naturally creates rotation.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Synchronous Speed.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Synchronous Speed is the speed of the Stator Rotating Magnetic Field. It is Ns=120f/P rpm, where f is supply Frequency and P is pole number.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Field speed, not Rotor speed
\- Directly proportional to f
\- Inversely proportional to P
\- Unit rpm

\*\*Remember\*\*

Ns = 120f/P.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Slip?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Slip is the difference between Synchronous Speed and Rotor speed relative to Synchronous Speed: s=(Ns−N)/Ns. Percentage Slip is s×100%.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Necessary relative speed
\- Zero at Ns
\- One at standstill
\- Increases with load

\*\*Remember\*\*

No Slip means no induction torque.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Rotor Frequency?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Rotor Frequency is the Frequency of induced Rotor EMF and Current: fr=sf. At standstill s=1 and fr=f; near normal speed, fr is low.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Depends on Slip
\- Equals supply frequency at start
\- Falls during acceleration
\- Unit Hertz

\*\*Remember\*\*

fr = sf.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the torque–speed characteristic.

\*\*Answer\*\*

At standstill the Motor develops starting torque. Torque rises through the stable region to Breakdown Torque, then falls toward zero at Synchronous Speed. The rated point lies below Breakdown Torque at low Slip.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Starting torque
\- Stable region
\- Breakdown Torque
\- Zero torque at Ns

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rated operation requires margin below Breakdown Torque.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Breakdown Torque?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Breakdown Torque is the maximum torque an Induction Motor can develop at a given supply condition without an abrupt speed collapse. Load torque must remain safely below it.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Maximum torque
\- Also pull-out torque
\- Above rated torque
\- Exceeding it causes stall

\*\*Remember\*\*

Breakdown Torque is the stability limit.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Motor starting Current high?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At standstill Slip is one, and the Motor behaves similarly to a Transformer with a short-circuited Secondary. Large Rotor and Stator Currents can flow until speed and effective impedance increase.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Slip equals one
\- Rotor frequency equals f
\- High inrush
\- Starter may be required

\*\*Remember\*\*

Starting method must suit supply and load.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare DOL and Star-Delta starting.

\*\*Answer\*\*

DOL applies full Voltage, giving high Current and high starting torque. Star-Delta starts suitable Delta-run windings in Star, reducing phase Voltage, Line Current and torque; it then changes to Delta.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- DOL is simple
\- DOL has high inrush
\- Star reduces Voltage
\- Star-Delta needs six terminals

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star-Delta reduces both Current and torque.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate a Soft Starter and VFD.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Soft Starter controls Motor Voltage mainly during starting and stopping, but normally runs at fixed supply Frequency. A VFD controls both Frequency and Voltage, providing starting control and continuous speed adjustment.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Soft Starter controls Voltage
\- VFD controls frequency
\- VFD provides speed control
\- Both can reduce mechanical shock

\*\*Remember\*\*

Soft start is not the same as variable speed.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain V/f speed control.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A VFD changes Frequency to change Ns. Below base speed it changes Voltage approximately in proportion to Frequency to maintain Air-Gap Flux and torque capability within ratings.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Frequency sets Ns
\- Voltage supports Flux
\- Constant V/f below base
\- Field weakening above base

\*\*Remember\*\*

V/f control prevents saturation and weak torque.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the main Motor losses?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Losses include Stator Copper Loss, Core Loss, Rotor Copper Loss, friction, windage and stray-load loss. Rotor Copper Loss equals sPg, and developed mechanical Power equals (1−s)Pg.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Stator I²R loss
\- Iron loss
\- Rotor sPg loss
\- Mechanical and stray loss

\*\*Remember\*\*

Every loss becomes heat or unavailable output.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Motor efficiency.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Efficiency is useful Shaft output divided by electrical input: η=Pout/Pin×100%. Balanced input is √3VLILcosφ, and Shaft output can be 2πNT/60.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Output/input ratio
\- Input includes Power Factor
\- Output uses torque and speed
\- Always below 100%

\*\*Remember\*\*

η = Pout/Pin × 100%.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is single phasing?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Single phasing is loss of one supply Phase while a Three-Phase Motor is energized or attempting to run. It causes severe Current unbalance, reduced torque and rapid overheating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Phase-loss fault
\- Unequal Currents
\- Reduced torque
\- Fast thermal damage

\*\*Remember\*\*

Use phase-loss and overload protection.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

What maintenance is essential?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Maintain clean cooling paths, correct bearing lubrication, alignment, tight terminals, sound insulation, balanced Voltage and Current, effective protection and condition records. Trend temperature and vibration under comparable conditions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Cooling and cleanliness
\- Bearings and alignment
\- Electrical tests
\- Condition trending

\*\*Remember\*\*

Correct the root cause, not only the failed part.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety precautions are required?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify supply, VFD DC link, control, heater and mechanical sources; isolate and Lockout/Tagout; wait the specified discharge time; verify absence of Voltage; prevent automatic restart and secure driven equipment before work.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- All energy sources
\- Stored DC-link charge
\- Unexpected rotation
\- Rated PPE and instruments

\*\*Remember\*\*

Isolate, discharge, verify and secure.
