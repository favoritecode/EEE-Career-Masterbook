\# Reference Notes

\- Balanced Three-Phase Stator Currents create an RMF rotating at Ns = 120f/P.

\- Rotor Current is induced by relative motion; normal Motoring torque requires positive Slip.

\- Slip is s = (Ns−N)/Ns and Rotor Frequency is fr = sf.

\- A Cage Rotor uses bars and End Rings; a Slip-Ring Rotor provides external winding access.

\- External Rotor Resistance improves wound-Rotor starting torque but increases loss.

\- Interchanging any two Stator Lines reverses Phase Sequence and Motor direction.

\- Breakdown Torque is the maximum torque before unstable speed collapse.

\- Motor torque capability is strongly related to the square of applied Voltage under comparable conditions.

\- DOL applies full Voltage; Star-Delta reduces both starting Current and torque.

\- A Soft Starter controls starting Voltage, while a VFD controls Voltage and Frequency.

\- Constant V/f operation supports approximately constant Air-Gap Flux below base speed.

\- Rotor Copper Loss equals sPg and mechanical developed Power equals (1−s)Pg.

\- Single phasing causes severe Current unbalance, torque reduction and overheating.

\- VFD DC-link capacitors may retain hazardous Voltage after supply isolation.

\- Insulation testing must follow Motor and VFD manufacturer requirements.

\- Chapter-18 extends Motor study to Single-Phase Motors and their starting arrangements.
