\## Electrical Estimation, Load Calculation and Energy Management

\---

\## About This Chapter

This chapter was prepared using established Electrical Estimation, installation-design, Distribution, Energy-management and Energy-efficiency textbooks together with IEC, IEEE and ISO standards. The references support deeper study of load inventories, Maximum Demand, demand and diversity, Cable and protective-device selection, Energy measurement, billing concepts, audits, baselines, Energy Performance Indicators and lifecycle decisions.

Demand allowances, tariff rates and installation requirements vary by jurisdiction and customer category. Practical calculations must use the current locally adopted code, serving-utility tariff, manufacturer data, measured operating records and approved project assumptions.

\# Reference Notes

\- Connected Load is an installed-load inventory, not necessarily Maximum Demand.

\- Demand Factor is normally not greater than 1; Diversity Factor is normally at least 1.

\- Load Factor uses Average Load and Maximum Demand over the same period.

\- Demand allowances require approved rules or justified operating data.

\- Preliminary load Current does not complete Cable or protection design.

\- Cable selection includes ampacity, derating, Voltage drop and fault withstand.

\- Breaking capacity must suit prospective fault Current.

\- kWh equals kW multiplied by operating hours for constant Power.

\- Actual tariff rates and customer categories must be verified from current official publications.

\- ISO 50001 supports continual improvement of Energy performance.

\- ISO 50002-1:2025 provides current general Energy-audit requirements.

\- EnPIs and baselines should account for relevant variables.

\- Energy conservation must not compromise safety or required service.

\- Efficient equipment should be evaluated by actual duty and lifecycle cost.

\- Chapter-26 continues with Semiconductor Fundamentals, Diodes and Rectifiers.
