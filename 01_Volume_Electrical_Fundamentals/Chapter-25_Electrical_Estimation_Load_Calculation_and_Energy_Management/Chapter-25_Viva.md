\*\*Question\*\*

What is Electrical Estimation and what are its main outputs?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Electrical Estimation determines loads, equipment ratings, material quantities, labour and cost from a defined scope. Main outputs include load schedules, single-line diagrams, Cable and protection schedules, BOQ, cost and assumptions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Load and equipment schedule
\- Material take-off
\- Labour and cost
\- Scope and assumptions

\*\*Remember\*\*

Design proves suitability; estimation quantifies the project.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Connected Load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Connected Load is the sum of electrical input ratings of all connected equipment within a defined boundary. Quantity is multiplied by unit rating and consistent kW or kVA units are used.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Sum of installed input ratings
\- Define system boundary
\- Use consistent units
\- Not normally simultaneous demand

\*\*Remember\*\*

Connected Load is an inventory, not necessarily the peak.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Maximum Demand?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Maximum Demand is the highest average load measured or expected over a specified demand interval during a stated period. It can be estimated from load-group demand allowances or measured data.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Has a demand interval
\- Below Connected Load in most cases
\- Used for capacity planning
\- Needs justified data

\*\*Remember\*\*

State the interval and period whenever quoting Maximum Demand.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Demand Factor with an example.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Demand Factor equals Maximum Demand divided by Connected Load. If Connected Load is 200 kW and Maximum Demand is 130 kW, Demand Factor is 0.65.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- DF = MD/Connected Load
\- Same boundary and units
\- Normally ≤ 1
\- Application-specific

\*\*Remember\*\*

Demand Factor converts installed rating into expected peak.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Diversity Factor with an example.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Diversity Factor equals the sum of individual noncoincident Maximum Demands divided by the coincident group Maximum Demand. Peaks of 30, 40 and 50 kW with a 90 kW group peak give 1.33.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Sum individual peaks
\- Divide by group peak
\- Normally ≥ 1
\- Measures noncoincidence

\*\*Remember\*\*

Demand Factor and Diversity Factor are not reciprocals by definition.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Load Factor with an example.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Load Factor equals Average Load divided by Maximum Demand over the same period, or E/(Maximum Demand×time). For 36,000 kWh in 720 h with 100 kW MD, it is 50%.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses average and maximum
\- Same period required
\- Between 0 and 1
\- Shows utilization

\*\*Remember\*\*

A flatter load profile gives a higher Load Factor.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Power Factor important in Energy management?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For the same kW and Voltage, low PF increases Current, kVA demand, I²R loss and Voltage drop. Correctly engineered compensation can release capacity, but harmonics, resonance and overcompensation must be checked.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- PF = kW/kVA
\- Low PF raises Current
\- Correction reduces upstream kvar
\- Needs harmonic study

\*\*Remember\*\*

PF correction reduces Current; it does not remove useful kWh.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

How is a basic residential load calculation prepared?

\*\*Answer\*\*

List lighting, fans, Sockets and fixed appliances using input ratings, apply locally approved demand allowances by load group, sum estimated demand, convert it to Current and check circuit division, starting and phase balance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Inventory every load
\- Demand by group
\- Convert MD to Current
\- Balance phases

\*\*Remember\*\*

Instructional percentages are not official code values.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

How does commercial load calculation differ from residential calculation?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Commercial calculation separates lighting, IT, HVAC, lifts, pumps, kitchen, tenant and critical loads. It must consider occupancy schedules, HVAC coincidence, harmonics, demand and backup classification.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Many load groups
\- HVAC often dominates
\- Electronic harmonics
\- Critical loads treated specially

\*\*Remember\*\*

Commercial demand requires operational schedules, not one blanket factor.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

How is electrical Energy consumption calculated?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For constant Power, Energy equals Power multiplied by time. Total consumption is the sum of each load's input kW multiplied by operating hours. Metered data is preferred for cycling loads.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- E = P×t
\- kW×h = kWh
\- Sum all loads
\- Use actual duty cycle

\*\*Remember\*\*

Convert watts to kilowatts before calculating kWh.

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Energy Management?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Energy Management is the organized process of establishing policy, measuring use, setting a baseline and EnPIs, identifying significant Energy uses, implementing action, verifying savings and continually improving.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Measure and analyze
\- Baseline and EnPI
\- Implement action
\- Verify and improve

\*\*Remember\*\*

Unadjusted bill reduction is not always verified Energy saving.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

How should a practical estimate be checked before industrial use?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The worksheet should record item, quantity, input rating, Connected Load, demand basis, phase, PF, hours and circuit. Reviewers must then check units, input versus output Power, diversity, starting, harmonics, Cable and protection safety, critical loads, system capacity and documented assumptions before industrial use.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Complete and traceable worksheet
\- Unit and diversity error checks
\- Cable, fault and protection safety
\- Industrial capacity and lifecycle review

\*\*Remember\*\*

A practical estimate must be reproducible, safe and suitable for the complete system.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What are common mistakes in load calculation?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Common errors include using output instead of input kW, adding kW and kVA, applying diversity twice, mixing line and phase Voltage, ignoring efficiency, starting and harmonics, and using outdated tariff data.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Input/output confusion
\- Unit errors
\- Double diversity
\- Ignored operating effects

\*\*Remember\*\*

Audit units, boundaries, formulas and assumptions.
