\# Chapter 14

\# Glossary

\## Three-Phase AC Systems

\---

\## Three-Phase System

An AC system containing three same-Frequency Phase quantities normally separated by 120 electrical degrees.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: 3φ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Polyphase System

An AC system containing two or more related Phase Voltages or Currents with defined angular displacement.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Phase

One sinusoidal electrical quantity or one branch of a Polyphase source or load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: φ or Phase A/B/C

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Phase Angle

The angular position of a sinusoidal quantity relative to a selected reference.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: θ or φ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Degree (°) or Radian (rad)

\---

\## Phase Displacement

The angular difference between two sinusoidal quantities of the same Frequency.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Δφ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Degree (°) or Radian (rad)

\---

\## Balanced Three-Phase Source

A source that produces three equal-magnitude, same-Frequency Voltages separated by exactly 120°.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Balanced Load

A Three-Phase load having equal Phase Impedances with identical Phase angles.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ZA = ZB = ZC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω) for each Phase Impedance

\---

\## Unbalanced Load

A Three-Phase load whose Phase Impedances or operating Currents are unequal.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Phase Sequence

The order in which the three Phase quantities reach the same reference condition or positive maximum.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ABC, ACB, RYB or RBY

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Positive Phase Sequence

The standard Phase order ABC, commonly represented by Phase angles 0°, −120° and +120°.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ABC or RYB

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Negative Phase Sequence

The reverse Phase order ACB, producing a rotating field opposite to the positive-sequence direction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ACB or RBY

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Phase Sequence Indicator

An instrument used to determine the order or rotation of a live Three-Phase supply.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PSI or Phase Rotation Meter

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Alternator

A rotating electrical machine that converts mechanical energy into AC electrical energy by Electromagnetic Induction.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: G or AC Generator

\*\*Unit\*\*: Rated in volt-ampere (VA) or Watt (W)

\---

\## Stator

The stationary part of a rotating electrical machine that commonly carries the Three-Phase armature windings in an alternator.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Rotor

The rotating part of an electrical machine that produces or interacts with a rotating Magnetic Field.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Electrical Angle

The angular position measured in terms of an electrical cycle; it depends on mechanical angle and the number of pole pairs.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: θe

\*\*Unit\*\*: Electrical degree (°e) or Radian

\---

\## Mechanical Angle

The physical angular displacement of a Rotor or rotating component.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: θm

\*\*Unit\*\*: Mechanical degree (°m) or Radian

\---

\## Number of Poles

The total number of North and South magnetic poles in a rotating electrical machine.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P

\*\*Unit\*\*: Count; dimensionless

\---

\## Rotational Speed

The number of complete mechanical revolutions made by a Rotor per unit time.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N or n

\*\*Unit\*\*: Revolution per minute (rpm) or radian per second (rad/s)

\---

\## Frequency

The number of complete electrical cycles per second.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: f

\*\*Unit\*\*: Hertz (Hz)

\---

\## Star Connection

A Three-Phase connection in which one end of each Phase is joined at a common Neutral Point.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Y

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Delta Connection

A Three-Phase connection in which the three Phase elements form a closed triangular loop.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Δ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Neutral Point

The common junction of the three Phase windings or load elements in a Star connection.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Neutral Conductor

A conductor connected to the Neutral Point that provides a return path for unbalanced and certain harmonic Currents.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: N

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Three-Wire System

A Three-Phase system using three Line conductors without a distributed Neutral conductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: 3φ, 3W

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Four-Wire System

A Three-Phase system using three Line conductors and one Neutral conductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: 3φ, 4W

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Line Conductor

One of the energized conductors connecting a Three-Phase source to a load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L1, L2 and L3

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Line Voltage

The Voltage measured between any two Line conductors in a Three-Phase system.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VL, VLL, VAB, VBC or VCA

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Phase Voltage

The Voltage measured across one Phase winding or one Phase element of a load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Vph

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Line-to-Neutral Voltage

The Voltage measured from a Line conductor to the Neutral Point in a Star-connected system.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VLN, VAN, VBN or VCN

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Line-to-Line Voltage

Another name for the Voltage measured between two Line conductors.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VLL

\*\*Unit\*\*: Volt (V)

\---

\## Line Current

The Current flowing through a Line conductor between a source and a load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IL, IA, IB or IC

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Phase Current

The Current flowing through one Phase winding or Phase Impedance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Iph

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Neutral Current

The resultant Current flowing in the Neutral conductor because of Phase unbalance or harmonic components.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IN

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Phase Impedance

The complex opposition presented by one Phase of a Three-Phase load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Zph

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ohm (Ω)

\---

\## Root Three

The numerical factor arising from the Phasor difference of two equal quantities separated by 120°.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: √3

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\---

\## Real Power

The average Power converted into useful work, heat, light or mechanical output.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Reactive Power

The Power exchanged between the source and reactive elements without net conversion into useful work over a complete cycle.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Q

\*\*Unit\*\*: volt-ampere reactive (var)

\---

\## Apparent Power

The product-based AC Power rating representing the combined effect of real and reactive Power.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: S

\*\*Unit\*\*: volt-ampere (VA)

\---

\## Power Factor

The ratio of real Power to apparent Power; for balanced sinusoidal conditions it equals cos φ.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PF or cos φ

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\---

\## Lagging Power Factor

A Power-Factor condition in which load Current lags the corresponding Voltage, commonly caused by Inductive loads.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PF lag or cos φ lag

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\---

\## Leading Power Factor

A Power-Factor condition in which load Current leads the corresponding Voltage, commonly caused by Capacitive behavior.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PF lead or cos φ lead

\*\*Unit\*\*: Dimensionless

\---

\## Constant Three-Phase Power

The nearly non-pulsating sum of the instantaneous Powers in an ideal balanced Three-Phase load.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ptotal

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Rotating Magnetic Field

A Magnetic Field whose resultant axis rotates in space because of Polyphase Currents in spatially displaced windings.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: B or Φ, depending on the quantity described

\*\*Unit\*\*: Tesla (T) for Flux Density or Weber (Wb) for Flux

\---

\## Synchronous Speed

The rotational speed of the magnetic field in an AC machine.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Ns

\*\*Unit\*\*: Revolution per minute (rpm)

\---

\## Phase Reversal

The change from one Phase Sequence to the opposite sequence by interchanging any two Line conductors.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ABC ↔ ACB

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Phase Loss

A fault condition in which one Phase supply or one Phase path becomes open or unavailable.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Single Phasing

The abnormal operation of a Three-Phase motor or load after one Phase is lost while the remaining Phases continue to supply it.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Voltage Unbalance

A condition in which the three Phase-Voltage magnitudes or angular displacements are unequal.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: VU or %VU

\*\*Unit\*\*: Percent (%)

\---

\## Current Unbalance

A condition in which the three Line or Phase Currents are unequal beyond an acceptable limit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: IU or %IU

\*\*Unit\*\*: Percent (%)

\---

\## Load Balancing

The process of distributing loads among the three Phases to reduce Current and Voltage unbalance.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---

\## Triplen Harmonics

Odd multiples of the third harmonic, such as the 3rd, 9th and 15th, whose zero-sequence components can add in a Four-Wire Neutral conductor.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: h = 3, 9, 15, ...

\*\*Unit\*\*: Harmonic order is dimensionless; Frequency is Hertz (Hz)

\---

\## Star-Delta Starter

A motor starter that initially connects suitable windings in Star and later reconnects them in Delta to reduce starting Current.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Y-Δ Starter

\*\*Unit\*\*: Rated in Volt (V), Ampere (A) and kilowatt (kW)

\---

\## Two-Wattmeter Method

A method using two Wattmeters to measure total real Power in a Three-Phase, Three-Wire system.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P = W1 + W2

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Three-Wattmeter Method

A method that measures each Phase Power separately and adds the three readings, suitable for systems where the required connections are available.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: P = W1 + W2 + W3

\*\*Unit\*\*: Watt (W)

\---

\## Clamp Meter

An instrument that measures conductor Current from its Magnetic Field without normally opening the Circuit.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: Not fixed

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A)

\---

\## Phase-Failure Relay

A protective relay that detects loss or abnormal condition of one or more Phases and initiates an alarm or trip.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PFR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable; device has Voltage and Frequency ratings

\---

\## Phase-Sequence Relay

A relay that detects incorrect Phase order and prevents unsuitable equipment operation.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: PSR

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable; device has Voltage and Frequency ratings

\---

\## Overcurrent Protection

Protection that operates when Current exceeds a defined value for a specified condition or time.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: ANSI 50 or 51 in common protection notation

\*\*Unit\*\*: Ampere (A) for pickup setting

\---

\## Earth Fault

An unintended conductive connection between an energized conductor and Earth or exposed conductive parts connected to Earth.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: EF

\*\*Unit\*\*: Fault Current in Ampere (A)

\---

\## Line-to-Line Fault

An unintended low-Impedance connection between two Line conductors.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: L-L Fault

\*\*Unit\*\*: Fault Current in Ampere (A)

\---

\## Phase Rotation

The directional order of a Three-Phase supply as interpreted by a sequence instrument or rotating machine.

\*\*Symbol\*\*: CW or CCW indication, depending on instrument

\*\*Unit\*\*: Not applicable

\---
