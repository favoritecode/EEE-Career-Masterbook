\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Three-Phase AC System?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Three-Phase AC System consists of three sinusoidal Voltages or Currents having the same Frequency and normally separated from one another by 120 electrical degrees. In a balanced system, the three Phase quantities also have equal magnitudes.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Three coordinated AC Phases
\- Same Frequency
\- 120° Phase displacement
\- Equal magnitudes under balanced conditions

\*\*Remember\*\*

Balanced Three-Phase = Equal magnitude + Same Frequency + 120°

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Why are the three Phases separated by 120°?

\*\*Answer\*\*

One electrical cycle contains 360°. Dividing the cycle equally among three Phases gives 360°/3 = 120°. In a generator, the three Stator windings are placed 120 electrical degrees apart, producing this time displacement in their induced EMFs.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- One cycle is 360°
\- Three equal intervals give 120°
\- Winding position produces waveform displacement
\- Balanced Phasors are equally spaced

\*\*Remember\*\*

360° ÷ 3 = 120°

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

How is Three-Phase Voltage generated?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Three-Phase Voltage is generated by placing three identical Stator windings 120 electrical degrees apart and rotating a Magnetic Field past them. According to Faraday's Law, changing Magnetic Flux induces three sinusoidal EMFs with the same Frequency and 120° displacement.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Three Stator windings
\- Rotating Rotor field
\- Electromagnetic Induction
\- Equal and displaced EMFs

\*\*Remember\*\*

Rotating Flux + Three spaced windings → Three-Phase EMF

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the relationship among alternator Frequency, poles and speed?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The generated Frequency is f = PN/120, where f is in Hertz, P is the number of magnetic poles and N is Rotor speed in revolutions per minute. Increasing speed or pole number increases Frequency when the other quantity remains constant.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Formula: f = PN/120
\- P represents poles
\- N represents rpm
\- Frequency unit is Hertz

\*\*Remember\*\*

Alternator Frequency → f = PN/120

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Phase Sequence?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Phase Sequence is the order in which the three Phase Voltages or Currents reach their positive maximum values or pass through the same reference point. Common sequences are ABC and ACB, also written as RYB and RBY.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It describes Phase order
\- ABC and ACB are opposite sequences
\- Sequence affects rotating-field direction
\- It is not determined from Voltage magnitude alone

\*\*Remember\*\*

Phase Sequence = Time order of the Phases

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

How can the Phase Sequence be reversed?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Phase Sequence is reversed by interchanging any two of the three Line conductors. In a Three-Phase motor, this normally reverses the rotating Magnetic Field and direction of rotation. Connections must only be changed after safe isolation and absence-of-Voltage verification.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Swap any two Lines
\- ABC becomes ACB
\- Motor direction normally reverses
\- Isolation is essential

\*\*Remember\*\*

Two Lines exchanged → Sequence reversed

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Star or Y Connection?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Star Connection is formed by joining one end of each of three Phase windings or loads at a common Neutral Point. The other three ends are connected to the three Line conductors.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Common Neutral Point
\- Three Line terminals
\- Can form a Four-Wire system
\- Provides Line-to-Line and Line-to-Neutral Voltages

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star → Three Phase ends meet at Neutral

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the Voltage and Current relationships in a balanced Star connection?

\*\*Answer\*\*

In a balanced Star connection, Line Voltage is √3 times Phase Voltage, so VL = √3 Vph. Line Current equals Phase Current, so IL = Iph.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- VL = √3 Vph
\- Vph = VL/√3
\- IL = Iph
\- Relationships assume a balanced system

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star: Voltage gets √3; Current stays equal

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Delta Connection?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Delta Connection is formed by connecting the end of each Phase winding or load to the beginning of the next, creating a closed triangular loop. The three junctions are connected to the three Line conductors.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Closed triangular connection
\- Three Line terminals
\- Basic Delta has no Neutral
\- Each Phase is connected Line-to-Line

\*\*Remember\*\*

Delta → Three Phase elements form a triangle

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Line Voltage and Phase Voltage?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Line Voltage is measured between any two Line conductors. Phase Voltage is measured across one Phase winding or load element. In Star, VL = √3 Vph; in Delta, VL = Vph for a balanced system.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Line Voltage is Line-to-Line
\- Phase Voltage is across one Phase
\- Connection determines the relationship
\- Correct identification prevents calculation errors

\*\*Remember\*\*

Identify Star or Delta before selecting the Voltage formula

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Line Current and Phase Current?

\*\*Answer\*\*

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Line Current flows in Line conductors
\- Phase Current flows through Phase elements
\- Star Currents are equal

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Neutral Current zero in an ideal balanced Star load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The three equal Phase Currents are separated by 120°. Their Phasor sum is zero, so no resultant fundamental Current flows through the Neutral conductor in an ideal balanced load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Equal Phase Currents
\- 120° separation
\- Vector sum is zero
\- Applies to ideal balanced fundamental Currents

\*\*Remember\*\*

IA + IB + IC = 0 under ideal balance

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the real-Power formula for a balanced Three-Phase load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Using Line quantities, balanced real Power is P = √3 VLIL cos φ. Using Phase quantities, it is P = 3VphIph cos φ. The unit is Watt.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P = √3 VLIL cos φ
\- P = 3VphIph cos φ
\- cos φ is Power Factor
\- Unit is Watt

\*\*Remember\*\*

Balanced Three-Phase real Power includes √3 and Power Factor

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the Reactive and Apparent Power formulas for a balanced Three-Phase load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Reactive Power is Q = √3 VLIL sin φ and is measured in var. Apparent Power is S = √3 VLIL and is measured in VA. They are related to real Power by S² = P² + Q² for sinusoidal conditions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Q = √3 VLIL sin φ
\- S = √3 VLIL
\- Q unit is var
\- S unit is VA

\*\*Remember\*\*

P uses cos φ; Q uses sin φ; S uses neither

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an unbalanced Three-Phase system?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Three-Phase system is unbalanced when Phase-Voltage magnitudes, Phase angles or load Impedances are unequal. It can cause unequal Line Currents, Neutral Current, additional losses, Voltage variation, vibration and motor overheating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Unequal Voltage or load conditions
\- Unequal Line Currents
\- Possible Neutral Current
\- Heating and performance problems

\*\*Remember\*\*

Unbalance should be measured, investigated and corrected

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Star-Delta motor starting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Star-Delta starting initially connects suitable motor windings in Star so each winding receives VL/√3, reducing starting Current and torque. After acceleration, the starter reconnects the windings in Delta for rated running operation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Star connection during starting
\- Reduced winding Voltage
\- Reduced starting Current and torque
\- Delta connection during normal running

\*\*Remember\*\*

Star start, Delta run—only for a correctly rated motor and load

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is load balancing important in a Three-Phase distribution system?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Load balancing distributes Single-Phase loads as evenly as practical among the three Phases. It reduces Neutral Current, Voltage unbalance, conductor heating and Transformer stress while improving equipment performance and Voltage quality.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Share loads among Phases
\- Reduce Neutral Current
\- Improve Voltage balance
\- Reduce heating and losses

\*\*Remember\*\*

Balanced loading improves safety, efficiency and equipment life

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety precautions are essential when working with Three-Phase systems?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Work must follow applicable regulations and authorized procedures. Identify all sources, isolate the equipment, secure it against re-energization, verify absence of Voltage with a suitable rated tester, use appropriate PPE and test instruments, and consider Line-to-Line Voltage, Arc-Flash energy, stored energy and mechanical hazards.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Safe isolation and Lockout/Tagout
\- Absence-of-Voltage verification
\- Correct instrument and PPE ratings
\- Electrical and mechanical hazard control

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never change Phase connections or touch terminals while energized
