\# Reference Notes

\- A balanced Three-Phase source has three equal same-Frequency Phase Voltages separated by 120°.

\- Phase labels may appear as A-B-C, R-Y-B or L1-L2-L3 depending on the adopted convention and standard.

\- Phase Sequence describes order, not Voltage magnitude. Interchanging any two Line conductors reverses the sequence.

\- Three-Phase Voltage is generated by three windings positioned 120 electrical degrees apart and linked by a rotating Magnetic Field.

\- Alternator Frequency is f = PN/120 when P is the number of poles and N is speed in rpm.

\- Electrical angle equals the number of pole pairs multiplied by the corresponding mechanical angle.

\- For a balanced Star connection, VL = √3 Vph and IL = Iph.

\- For a balanced Delta connection, VL = Vph and IL = √3 Iph.

\- The √3 relationships are Phasor results; RMS magnitudes must not be added arithmetically without their Phase angles.

\- In a positive-sequence balanced Star system, a Line Voltage has a 30° angular relationship with the associated Phase Voltage. The exact lead or lag statement depends on the selected reference and notation.

\- In balanced Delta, Line Current is the Phasor difference of the two Phase Currents meeting at the Line terminal.

\- Ideal balanced fundamental Currents sum to zero at the Neutral Point.

\- An unbalanced load or zero-sequence harmonic Current can produce significant Neutral Current.

\- Triplen harmonics such as the 3rd, 9th and 15th can add in the Neutral of a Three-Phase, Four-Wire system.

\- Balanced real Power is P = √3 VLIL cos φ or P = 3VphIph cos φ.

\- Balanced reactive Power is Q = √3 VLIL sin φ, and apparent Power is S = √3 VLIL.

\- The simple √3 Power formulas assume balanced sinusoidal operation. For unbalanced loads, determine individual Phase Powers or use a suitable measurement method.

\- Motor electrical input Power is not mechanical output Power; losses and efficiency must be considered separately.

\- Balanced Three-Phase instantaneous Power is nearly constant, producing smoother torque than an equivalent Single-Phase supply.

\- Star-Delta starting is suitable only when motor winding ratings, accessible terminals, starting torque and load conditions permit the connection method.

\- A basic Delta connection has no Neutral Point, while a Star connection can provide a Neutral Point.

\- A Phase Sequence Indicator verifies Phase order; a Voltmeter reading alone does not normally establish sequence.

\- Line-to-Line measurement can involve higher Voltage and fault energy than Line-to-Neutral measurement.

\- Three-Phase testing must use instruments, probes and PPE suitable for the installation category, Voltage and available fault energy.

\- Phase conductors must not be interchanged while energized. Use approved isolation, secure against re-energization and verify absence of Voltage.

\- Chapter-15 applies AC and Three-Phase foundations to Transformers, including winding connections, Turns Ratio, losses, efficiency and Voltage Regulation.
