\# Chapter 12

\# AC Circuit Analysis

\---

\## Introduction

AC Circuit Analysis studies how Resistance, Inductance and Capacitance affect sinusoidal Voltage, Current, Phase and Power. Unlike basic DC circuits, AC circuits require Reactance, Impedance and phasor relationships.

These concepts are essential for motors, transformers, filters, power-factor correction, power electronics, industrial systems and electrical power networks.

This chapter presents AC Circuit quantities and calculations in a beginner-friendly, technically correct and engineering-oriented manner.

\## Topic 01: Resistance in AC Circuits

\### Definition

Resistance opposes Current and converts electrical energy into heat. For an ideal resistor, Voltage and Current are in phase and Z=R.

\---

\### Importance

It establishes the real-power component and is fundamental to heaters, lamps, cables and practical loads.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law: V=IR using consistent RMS or instantaneous quantities.

\#### 2. Voltage and Current reach zero and Peak together

Voltage and Current reach zero and Peak together.

\#### 3. Real Power

Real Power: P=VrmsIrms=I²R=V²/R.

\#### 4. Ideal resistor has zero reactive power

Ideal resistor has zero reactive power.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Heaters
\- Incandescent filaments
\- Cable resistance
\- Electronic load models

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- Pure R: V and I in phase.
\- Z=R∠0°.
\- Resistors consume real Power.

\## Topic 02: Inductance

\### Definition

Inductance is the property by which changing Current produces an opposing induced EMF. Its symbol is L and unit Henry.

\---

\### Importance

It explains coils, chokes, motors, transformers, filters and magnetic energy storage.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. e=−L(di/dt)

e=−L(di/dt).

\#### 2. Magnetic energy W=½LI²

Magnetic energy W=½LI².

\#### 3. An ideal inductor has zero winding Resistance

An ideal inductor has zero winding Resistance.

\#### 4. In sinusoidal steady state, Current lags Voltage by 90°

In sinusoidal steady state, Current lags Voltage by 90°.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Motor windings
\- Chokes
\- Relays
\- Filters

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- Inductance opposes Current change.
\- Unit: Henry.
\- Ideal L: Current lags Voltage.

\## Topic 04: Inductive Reactance (XL)

\### Definition

Inductive Reactance is the opposition an ideal inductor presents to sinusoidal AC: XL=2πfL.

\---

\### Importance

It determines inductor Current, Voltage drop and frequency response.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. Symbol XL; unit Ohm

Symbol XL; unit Ohm.

\#### 2. XL increases directly with Frequency and Inductance

XL increases directly with Frequency and Inductance.

\#### 3. At DC steady state, ideal XL=0

At DC steady state, ideal XL=0.

\#### 4. Inductor Impedance ZL=jXL

Inductor Impedance ZL=jXL.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Chokes
\- Motor windings
\- Filters
\- Current limiting

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- XL=2πfL.
\- Frequency up → XL up.
\- ZL=jXL.

Capacitive Reactance is the opposition an ideal capacitor presents to sinusoidal AC: XC=1/(2πfC).

\### Importance

Symbol XC; unit Ohm.

\#### 2. XC decreases as Frequency or Capacitance increases

XC decreases as Frequency or Capacitance increases.

\#### 3. At steady DC, ideal XC tends to infinity

At steady DC, ideal XC tends to infinity.

\#### 4. Capacitor Impedance ZC=−jXC

Capacitor Impedance ZC=−jXC.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Filters
\- Power-factor correction
\- Timing circuits

\---

\### Key Takeaways

Magnitude |Z|=√(R²+X²).

\#### 2. Angle θ=tan⁻¹(X/R)

Angle θ=tan⁻¹(X/R).

\#### 3. Inductive X positive; Capacitive X negative

Inductive X positive; Capacitive X negative.

\#### 4. Ohm's Law in phasors

Ohm's Law in phasors: V=IZ.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Motor circuits

\## Topic 07: Phase Relationship in R, L and C Circuits

\### Definition

Phase Relationship describes the displacement between sinusoidal Voltage and Current.

\---

\### Importance

It determines reactive behavior, Power Factor and AC Power.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. Pure R

Pure R: V and I in phase.

\#### 2. Pure L

Pure L: I lags V by 90°.

\#### 3. Pure C

Pure C: I leads V by 90°.

\#### 4. Practical components include loss and are not perfectly ideal

Practical components include loss and are not perfectly ideal.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Phasor diagrams
\- Power-factor studies
\- Filters
\- Motor analysis

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- R: in phase.
\- L: Current lags.
\- C: Current leads.

\## Topic 08: Series RLC Circuit

\### Definition

A Series RLC Circuit connects R, L and C in one Current path. Its Impedance is Z=R+j(XL−XC).

\---

\### Importance

It demonstrates resonance, frequency selection, Phase and Power Factor.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. |Z|=√[R²+(XL−XC)²]

|Z|=√[R²+(XL−XC)²].

\#### 2. I=V/|Z|

I=V/|Z|.

\#### 3. Resonance occurs when XL=XC

Resonance occurs when XL=XC.

\#### 4. At resonance, Z=R, Current is maximum and Power Factor is unity in the ideal series model

At resonance, Z=R, Current is maximum and Power Factor is unity in the ideal series model.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Tuned circuits
\- Band-pass filters
\- Frequency selection
\- Resonant converters

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- Net Reactance=XL−XC.
\- Resonance: XL=XC.
\- At series resonance, Z is minimum.

\## Topic 09: Power Factor

\### Definition

Power Factor is the ratio of real Power to apparent Power: PF=P/S=cosφ for sinusoidal systems.

\---

\### Importance

It affects Current, losses, Voltage drop and equipment utilization.

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. Unity PF means Voltage and Current are in phase

Unity PF means Voltage and Current are in phase.

\#### 2. Inductive loads commonly have lagging PF

Inductive loads commonly have lagging PF.

\#### 3. Capacitive loads commonly have leading PF

Capacitive loads commonly have leading PF.

\#### 4. Low PF requires more Current for the same real Power

Low PF requires more Current for the same real Power.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\- Motor correction
\- Utility systems
\- Generators
\- Industrial loads

\---

\### Key Takeaways

\- PF=P/S.
\- Low PF increases Current.
\- Correction must avoid overcompensation and resonance.

The Power Triangle is a right-triangle representation relating real, reactive and apparent Power.

\---

\### Importance

\---

\### Main Concepts

\#### 1. Real Power P in Watt

Real Power P in Watt.

\#### 2. Reactive Power Q in var

Reactive Power Q in var.

\#### 3. Apparent Power S in VA

Apparent Power S in VA.

\#### 4. S²=P²+Q² and tanφ=Q/P

S²=P²+Q² and tanφ=Q/P.

\---

\### Real-Life Applications

\# Chapter Summary

\- Resistance consumes real Power and keeps Voltage and Current in phase.
\- Inductance opposes Current change; ideal Inductor Current lags Voltage.
\- Capacitance stores electric-field energy; ideal Capacitor Current leads Voltage.
\- XL=2πfL and XC=1/(2πfC).
\- Impedance combines Resistance and Reactance.
\- Series RLC resonance occurs when XL=XC.
\- Power Factor equals real Power divided by apparent Power.
\- The Power Triangle relates P, Q and S.
\- Low Power Factor increases Current for the same real Power.
\- Practical systems require correct RMS measurement, ratings and safety.
