\# Basic Viva



\### Viva-001



\*\*Question\*\*



What is Voltage?



\*\*Answer\*\*



Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. It represents the electrical energy transferred per unit charge and is measured in Volt (V).



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Energy transferred per unit charge.

\- Measured between two points.

\- SI Unit: Volt (V).



\*\*Remember\*\*



Voltage = Energy / Charge

\### Viva-002



\*\*Question\*\*



What is one Volt?



\*\*Answer\*\*



One Volt is the Potential Difference when one Joule of energy is transferred for each Coulomb of charge.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- 1 V = 1 J/C.

\- Voltage is energy per charge.

\- Volt is an SI derived unit.



\*\*Remember\*\*



1 Volt = 1 Joule per Coulomb

\### Viva-003



\*\*Question\*\*



What is Electromotive Force (EMF)?



\*\*Answer\*\*



Electromotive Force is the energy supplied by an electrical source per unit charge. It is measured in Volt and is not a mechanical force.

\- Energy supplied per unit charge.

\- Produced by an electrical source.

\- SI Unit: Volt (V).

\### Viva-006



\*\*Question\*\*



What is the main difference between EMF and Potential Difference?



\*\*Answer\*\*



EMF is the energy supplied by an electrical source per unit charge, whereas Potential Difference is the energy converted or transferred per unit charge between two circuit points.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- EMF is associated with a source.

\- Potential Difference is associated with two circuit points or a load.

\- Both are measured in Volt.



\*\*Remember\*\*



EMF → Energy Supplied

Potential Difference → Energy Converted

\### Viva-007



\*\*Question\*\*



What is Electrical Resistance?



\*\*Answer\*\*



Electrical Resistance is the property of a material or circuit component that opposes the flow of electric current. Its SI unit is Ohm (Ω).



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Opposes current flow.

\- SI Unit: Ohm (Ω).

\### Viva-008



\*\*Question\*\*



What is one Ohm?



\*\*Answer\*\*



One Ohm is the resistance of a conductor when a Potential Difference of one Volt produces a Current of one Ampere through it.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- 1 Ω = 1 V/A.

\- Ohm is the SI unit of Resistance.

\- Symbol: Ω



\*\*Remember\*\*



1 Ohm = 1 Volt per Ampere

\### Viva-010



\*\*Question\*\*



State Ohm's Law.



\*\*Answer\*\*



Ohm's Law states that current through a conductor is directly proportional to the Potential Difference across it, provided that temperature and other physical conditions remain constant.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- V is directly proportional to I.

\- Physical conditions must remain constant.

\- Formula: V = IR.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Ohm's Law → V = IR

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- SI Unit: Volt.

\- Newton is the unit of mechanical force.



\*\*Remember\*\*



EMF Name Contains “Force,” but Unit is Volt

\### Viva-012



\*\*Question\*\*



Why is the terminal voltage of a battery lower than its EMF during discharge?



\*\*Answer\*\*



When load current flows, a voltage drop occurs across the battery's internal resistance. Therefore, the terminal voltage is V = E − Ir and is lower than the EMF.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Practical sources have internal resistance.

\- Internal drop equals Ir.

\- Discharge terminal voltage: V = E − Ir.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Higher Load Current → Greater Internal Voltage Drop

\### Viva-013



\*\*Question\*\*



Why is a Voltmeter connected in parallel?



\*\*Answer\*\*



A Voltmeter is connected in parallel so that it measures the Potential Difference between the same two terminals as the component. Its high internal resistance minimizes circuit disturbance.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Measures between two points.

\- Connected across component terminals.

\- Has high internal resistance.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Voltmeter → Parallel → High Resistance

\### Viva-014



\*\*Question\*\*



Why must a circuit normally be de-energized before measuring Resistance?



\*\*Answer\*\*



An Ohmmeter uses its internal source to apply a test current. External circuit voltage can produce incorrect readings, damage the meter and expose the user to danger.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Ohmmeter has an internal source.

\- External Voltage causes incorrect readings.

\- De-energization improves safety and protects the meter.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Resistance Measurement → Power OFF

\### Viva-015



\*\*Question\*\*



Why does a longer conductor have higher Resistance?



\*\*Answer\*\*



A longer conductor provides a longer path for charge carriers, increasing the number of interactions that oppose their motion. For constant material, area and temperature, R is directly proportional to L.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- R ∝ L.

\- Longer path causes greater opposition.

\- Other factors must remain constant.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Double Length → Double Resistance

\### Viva-016



\*\*Question\*\*



Why does a thicker conductor have lower Resistance?



\*\*Answer\*\*



A thicker conductor has a larger cross-sectional area and provides more available paths for charge carriers. Therefore, Resistance is inversely proportional to area.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- R ∝ 1/A.

\- Larger area provides more current paths.

\- Lower resistance reduces voltage drop and loss.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Larger Area → Lower Resistance

\### Viva-017



\*\*Question\*\*



How does temperature affect the Resistance of metallic conductors?



\*\*Answer\*\*



For most metallic conductors, Resistance increases as temperature increases because greater lattice vibration causes more opposition to charge-carrier movement.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Most metals have a positive temperature coefficient.

\- Higher temperature generally increases metallic Resistance.

\- Temperature must remain constant when applying Ohm's Law.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Metal: Temperature ↑ → Resistance ↑

\### Viva-018



\*\*Question\*\*



What is an ohmic conductor?



\*\*Answer\*\*



An ohmic conductor obeys Ohm's Law and maintains a constant ratio of Voltage to Current when temperature and other physical conditions remain constant. Its V–I graph is a straight line through the origin.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Obeys V = IR.

\- V/I remains constant.

\- Has a linear V–I characteristic.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Ohmic Conductor → Linear V–I Graph

\### Viva-019



\*\*Question\*\*



Does Ohm's Law apply to every electrical device?



\*\*Answer\*\*



No. Ohm's Law with constant Resistance applies to ohmic conductors under constant physical conditions. Diodes, thermistors and filament lamps over a wide operating range have nonlinear or changing V–I relationships.



\*\*Key Points\*\*



\- Not universal for every device.

\- Applies to ohmic conductors.

\- Non-ohmic devices have nonlinear or changing V–I characteristics.



\*\*Remember\*\*



Constant V/I → Ohmic
