\## Transmission and Distribution

\---

\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the difference between Transmission and Distribution?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Transmission transfers bulk electrical Power at High Voltage between generating stations and major Substations, while Distribution delivers Power from Substations to individual consumers.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Transmission handles large Power blocks
\- Distribution is closer to consumers
\- Transformers connect Voltage levels
\- Both require protection and control

\*\*Remember\*\*

Transmission moves bulk Power; Distribution completes delivery.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is electrical Power transmitted at High Voltage?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For fixed Power and Power Factor, raising Voltage lowers Current. Lower Current reduces I²R loss, Voltage drop, conductor heating and required conductor area.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- P = √3VLILcosφ
\- Current is inversely proportional to Voltage
\- Loss is proportional to Current squared
\- High Voltage improves conductor economy

\*\*Remember\*\*

Same Power: higher V → lower I → lower I²R loss.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Overhead and Underground transmission.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Overhead Lines are generally cheaper, easier to inspect and easier to repair, but they are exposed to weather and require visible clearances. Underground Cables have less visual and weather exposure but cost more, dissipate Heat less easily and have higher Capacitance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Overhead has lower initial cost
\- Underground suits restricted urban routes
\- Cable fault repair is usually more difficult
\- Cable charging Current is important in AC service

\*\*Remember\*\*

Overhead is accessible; Underground is protected but costly and capacitive.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is HVDC Transmission?

\*\*Answer\*\*

HVDC Transmission transfers Power as High-Voltage Direct Current using converter stations between AC networks and the DC link.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Uses AC/DC converter stations
\- Provides controllable Power flow
\- Useful for long submarine or Underground links
\- Can connect asynchronous AC Grids

\*\*Remember\*\*

HVDC line advantages must justify expensive converter terminals.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

What are the four basic Transmission-Line parameters?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The basic distributed parameters are Resistance R, Inductance L, Capacitance C and leakage Conductance G.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- R and L form Series Impedance
\- G and C form Shunt Admittance
\- Parameters are specified per unit length
\- They determine Voltage, Current and loss behavior

\*\*Remember\*\*

Series: R and L; Shunt: G and C.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the practical effects of line Inductance and Capacitance.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Inductance produces Inductive Reactance and Reactive Voltage drop, while Capacitance draws charging Current and can raise receiving-end Voltage under light-load conditions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- XL = 2πfL
\- IC = 2πfCV
\- L stores magnetic-field Energy
\- C stores electric-field Energy

\*\*Remember\*\*

Inductance opposes Current change; Capacitance creates line-charging Current.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

How are Short, Medium and Long Transmission Lines modeled?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Short Line uses Series Impedance and neglects Shunt Capacitance, a Medium Line uses lumped Nominal-π or Nominal-T parameters, and a Long Line uses continuously distributed parameters.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Short Line: C neglected
\- Medium Line: C lumped
\- Long Line: parameters distributed
\- Distance boundaries are approximate

\*\*Remember\*\*

The more significant the Capacitance, the more detailed the model.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

State the ABCD equations of a Transmission Line.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The sending-end quantities are VS = AVR + BIR and IS = CVR + DIR.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- A and D are dimensionless
\- B has Impedance unit
\- C has Admittance unit
\- Constants depend on the line model

\*\*Remember\*\*

ABCD relates sending-end quantities to receiving-end quantities.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate Primary and Secondary Distribution.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Primary Distribution carries Medium-Voltage Power from a Substation to Distribution Transformers or major MV consumers, while Secondary Distribution carries Low-Voltage Power from those Transformers to consumer services.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Primary uses MV Feeders
\- Secondary uses LV Distributors
\- A Distribution Transformer separates the stages
\- Secondary Current is higher for the same Power

\*\*Remember\*\*

Primary: Substation to Transformer; Secondary: Transformer to consumer.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Distribution Transformer?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Distribution Transformer reduces Primary Distribution Voltage to Secondary Distribution or utilization Voltage near the load center.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Rated mainly in kVA
\- Frequency remains unchanged
\- Has Core and Copper losses
\- May be pole-, pad- or Substation-mounted

\*\*Remember\*\*

Locate transformation near the load to reduce long high-current LV paths.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate a Feeder, Distributor and Service Main.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Feeder carries Power from a Substation to a distribution area, a Distributor has multiple load tappings, and a Service Main connects the Distributor to one consumer supply point.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Feeder design emphasizes ampacity
\- Distributor design emphasizes Voltage drop
\- Service design follows consumer demand
\- Utility terminology should be confirmed

\*\*Remember\*\*

Feeder → Distributor → Service Main → Consumer.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate technical and non-technical losses.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Technical losses arise from physical network behavior such as I²R and Transformer loss, while non-technical losses arise from unauthorized use, metering, data or billing problems.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Conductor loss is technical
\- Core and Copper losses are technical
\- Meter tampering is non-technical
\- Loss boundaries and periods must match

\*\*Remember\*\*

Physical network loss differs from commercial or data loss.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

How can technical Distribution losses be reduced?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Losses can be reduced by using appropriate Voltage and conductors, correcting Power Factor, balancing phases, controlling harmonics, locating efficient Transformers near loads, optimizing Feeders and maintaining sound connections.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Reduce unnecessary Current
\- Reduce Resistance and path length
\- Improve phase balance
\- Select efficient equipment

\*\*Remember\*\*

Loss reduction must remain safe, reliable and economically justified.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Transfers Power between regions
\- Maintains frequency and Voltage
\- Supports restoration after disturbances

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare radial, ring and interconnected Distribution systems.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A radial system has one normal supply path and is simple; a ring provides an alternative path around a loop; an interconnected system uses multiple Feeders or sources for the highest flexibility and complexity.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Radial has low cost and lower reliability
\- Ring improves restoration
\- Interconnection improves flexibility
\- Complexity and fault level rise with multiple paths

\*\*Remember\*\*

More supply paths generally improve continuity but complicate protection.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is protection coordination important in industrial Distribution?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Protection coordination ensures that the device nearest a fault operates first where practical, limiting equipment damage and preventing unnecessary shutdown of healthy plant sections.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Improves selectivity
\- Limits outage area
\- Coordinates utility and plant protection
\- Must consider fault level and operating time

\*\*Remember\*\*

Clear the smallest practical faulty section safely.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must an industrial Power System be reviewed before adding a large load?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A large load can change Transformer and cable loading, Voltage drop, fault level, starting dip, harmonics, Power Factor and protection coordination.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Confirm thermal capacity
\- Check Voltage and Power quality
\- Update short-circuit study
\- Review protection and future growth

\*\*Remember\*\*

Plant expansion changes the system, not only the final circuit.
