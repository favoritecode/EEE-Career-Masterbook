\## Transmission and Distribution

\## About This Chapter

This chapter was prepared using established Power-System, Transmission, Distribution and Substation textbooks; international line, Cable, Transformer and Switchgear standards; industrial Power-System guidance; and official Bangladesh Grid and utility resources. The references support deeper study of High-Voltage transfer, Overhead and Underground systems, line parameters, line models, Distribution planning, losses, Substations, protection and industrial applications.

Voltage classifications, network arrangements and operating practices vary by country and utility. Always apply the current local Grid code, Distribution code, safety rules and equipment instructions to practical work.

\# Reference Notes

\- Transmission transfers bulk Power; Distribution delivers it to consumers.

\- For fixed balanced Three-Phase Power and Power Factor, Current decreases as line Voltage increases.

\- Three-Phase conductor loss is approximately 3I²R.

\- High Voltage reduces Current, loss, Voltage drop and conductor requirement but increases insulation and equipment cost.

\- Overhead Lines are generally economical and accessible; Underground Cables are less visible but costlier and more capacitive.

\- R and L form Series Impedance; G and C form Shunt Admittance.

\- Short-Line models neglect Shunt Capacitance.

\- Medium-Line models commonly use Nominal-π or Nominal-T equivalents.

\- Long-Line models treat parameters as distributed.

\- Traditional line-length classifications are approximate guides, not universal limits.

\- Primary Distribution normally operates at MV and Secondary Distribution at LV.

\- Distribution Transformers are rated mainly in VA and should be located with load, loss and safety in mind.

\- A Feeder carries Power to an area, a Distributor has multiple tappings and a Service Main supplies one consumer.

\- Technical loss differs from unauthorized-use, metering and billing-related non-technical loss.

\- Correct Voltage, conductor sizing, Power-Factor correction, phase balance and efficient Transformers reduce technical loss.

\- A Substation transforms, switches, protects, measures and controls Power.

\- Radial systems are simple; ring and interconnected systems provide more supply paths with greater complexity.

\- Chapter-23 continues with Electrical Wiring, Cables and Installation.
