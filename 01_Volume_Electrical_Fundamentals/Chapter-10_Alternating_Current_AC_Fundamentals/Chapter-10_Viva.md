\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Alternating Current?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Alternating Current is an Electric Current that changes its magnitude with time and reverses its direction periodically.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- AC varies with time.
\- It reverses direction periodically.
\- It is widely used in electrical power systems.

\*\*Remember\*\*

AC → Changing Magnitude and Periodic Direction Reversal

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Frequency and state its unit.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Frequency is the number of complete Cycles of a periodic waveform occurring per second. Its symbol is f and its SI unit is Hertz (Hz).

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Symbol: f
\- Unit: Hertz
\- One Hertz equals one Cycle per second.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Frequency = Cycles per Second

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Time Period and state its relationship with Frequency.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Time Period is the time required for a periodic waveform to complete one full Cycle. Its symbol is T, its unit is second and T = 1/f.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Time Period is measured in second.
\- T = 1/f.
\- Higher Frequency means shorter Period.

\*\*Remember\*\*

T = 1/f and f = 1/T

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is RMS Value?

\*\*Answer\*\*

RMS Value is the square root of the mean of the squares of Instantaneous Values over one complete Cycle. It represents the equivalent DC value that produces the same average heating in a resistor.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- RMS means Root Mean Square.
\- It represents equivalent resistive heating.
\- AC equipment ratings normally use RMS Values.

\*\*Remember\*\*

RMS → Effective Heating Value

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is the Average Value of a symmetrical sine wave?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Its full-cycle algebraic Average Value is zero because equal positive and negative areas cancel. Its positive half-cycle Average Value is 2Vm/π, approximately 0.637Vm.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Full-cycle algebraic average is zero.
\- Positive half-cycle average is 0.637 × Peak.
\- Rectified AC can have a non-zero average.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Full Cycle = 0; Positive Half-Cycle = 0.637 Peak

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Phase and Phase Difference?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Phase describes a periodic waveform's angular position relative to a reference. Phase Difference is the angular or equivalent time displacement between two waveforms of the same Frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Phase is measured in degrees or radians.
\- Phase Difference compares same-Frequency waveforms.
\- One Cycle equals 360°.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Phase Difference → Relative Waveform Position

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is AC widely used in power systems?

\*\*Answer\*\*

AC is widely used because transformers can efficiently change its Voltage level, allowing high-Voltage low-Current transmission and convenient distribution. AC is also produced naturally by alternators and operates many motors directly.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- AC Voltage is easily transformed.
\- High transmission Voltage reduces Current and I²R Loss.
\- AC generators and motors are widely used.

\*\*Remember\*\*

AC → Generation, Transformation, Transmission and Motors

\# Advanced Viva

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Write and explain the general sinusoidal Voltage equation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The equation is v(t) = Vm sin(ωt + φ), where Vm is Peak Voltage, ω is Angular Frequency in rad/s, t is Time and φ is the initial Phase Angle.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Vm sets Amplitude.
\- ω = 2πf sets the rate of oscillation.
\- φ sets the waveform's initial Phase.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Sinusoid = Amplitude × sin(Angle + Phase)

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Peak, RMS and Average Values.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Peak is the maximum magnitude from zero. RMS represents equivalent resistive heating. Average is the algebraic mean over a stated interval; for a symmetrical sine wave it is zero over a full Cycle but 0.637 times Peak over a positive half-cycle.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Peak describes maximum stress.
\- RMS describes effective heating.
\- Average depends on the stated interval and polarity.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Peak → Maximum; RMS → Effective; Average → Algebraic Mean

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Why does a symmetrical AC waveform produce heating even when its full-cycle Average Current is zero?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Resistive heating depends on i²R. Squaring makes both positive and negative Current portions contribute positively, so they do not cancel. RMS Current represents this heating effect.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Heating depends on the square of Current.
\- Direction reversal does not reverse heat generation.
\- Algebraic Average and RMS measure different properties.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Average May Be Zero While RMS and Heating Are Non-Zero

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Crest Factor and Form Factor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Crest Factor is Peak Value divided by RMS Value. Form Factor is RMS Value divided by Average Rectified Value. For a sine wave, they are approximately 1.414 and 1.11 respectively.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Crest Factor compares Peak with RMS.
\- Form Factor compares RMS with Average Rectified Value.
\- Both depend on waveform shape.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Sine Wave: Crest Factor 1.414; Form Factor 1.11

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

How is Phase Difference calculated from time displacement?

\*\*Answer\*\*

For two same-Frequency waveforms, Phase Difference in degrees is (Δt/T) × 360°, where Δt is their time displacement and T is the common Time Period.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both waveforms must have the same Frequency for a constant Phase Difference.
\- One complete Period equals 360°.
\- Leading and lagging depend on which waveform arrives first.

\*\*Remember\*\*

φ = (Δt/T) × 360°

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Phasor, and what is its limitation?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Phasor is a rotating-vector mathematical representation of sinusoidal magnitude and Phase at one Frequency. It simplifies steady-state AC analysis but is not the physical waveform and cannot directly combine different Frequencies in one ordinary phasor calculation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It represents magnitude and Phase.
\- It is used for sinusoidal steady-state analysis.
\- Quantities must share one Frequency.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Phasor → One-Frequency Sinusoidal Representation

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is a True-RMS meter important for distorted waveforms?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A suitable True-RMS meter calculates the heating-equivalent RMS Value from the actual waveform within its specified bandwidth and Crest Factor limits. An average-responding RMS-calibrated meter assumes a sine-wave conversion and may be inaccurate for distortion.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Distortion changes the Average-to-RMS relationship.
\- True-RMS instruments still have bandwidth and Crest Factor limits.
\- Instrument ratings and specifications must be checked.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Distorted Waveform → Suitable True-RMS Measurement

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the basic Phase relationships of ideal R, L and C.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In an ideal resistor, Voltage and Current are in phase. In an ideal inductor, Current lags Voltage by 90°. In an ideal capacitor, Current leads Voltage by 90°.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- R: V and I in phase.
\- L: I lags V by 90°.
\- C: I leads V by 90°.

\*\*Remember\*\*

R In Phase; L Current Lags; C Current Leads

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is Frequency important for AC equipment?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Frequency affects transformer Flux and losses, motor speed, Inductive and Capacitive Reactance, timing and heating. Equipment designed for one Frequency may not operate correctly or safely at another without suitable approval or derating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Motor synchronous speed depends on Frequency.
\- Reactance changes with Frequency.
\- Transformer operation depends on Voltage-to-Frequency conditions.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Check Both Voltage and Frequency Ratings

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

How should an unknown AC waveform be characterized safely and correctly?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify its waveform shape, RMS Value, Peak Value, Frequency, Period, Average or DC component and Phase relative to a defined reference. Use instruments and probes with suitable Voltage, Current, Frequency, bandwidth, category and safety ratings.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- One number does not fully describe a waveform.
\- Measurement method must suit the waveform.
\- Safe connection and instrument ratings are essential.

\*\*Remember\*\*

Waveform + RMS + Peak + Frequency + Phase + Safety
