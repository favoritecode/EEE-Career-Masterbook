\# Basic Viva

\### Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Transformer?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Transformer is a static electromagnetic device that transfers AC electrical energy between circuits through Mutual Induction, normally at the same Frequency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Static electromagnetic device
\- Transfers AC energy through Mutual Induction
\- Normally does not change Frequency

\*\*Remember\*\*

Transformer → AC Energy Transfer at Same Frequency

\### Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

State the working principle of a Transformer.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Transformer works on Mutual Induction. Alternating Primary Current creates changing core Flux that links the Secondary and induces EMF according to Faraday's Law.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Primary AC produces changing Flux
\- Flux links both windings
\- Faraday's Law produces induced EMF

\*\*Remember\*\*

Changing Flux + Coupled Windings → Transformer Action

\### Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Name the main parts of a Transformer.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Main parts include the magnetic core, Primary and Secondary windings, insulation, terminals or bushings, enclosure and cooling system. Larger units may include a tank, insulating liquid, radiators and Tap Changer.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Core guides Flux
\- Windings transfer electrical energy
\- Insulation and cooling ensure reliable operation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Core + Windings + Insulation + Cooling

\### Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Core-Type and Shell-Type Transformers.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In Core-Type construction, windings surround substantial parts of the core limbs. In Shell-Type construction, the core surrounds a substantial part of the windings, commonly on the central limb.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Both use a magnetic core
\- Their core-winding arrangement differs
\- Construction affects coupling and mechanical design

\*\*Remember\*\*

Core-Type: Windings Around Core; Shell-Type: Core Around Windings

\### Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between Step-Up and Step-Down Transformers.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Step-Up Transformer has higher Secondary Voltage and more Secondary turns in ideal operation. A Step-Down Transformer has lower Secondary Voltage and fewer Secondary turns.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Step-Up: V₂>V₁
\- Step-Down: V₂<V₁
\- Ideal Current ratio is inverse to Voltage ratio

\*\*Remember\*\*

Voltage Up → Current Capability Down for Equal VA

\### Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

State the Transformer EMF Equation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

For sinusoidal core Flux, the RMS induced EMF is E=4.44fNΦm, where f is Frequency, N is turns and Φm is maximum Flux.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Equation assumes sinusoidal Flux
\- EMF is proportional to f, N and Φm
\- EMF per turn is 4.44fΦm

\*\*Remember\*\*

E = 4.44fNΦm

\### Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

What is Turns Ratio?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Turns Ratio compares winding turns. Using a=N₂/N₁, an ideal Transformer has V₂/V₁=N₂/N₁, while its Current ratio is inverse.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Turns Ratio determines ideal Voltage Ratio
\- Current Ratio is inverse
\- The ratio convention must be stated

\*\*Remember\*\*

V₂/V₁=N₂/N₁

\### Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Name the main Transformer losses.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The main losses are Core Loss and Copper Loss. Core Loss includes Hysteresis and Eddy-Current Loss, while Copper Loss is caused by winding Resistance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Core Loss occurs in the magnetic core
\- Copper Loss is I²R
\- Stray and dielectric losses may also occur

\*\*Remember\*\*

Total Loss = Core + Copper + Other Losses

\### Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Transformer Efficiency.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Transformer Efficiency is useful output Power divided by input Power, multiplied by 100%. Input Power equals output Power plus losses.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- η=Pout/Pin×100%
\- Efficiency is below 100% in practice
\- Loss reduction improves Efficiency

\*\*Remember\*\*

Efficiency = Output / Input

\# Advanced Viva

\### Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Why must a normal Transformer not be connected to steady DC?

\*\*Answer\*\*

After the switching transient, steady DC produces no continuing Flux change and therefore no normal counter EMF. Current may then be limited mainly by winding Resistance, causing overheating and saturation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Transformer action requires changing Flux
\- Steady DC removes continuous induced counter EMF
\- Excessive Current can damage the winding

\*\*Remember\*\*

Normal Transformer Requires AC or Changing Flux

\### Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain Transformer operation under load.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Secondary Current produces an MMF opposing the Main Flux change. The Primary draws additional Current so the Main Flux remains approximately set by applied Voltage and Frequency while Power transfers to the load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Secondary Current creates opposing MMF
\- Primary Current increases with load
\- Core Flux remains approximately constant

\*\*Remember\*\*

Load Current Is Reflected to the Primary

\### Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

Differentiate between an Isolation Transformer and an Autotransformer.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Isolation Transformer has separate windings and provides galvanic separation. An Autotransformer uses a shared tapped winding, is often smaller and more efficient for suitable ratios, but provides no galvanic isolation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Separate versus shared winding
\- Isolation differs fundamentally
\- Autotransformer can save material

\*\*Remember\*\*

Autotransformer ≠ Isolation Transformer

\### Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is an energized CT Secondary dangerous if open-circuited?

\*\*Answer\*\*

With Primary Current flowing, an open CT Secondary cannot provide the opposing ampere-turns of normal operation. Core Flux and Secondary Voltage may rise dangerously, threatening insulation and personnel.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- CT Secondary should normally remain closed through its burden
\- Open circuit can create high Voltage
\- Safe shorting and grounding procedures are required

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never Open an Energized CT Secondary

\### Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the effect of Voltage and Frequency on core Flux.

\*\*Answer\*\*

From E≈4.44fNΦm, maximum Flux is approximately proportional to V/f for fixed turns. Excessive Voltage or reduced Frequency increases Flux and may cause saturation, high magnetizing Current and overheating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Flux depends on V/f
\- Low Frequency can overflux the core
\- Ratings must include Frequency

\*\*Remember\*\*

Maintain the Designed V/f Ratio

\### Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain ideal Impedance transformation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

An impedance Z₂ connected to the Secondary appears from the Primary as Z₂′=(N₁/N₂)²Z₂. Thus Impedance transforms by the square of the turns ratio.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Impedance ratio is the square of turns ratio
\- Reference side must be stated
\- Used in matching and equivalent circuits

\*\*Remember\*\*

Impedance Transforms by Turns Ratio Squared

\### Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

When does maximum Transformer Efficiency occur?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At fixed Voltage and Frequency, maximum Efficiency occurs approximately when variable Copper Loss equals constant Core Loss.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Core Loss is approximately constant
\- Copper Loss varies with load squared
\- Equality gives the maximum-efficiency loading condition

\*\*Remember\*\*

Maximum η: Copper Loss = Core Loss

\### Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What is All-Day Efficiency?

\*\*Answer\*\*

All-Day Efficiency is output energy divided by input energy over a 24-hour period. It is especially important for Distribution Transformers whose load varies throughout the day.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- It is energy based
\- It covers 24 hours
\- It includes constant No-Load Loss during lightly loaded periods

\*\*Remember\*\*

All-Day Efficiency → 24-Hour Energy Efficiency

\### Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

Define Voltage Regulation and explain the effect of Power Factor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Voltage Regulation measures Secondary terminal-Voltage change from No-Load to specified load at constant Primary Voltage and Frequency. Lagging loads usually give positive Regulation, while sufficiently leading loads can give negative Regulation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Internal Resistance and Leakage Reactance cause drop
\- Regulation depends on load Power Factor
\- The denominator convention must be stated

\*\*Remember\*\*

Regulation Depends on Load and Power Factor

\### Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

How should a Transformer be selected and operated safely?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Select rated VA or kVA, Voltages, Frequency, phase, Impedance, cooling, insulation, environment and load characteristics. Provide correct protection, grounding, isolation, ventilation and maintenance according to standards and manufacturer instructions.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Nameplate ratings must match the system
\- Protection and cooling are essential
\- CT, liquid, lifting and high-Voltage hazards require special procedures

\*\*Remember\*\*

Correct Rating + Protection + Cooling + Safety
