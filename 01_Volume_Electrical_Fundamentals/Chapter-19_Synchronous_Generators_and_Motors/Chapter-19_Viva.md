\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Synchronous Machine?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Synchronous Machine is an AC rotating machine whose Rotor field maintains a fixed average angular relationship with the Stator Rotating Magnetic Field during steady operation.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- AC rotating machine
\- Rotor and Stator fields remain locked
\- Zero steady-state slip
\- Generator or Motor operation

\*\*Remember\*\*

Synchronous means operating in step with the rotating field.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operating principle of a Synchronous Machine.

\*\*Answer\*\*

In Generator mode, a rotating Rotor field induces Three-Phase EMF in the Stator. In Motor mode, the excited Rotor field locks with the Stator Rotating Magnetic Field and develops torque at Synchronous Speed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Generator action uses electromagnetic induction
\- Motor action uses field interaction
\- Rotor field comes from excitation or magnets
\- Energy flow determines operating mode

\*\*Remember\*\*

Generator: induction; Motor: magnetic locking.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is the Armature winding normally placed on the Stator?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The high-Voltage, high-Power AC winding is easier to insulate, cool, protect and connect when stationary. Only the lower Rotor field Power needs transfer to the rotating part.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Easier high-Voltage insulation
\- Simpler external connections
\- Better cooling and protection
\- Smaller rotating electrical connection

\*\*Remember\*\*

Stator carries the main AC Power; Rotor carries the field.

\## Viva-005

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Salient-Pole and Cylindrical Rotors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Salient Rotors have projecting poles, many poles, large diameter and suit low speed. Cylindrical Rotors have a smooth surface, usually two or four poles, long axial length and high mechanical strength for high speed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Salient: low speed and non-uniform Air Gap
\- Cylindrical: high speed and nearly uniform Air Gap
\- Hydro machines commonly use Salient Rotors
\- Turbo-Alternators use Cylindrical Rotors

\*\*Remember\*\*

Hydro → Salient; steam turbine → Cylindrical.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

State and explain the Synchronous Speed formula.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Ns = 120f/P, where Ns is speed in rpm, f is electrical frequency in hertz and P is total pole number. Speed increases with frequency and decreases with pole number.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ns = 120f/P
\- Direct relation with frequency
\- Inverse relation with poles
\- Zero steady Motor slip

\*\*Remember\*\*

At 50 Hz, a 4-pole machine runs at 1500 rpm.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an Alternator?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An Alternator is a Synchronous Generator that converts mechanical Power from a prime mover into AC electrical Power at a frequency set by Rotor speed and pole number.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Three-Phase AC output
\- Excited Rotor field
\- Frequency f = PN/120

Alternator is the standard Synchronous Generator.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

State the Alternator generated-EMF equation.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The RMS generated EMF per phase is Eph = 4.44kwfΦT, where kw is winding factor, f is frequency, Φ is Flux per pole and T is series turns per phase.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Eph = 4.44kwfΦT
\- RMS phase value
\- Depends on winding distribution
\- Proportional to frequency, Flux and turns

\*\*Remember\*\*

More field Flux generally produces more internal generated EMF.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

What controls real and reactive Power of a grid-connected Alternator?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Prime-mover mechanical input mainly controls real-Power output, while Rotor excitation mainly controls reactive-Power exchange and Voltage contribution. The controls interact and must remain within machine limits.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Governor/torque controls real Power
\- Excitation controls reactive behavior
\- Grid fixes frequency closely
\- Limits and protection remain active

\*\*Remember\*\*

Mechanical input → MW; excitation → MVAr.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is a basic Synchronous Motor not self-starting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At standstill, the Rotating Stator field moves past the stationary Rotor rapidly and produces alternating torque directions. Rotor inertia prevents immediate locking, so average starting torque is zero in the ideal basic machine.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Rapidly rotating Stator field
\- Alternating torque at standstill
\- Rotor inertia
\- Starting method required

\*\*Remember\*\*

The Rotor must approach Synchronous Speed before it can pull into step.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

How does excitation affect Synchronous-Motor Power Factor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

At a fixed mechanical load, underexcitation generally produces lagging Current, suitable excitation gives approximately unity Power Factor and overexcitation can produce leading Current and reactive-Power support.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Underexcited: lagging
\- Correctly excited: near unity
\- Overexcited: leading
\- Operate within thermal and stability limits

\*\*Remember\*\*

Excitation changes reactive Power, not Synchronous Speed.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is an excitation system?

\*\*Answer\*\*

An excitation system supplies and regulates DC field Current to the Rotor of a wound-field Synchronous Machine. It may use static Slip-Ring excitation or a brushless exciter with rotating rectifiers.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Establishes Rotor Flux
\- Regulates field Current
\- Static or brushless forms
\- Works with AVR, limits and protection

\*\*Remember\*\*

No healthy field means no normal wound-field synchronous operation.

\## Viva-013

\*\*Question\*\*

What conditions are required for Alternator synchronization?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Incoming Voltage must match the bus within limits, frequencies must be nearly equal, Phase Sequence must be identical and corresponding phase angles must be nearly equal at breaker closing.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Equal Voltage
\- Equal frequency
\- Same Phase Sequence
\- Correct phase angle at closing

\*\*Remember\*\*

V, f, sequence and angle must match.

\## Viva-014

\*\*Question\*\*

Describe the practical synchronization sequence.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Confirm protection and Phase Sequence, bring the incoming machine to rated speed, match Voltage through excitation, match frequency through prime-mover speed, observe phase angle using an approved synchronizer and close with allowance for breaker time.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Check system permission and interlocks
\- Excitation matches Voltage
\- Speed matches frequency
\- Synchronizer determines closing instant

\*\*Remember\*\*

Never parallel merely because Voltage magnitudes are equal.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

What is hunting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Hunting is oscillation of the Rotor about its steady synchronous angular position after a load change, fault, switching event or other disturbance.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Rotor-angle oscillation
\- Caused by disturbance
\- Produces Current and torque fluctuation
\- Severe hunting can cause loss of synchronism

\*\*Remember\*\*

Hunting concerns angle, not a permanent change in average Synchronous Speed.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

What are Damper Windings and what do they do?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Damper Windings are short-circuited bars, commonly embedded in Salient-Pole faces. Induced Currents create torque opposing Rotor oscillation, and they can also develop induction torque during Synchronous-Motor starting.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Cage-like short-circuited bars
\- Oppose relative motion
\- Reduce hunting
\- Assist Motor starting

\*\*Remember\*\*

Damper action follows Lenz's Law.

\## Viva-017

\*\*Question\*\*

State the main advantages and limitations of Synchronous Motors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

They provide exact Synchronous Speed, high efficiency at suitable ratings and adjustable Power Factor. Their limitations include non-self-starting behavior, excitation complexity, higher control requirements and risk of losing synchronism under excessive disturbance or load.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Constant synchronous speed
\- Reactive-Power control
\- Starting arrangement required
\- Excitation and protection complexity

\*\*Remember\*\*

Use lifecycle value to justify added complexity.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Synchronous and Induction Motors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Synchronous Motor runs exactly at Ns and uses excitation or magnets; it needs a starting method and can control reactive Power. An Induction Motor runs below Ns with slip, is inherently self-starting in Three-Phase form and is generally simpler and more rugged.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Zero slip versus positive slip
\- Excited field versus induced Rotor Current
\- Starting arrangement versus inherent start
\- Adjustable versus normally lagging Power Factor

\*\*Remember\*\*

Synchronous gives special performance; induction gives general-purpose simplicity.

\## Viva-019

\*\*Question\*\*

What common faults occur in Synchronous Machines?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Common faults include Stator phase or earth faults, field-circuit faults, loss of excitation, unbalanced Current, AVR or rotating-diode failure, overheating, bearing damage, cooling failure, excessive vibration and loss of synchronism.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Stator and Rotor electrical faults
\- Excitation and control faults
\- Cooling and bearing faults
\- Stability abnormalities

\*\*Remember\*\*

Electrical, control and mechanical evidence must be reviewed together.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety precautions are required before maintaining a Synchronous Machine?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify and isolate Stator supply, Rotor excitation, prime mover, auxiliaries, heaters and all stored mechanical or pressure energy. Apply required lockout/tagout, verify absence of Voltage, prevent backfeed and automatic restart, and secure rotation before work.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Isolate AC and DC sources
\- Control prime mover and stored energy
\- Verify absence of Voltage
\- Prevent backfeed, rotation and restart

\*\*Remember\*\*

Opening the main breaker alone does not make a Generator safe.
