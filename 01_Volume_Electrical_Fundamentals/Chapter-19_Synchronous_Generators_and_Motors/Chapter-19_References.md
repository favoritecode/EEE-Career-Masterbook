\# Reference Notes

\- A Synchronous Machine can operate as a Generator or Motor.

\- The Stator normally carries the Three-Phase Armature winding and the Rotor carries the field.

\- Salient-Pole Rotors suit low-speed, many-pole machines.

\- Cylindrical Rotors suit high-speed turbo-Alternators.

\- Synchronous Speed is Ns = 120f/P.

\- Alternator frequency is f = PN/120.

\- Generated phase EMF is Eph = 4.44kwfΦT under sinusoidal conditions.

\- A basic Synchronous Motor is not inherently self-starting.

\- Excitation controls Rotor Flux and strongly affects reactive-Power behavior.

\- Prime-mover input mainly controls real Power after grid connection.

\- Synchronization requires matched Voltage, frequency, Phase Sequence and phase angle.

\- Closing out of phase can produce severe electrical Current and shaft torque.

\- Hunting is Rotor-angle oscillation after a disturbance.

\- Damper Windings reduce hunting and can assist Motor starting.

\- Synchronous Motors can operate at lagging, unity or leading Power Factor within limits.

\- Safe maintenance must isolate Stator, field, prime mover, auxiliaries and stored energy.

\- Chapter-20 integrates machine knowledge through Motor Starting, Speed Control and Maintenance.
