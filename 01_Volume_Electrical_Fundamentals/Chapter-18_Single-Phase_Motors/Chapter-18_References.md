\# Reference Notes

\- A basic Single-Phase Stator winding produces a pulsating Magnetic Field.

\- Double-revolving-field theory resolves the pulsating field into equal forward and backward fields.

\- At standstill, equal opposite torque components give zero net starting torque.

\- Spatially displaced windings with phase-displaced Currents produce starting torque.

\- Synchronous Speed is Ns = 120f/P.

\- Most Single-Phase Induction Motors use a Squirrel-Cage Rotor.

\- Split-Phase Motors use winding impedance to obtain moderate starting torque.

\- Capacitor-Start Motors use a large short-time-duty Capacitor for high starting torque.

\- Capacitor-Start Capacitor-Run Motors improve both starting and running performance.

\- Shaded-Pole Motors are simple but provide low starting torque and efficiency.

\- Universal Motors can operate on AC or DC and require brush and Commutator maintenance.

\- Under simplified induction-motor conditions, torque is approximately proportional to Voltage squared.

\- A failed start circuit may cause humming, stalling, high Current and rapid overheating.

\- Capacitors may retain hazardous energy after supply isolation.

\- Safe work requires isolation, verification of absence of Voltage and control of unexpected restart.

\- Chapter-19 extends electric-machine study to Synchronous Generators and Motors.
