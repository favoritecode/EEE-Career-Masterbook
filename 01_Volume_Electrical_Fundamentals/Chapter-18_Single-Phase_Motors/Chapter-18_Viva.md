\## Viva-001

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Single-Phase Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Single-Phase Motor is an electric Motor designed mainly to operate from a Single-Phase AC supply and convert electrical energy into rotating mechanical energy.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Single-Phase AC input
\- Mechanical shaft output
\- Common at small ratings
\- Widely used in appliances

\*\*Remember\*\*

Single-Phase supply → Motor torque → shaft rotation.

\## Viva-002

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the operating principle of a Single-Phase Induction Motor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

Single-Phase Stator Current produces alternating Flux. This Flux induces Rotor EMF and Current. After a starting arrangement establishes rotation, interaction between Stator Flux and Rotor Current develops continuing electromagnetic torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Alternating Stator Flux
\- Rotor EMF by induction
\- Closed Rotor carries Current
\- Flux–Current interaction produces torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Flux → Rotor EMF → Rotor Current → torque.

\## Viva-003

\*\*Question\*\*

Why is a basic Single-Phase Induction Motor not self-starting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Its single Stator winding produces a pulsating Magnetic Field. This field is equivalent to equal forward and backward rotating fields. At standstill, they develop equal opposite torques, so net starting torque is zero.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Pulsating field
\- Equal forward and backward components
\- Equal opposite torque at standstill
\- Zero net starting torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Tf − Tb = 0 at standstill.

\## Viva-004

\*\*Question\*\*

What is double-revolving-field theory?

\*\*Answer\*\*

It states that a pulsating Magnetic Field can be resolved into two equal fields rotating at Synchronous Speed in opposite directions. Their torques cancel at standstill but become unequal after rotation begins.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Two equal component fields
\- Opposite directions
\- Equal torque at standstill
\- Forward torque dominates during forward running

\*\*Remember\*\*

One pulsating field behaves like two opposite rotating fields.

\## Viva-006

\*\*Question\*\*

How is a rotating starting field produced in a Single-Phase Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

The main and auxiliary windings are spatially displaced, and their Currents are shifted in time phase by winding impedance or a Capacitor. The two Flux components combine to create an approximate rotating field and starting torque.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Spatial winding displacement
\- Current phase displacement
\- Approximate two-phase condition
\- Directional starting torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Spatial shift + time-phase shift = rotating effect.

\## Viva-007

\*\*Question\*\*

State the Synchronous Speed formula.

\*\*Answer\*\*

The formula is Ns = 120f/P, where Ns is Synchronous Speed in rpm, f is supply frequency in hertz and P is the number of poles.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Ns = 120f/P
\- Directly proportional to frequency
\- Inversely proportional to poles
\- Rotor speed remains below Ns in induction operation

\*\*Remember\*\*

At 50 Hz, a 4-pole field rotates at 1500 rpm.

\## Viva-008

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the Split-Phase Motor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Split-Phase Motor uses a main winding and a high-Resistance auxiliary winding. Their different impedance angles create a moderate Current phase difference and starting torque. A switch disconnects the auxiliary winding after acceleration.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Resistive phase splitting
\- Moderate starting torque
\- Auxiliary winding disconnected
\- Suitable for easy-start loads

\*\*Remember\*\*

Split phase uses winding impedance, not a large start Capacitor.

\## Viva-009

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the Capacitor-Start Motor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A large Capacitor is connected in series with the auxiliary winding during starting. It creates a large Current phase shift and high starting torque. The start circuit is disconnected near running speed.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Large start Capacitor
\- High starting torque
\- Short-time Capacitor duty
\- Start circuit removed after acceleration

\*\*Remember\*\*

Capacitor start is suitable for pumps and compressors.

\## Viva-010

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Capacitor-Start Capacitor-Run Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

It uses a large start Capacitor during acceleration and a smaller continuous-duty run Capacitor during operation. This provides high starting torque, smoother running, improved Power Factor and better efficiency.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Two Capacitors or equivalent arrangement
\- Strong starting performance
\- Run Capacitor remains connected
\- Smooth running torque

\*\*Remember\*\*

Start Capacitor helps starting; run Capacitor improves running.

\## Viva-011

\*\*Question\*\*

Explain the Shaded-Pole Motor.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A copper shading ring surrounds part of each pole. Induced Current delays Flux in the shaded portion, creating a weak sweeping field and starting torque from the unshaded part toward the shaded part.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Copper shading coil
\- Delayed shaded-pole Flux
\- Low starting torque
\- Simple and inexpensive

\*\*Remember\*\*

Shaded pole: lowest complexity, low torque and low efficiency.

\## Viva-012

\*\*Question\*\*

What is a Universal Motor?

\*\*Answer\*\*

A Universal Motor is a series-commutator Motor designed to operate on AC or DC. Field and armature Currents reverse together on AC, so electromagnetic torque remains in the same direction.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- AC or DC operation
\- Series field and armature
\- High starting torque
\- High speed and brush maintenance

\*\*Remember\*\*

Universal Motor is common in compact high-speed appliances.

\## Viva-015

\*\*Question\*\*

How does low supply Voltage affect induction-motor torque?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Under simplified conditions, torque is approximately proportional to Voltage squared. Therefore a moderate Voltage reduction causes a much larger reduction in available starting and running torque and may cause stalling.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Approximate relation T ∝ V²
\- Starting torque falls strongly
\- Acceleration becomes slower
\- Current and temperature may become excessive

\*\*Remember\*\*

Low Voltage does not protect a loaded Motor; it may make the Motor overheat.

\## Viva-016

\*\*Question\*\*

Why may a Single-Phase Motor hum without starting?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Possible causes include a failed Capacitor, open auxiliary winding, defective starting switch or relay, low Voltage, seized bearing or locked driven load. The supply should be disconnected quickly to prevent overheating.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Start-circuit failure
\- Supply problem
\- Mechanical seizure
\- High stall Current

\*\*Remember\*\*

Humming is a symptom; do not repeatedly re-energize the Motor.

\## Viva-018

\*\*Question\*\*

What safety precautions are essential before Motor maintenance?

\*\*Answer\*\*

Identify and isolate every energy source, apply required lockout/tagout, verify absence of Voltage, control stored Capacitor energy, prevent automatic restart and secure moving parts before work begins.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Safe isolation
\- Verify absence of Voltage
\- Discharge Capacitors by an approved method
\- Prevent unexpected movement or restart

\*\*Remember\*\*

Isolation is incomplete until absence of Voltage is verified.

\## Viva-020

\*\*Question\*\*

Compare Single-Phase and Three-Phase Induction Motors.

\*\*Answer\*\*

A basic Single-Phase Induction Motor is not self-starting and normally needs phase-splitting components. A balanced Three-Phase Motor naturally produces a rotating field, is self-starting and generally offers smoother torque, higher efficiency and better suitability for larger loads.

\*\*Key Points\*\*

\- Pulsating field versus natural RMF
\- Extra starting circuit versus inherent starting
\- Small domestic versus larger industrial duty
\- Lower versus generally higher efficiency

\*\*Remember\*\*

Single phase gives convenience; Three phase gives stronger industrial performance.
