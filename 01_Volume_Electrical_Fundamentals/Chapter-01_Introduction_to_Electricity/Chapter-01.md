\## Topic 01: What is Electricity?



\### Definition



Electricity is a physical phenomenon associated with the presence or movement of electric charge. It is one of the most important forms of energy used in modern science, engineering and everyday life.



In Electrical Engineering, electricity is mainly utilized through the controlled movement of electric charge, known as \*\*Electric Current\*\*.



\---



\### Importance



Electricity plays a vital role in modern life and engineering. It is essential for:



\- Power generation, transmission and distribution

\- Residential, commercial and industrial electrical systems

\- Electronic devices and embedded systems

\- Communication and networking

\- Industrial automation and control

\- Medical equipment

\- Transportation systems

\- Renewable energy technologies



\---



\### Main Concepts



Electricity is generally classified into two main types:



\#### 1. Static Electricity



Static Electricity is the accumulation of electric charge on the surface of a material. The charges remain at rest until they are discharged.



\*\*Examples\*\*



\- Small spark after touching a metal object.

\- Attraction of paper pieces by a rubbed plastic ruler.



\---



\#### 2. Current Electricity



Current Electricity is the continuous flow of electric charge through a conductor. It is the type of electricity used in homes, industries and electronic devices.



\*\*Examples\*\*



\- Electric lighting

\- Ceiling fans

\- Mobile phone chargers

\- Industrial machines



\---





\### Real-Life Applications



Electricity is used in almost every aspect of modern life, including:



\- Household appliances

\- Lighting systems

\- Mobile phones and computers

\- Industrial machinery

\- Hospitals and medical equipment

\- Communication systems

\- Electric vehicles

\- Renewable energy systems



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- Electricity is associated with the presence or movement of electric charge.

\- Electricity is one of the most important forms of energy.

\- Electricity exists mainly as Static Electricity and Current Electricity.

\- Current Electricity powers most electrical and electronic systems.

\- Understanding electricity is the first step in learning Electrical Engineering.

\## Topic 02: Atomic Structure



\### Definition



An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties. It is the basic building block of all matter.



Every substance is composed of atoms. Understanding atomic structure is essential in Electrical Engineering because the movement of electrons within atoms is responsible for electric current.



\---



\### Importance



Knowledge of atomic structure helps engineers understand:



\- The origin of electric current.

\- The behavior of electrical materials.

\- The difference between conductors, insulators and semiconductors.

\- The working principles of electrical and electronic devices.



\---



\### Main Concepts



An atom consists of two main parts:



\#### 1. Nucleus



The nucleus is located at the center of the atom and contains:



\- Proton (Positive Charge)

\- Neutron (Neutral Charge)



The nucleus contains almost all of the mass of an atom.



\---



\#### 2. Electron Shells



Electrons move around the nucleus in different energy levels called electron shells.



These electrons are mainly responsible for the flow of electric current in conductive materials.



\---





\### Subatomic Particles



An atom is composed of three fundamental subatomic particles.



| Particle | Symbol | Charge | Location | Role |

|----------|--------|--------|----------|------|

| Proton | p⁺ | Positive (+) | Nucleus | Determines the atomic number of an element. |

| Neutron | n⁰ | Neutral (0) | Nucleus | Contributes to the atomic mass and stability of the nucleus. |

| Electron | e⁻ | Negative (−) | Around the nucleus | Responsible for electric current in conductive materials. |



\---



\### Real-Life Applications



Understanding atomic structure is important in:



\- Electrical wiring

\- Semiconductor devices

\- Batteries

\- Solar cells

\- Integrated Circuits (ICs)

\- Electronic components



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- An atom is the smallest unit of an element.

\- Every atom consists of protons, neutrons and electrons.

\- Protons and neutrons are located inside the nucleus.

\- Electrons move around the nucleus.

\- Free electrons are mainly responsible for electric current.

\## Topic 03: Electric Charge



\### Definition



Electric Charge is a fundamental property of matter that causes electrical attraction and repulsion between particles. Every electrically charged particle carries either a positive charge or a negative charge.



The SI unit of Electric Charge is \*\*Coulomb (C)\*\*.



\---



\### Importance



Electric Charge is one of the most fundamental concepts in Electrical Engineering. Understanding electric charge helps explain:



\- Electric current

\- Static electricity

\- Electrical attraction and repulsion

\- Electrical and electronic devices

\- Basic circuit theory



\---



\### Main Concepts



Electric Charge exists in two forms:



\#### 1. Positive Charge (+)



A body becomes positively charged when it loses one or more electrons.



\*\*Examples\*\*



\- Proton

\- Glass rod rubbed with silk



\---



\#### 2. Negative Charge (−)



A body becomes negatively charged when it gains one or more electrons.



\*\*Examples\*\*



\- Electron

\- Plastic rod rubbed with wool



\---





\### Basic Laws of Electric Charge



Electric charges obey two fundamental laws:



\- Like charges repel each other.

\- Unlike charges attract each other.



These laws explain many electrical phenomena observed in nature and engineering applications.



\---



\### SI Unit



The SI unit of Electric Charge is \*\*Coulomb (C)\*\*.



One Coulomb is the amount of electric charge transferred by a current of one ampere in one second.



\---



\### Real-Life Applications



Electric Charge plays an important role in:



\- Batteries

\- Capacitors

\- Laser printers

\- Photocopiers

\- Lightning

\- Electrostatic painting

\- Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) protection



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- Electric Charge is a fundamental property of matter.

\- Electric Charge exists in two forms: Positive and Negative.

\- Like charges repel each other.

\- Unlike charges attract each other.

\- The SI unit of Electric Charge is Coulomb (C).

\## Topic 04: Conductors, Insulators and Semiconductors



\### Definition



Electrical materials are classified according to their ability to conduct electric current. Based on their electrical conductivity, they are grouped into three main categories:



\- Conductors

\- Insulators

\- Semiconductors



Understanding these materials is essential for selecting the right material for electrical and electronic applications.



\---



\### Importance



Knowledge of electrical materials helps engineers:



\- Select suitable wiring materials.

\- Design safe electrical systems.

\- Understand electronic components.

\- Improve electrical efficiency and safety.



\---



\### Main Concepts



\#### 1. Conductors



A conductor is a material that allows electric current to flow easily because it contains a large number of free electrons.



\*\*Common Examples\*\*



\- Copper (Cu)

\- Silver (Ag)

\- Aluminium (Al)

\- Gold (Au)



\---





\#### 2. Insulators



An insulator is a material that strongly resists the flow of electric current because it contains very few free electrons.



\*\*Common Examples\*\*



\- Rubber

\- Plastic

\- Glass

\- Porcelain

\- Dry Wood



\*\*Common Applications\*\*



\- Wire insulation

\- Switch covers

\- Electrical safety gloves

\- Insulating supports



\---



\#### 3. Semiconductors



A semiconductor is a material whose electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator. Its conductivity can be controlled under specific conditions, making it suitable for electronic devices.



\*\*Common Examples\*\*



\- Silicon (Si)

\- Germanium (Ge)



\*\*Common Applications\*\*



\- Diodes

\- Transistors

\- Integrated Circuits (ICs)

\- Solar Cells

\- Microprocessors



\---



\### Comparison of Electrical Materials



| Property | Conductor | Semiconductor | Insulator |

|----------|-----------|---------------|------------|

| Electrical Conductivity | High | Moderate | Very Low |

| Free Electrons | Many | Limited | Very Few |

| Current Flow | Easy | Controlled | Highly Restricted |

| Examples | Copper, Silver | Silicon, Germanium | Rubber, Glass |



\---



\### Real-Life Applications



Electrical materials are widely used in:



\- House wiring

\- Power transmission lines

\- Electrical cables

\- Electronic circuits

\- Computer chips

\- Mobile phones

\- Power control systems



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- Conductors allow electric current to flow easily.

\- Insulators resist the flow of electric current.

\- Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators.

\- Copper is the most common material for electrical wiring.

\- Silicon is the most widely used semiconductor material.

\## Topic 05: Static Electricity



\### Definition



Static Electricity is the accumulation of electric charge on the surface of a material. The electric charges remain at rest until they are discharged through a conductive path.



\---



\### Importance



Static Electricity is important because it helps explain many natural phenomena and practical engineering applications. Understanding static electricity also helps engineers prevent damage to sensitive electronic components caused by Electrostatic Discharge (ESD).



\---



\### Main Concepts



Static electricity is commonly produced by friction between two different materials. During rubbing, electrons are transferred from one material to another, causing one object to become positively charged and the other negatively charged.



\*\*Common Examples\*\*



\- Small spark after touching a metal object.

\- Balloon rubbed on hair sticking to a wall.

\- Plastic ruler attracting small pieces of paper.



\---





\### Causes of Static Electricity



Static electricity is mainly produced by:



\- Friction between two different materials.

\- Separation of two contacting materials.

\- Induction caused by a nearby charged object.



The most common cause in everyday life is friction.



\---



\### Real-Life Applications



Static Electricity is used in various practical applications, including:



\- Laser printers

\- Photocopiers

\- Electrostatic painting

\- Air purification systems

\- Electrostatic precipitators used in industries



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- Static Electricity is the accumulation of electric charge at rest.

\- It is commonly produced by friction.

\- A sudden discharge of static electricity can produce a small spark.

\- Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) can damage sensitive electronic components.

\- Static electricity has many useful industrial applications.

\## Topic 06: Electric Current



\### Definition



Electric Current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. It is the movement of free electrons under the influence of an applied potential difference (voltage).



The SI unit of Electric Current is \*\*Ampere (A)\*\*.



The symbol of Electric Current is \*\*I\*\*.



\---



\### Importance



Electric Current is one of the most fundamental quantities in Electrical Engineering. It is essential for:



\- Operating electrical devices

\- Power transmission and distribution

\- Electronic circuits

\- Industrial automation

\- Electrical measurements



\---



\### Formula



The mathematical relationship between electric charge and electric current is:



\\\[

I = \\frac{Q}{t}

\\]



Where:



\- \*\*I\*\* = Electric Current (Ampere, A)

\- \*\*Q\*\* = Electric Charge (Coulomb, C)

\- \*\*t\*\* = Time (Second, s)



\---



\### Main Concepts



Electric current flows only when a closed path is available.



The flow of current depends on:



\- Availability of free electrons.

\- Presence of a potential difference (Voltage).

\- A closed electrical circuit.



\---





\### Types of Electric Current



Electric Current is commonly classified into two types:



\#### 1. Direct Current (DC)



Direct Current (DC) flows in one direction only.



\*\*Common Sources\*\*



\- Batteries

\- Solar Cells

\- DC Power Supplies



\---



\#### 2. Alternating Current (AC)



Alternating Current (AC) periodically changes both its magnitude and direction.



\*\*Common Sources\*\*



\- Electrical Power Stations

\- Household Power Supply

\- Industrial Power Systems



\---



\### Measuring Instrument



Electric Current is measured using an \*\*Ammeter\*\*.



\*\*Important Notes\*\*



\- SI Unit: Ampere (A)

\- Symbol: I

\- Measuring Instrument: Ammeter

\- Ammeter is connected in \*\*series\*\* with the electrical circuit.



\---



\### Real-Life Applications



Electric Current is essential for operating:



\- Household appliances

\- Electric motors

\- Lighting systems

\- Industrial machines

\- Computers and mobile devices

\- Medical equipment

\- Communication systems

\- Electric vehicles



\---



\### Key Takeaways



\- Electric Current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

\- The SI unit of Electric Current is Ampere (A).

\- The symbol of Electric Current is I.

\- Electric Current requires a closed circuit to flow.

\- Electric Current is measured using an Ammeter.

\- Electric Current may be Direct Current (DC) or Alternating Current (AC).
